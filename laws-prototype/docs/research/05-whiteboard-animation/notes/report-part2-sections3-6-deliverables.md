## 3. Research evidence

- **Mayer & Fiorella (2020) "Five ways to increase effectiveness of instructional video", Educ Tech Res Dev 68:837–852** — https://learningglass.eu/jp/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/Mayer2020_5-ways-to-increase-the-effectiveness-of-instructional-video.pdf — index, 2026-11-01. Dynamic drawing principle: learn better when onscreen instructor draws graphics while lecturing. Mechanisms: social agency (drawing = social partnership), embodiment (seeing hand primes self-reference — "as if their hand is doing the drawing"), signaling (drawing shows where to look), segmenting (graphic broken into parts). Explicit caveat: Khan-style drawing *without* a visible hand/instrument "is not entirely consistent with the dynamic drawing principle… it may be particularly important for learners to see the hand."
- **Fiorella & Mayer (2016), J Educ Psychol** (summarised in Mayer 2020 and Zhang 2025, both cited): watching instructor draw Doppler-effect diagrams (full body OR hand only) > completed diagrams; dynamic drawing *without* visible hand did NOT beat static in that study. Fiorella et al. (2019): dynamic drawing (Khan-style biology) > final product only.
- **Zhang, Guo, Son, Blank & Stigler (2024/2025), Memory & Cognition 53:262–281** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11779760/ — verified live. Study 2: static slides vs dynamic drawing vs dynamic drawing by visible hand (normal distribution): hand version best. Study 3: dynamic cursor over static slides ≈ hand, both > control. Honest implication: much of hand benefit may be *attention guidance*, reproducible by a moving pen-tip/cursor — crucial for an app with no real hand.
- **Schneider, Krieglstein, Beege & Rey (2023), Heliyon 9:e13229** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9898452/ — verified live. 2 exps, N=133 secondary students, 2×2 (progressive drawing vs static pictures × narrative vs none): progressive drawing → higher retention (Exp1 F(1,129)=25.72, p<.001, ηp²=.17) and transfer, *lower* mental load; narrative → better transfer. Conclusion quoted: "Without a progressive presentation of drawings and a storyline in the verbal explanation, learning results were significantly lower."
- **Türkay (2016, Computers & Education; 2022, AJET 38(5))** — AJET PDF https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/ajet/ajet-7639.pdf — index. N=299, whiteboard vs narrated slides vs on-stage lecture vs audio only, same social-science content: whiteboard group answered significantly more comprehension questions than lecture/slides groups; whiteboard + audio highest enjoyment/engagement. Author cautions novelty could contribute (2016); 2022 replication after format became common argues against pure novelty.
- **Brosnan & Hasso (2025), Smart Learning Environments 12:65** — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-025-00420-2 — index (publisher page + VideoScribe summary https://blog.videoscribe.co/whiteboard-animation-beats-slides-for-retention-new-research). n=521 adults, accounting, 5 arms (whiteboard+narration / narrated slideshow / audio / text / no-treatment control): retention /5 — whiteboard 3.8, text 3.4, audio 3.4, slideshow 3.3; enjoyment & engagement significantly higher; advantage still present at 1-week follow-up but decay rate identical across groups (better initial encoding, not slower forgetting).
- **Wiseman (2012, with Cognitive / Univ. of Hertfordshire)** — https://www.harrietmatsaert.com/research-and-best-practice/research-on-using-visual-tools and https://blog.videoscribe.co/what-is-whiteboard-animation — index. ~1,000 people, same audio as talking-head vs whiteboard: +15% recall overall; one item 92% vs 70%. Widely quoted but NOT a peer-reviewed journal paper and vendor-associated — cite as suggestive, not definitive.
- **Null / critical results (must not be omitted):**
  - van der Meij & Draijer (2021), J Educ Multimedia Hypermedia 30(4):393–418, ERIC https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1323338 — index: whiteboard vs PowerPoint, same audio: *similar knowledge-test results*, only satisfaction significantly higher; warns of seductive-details effect; notes whiteboard complies with several Mayer principles "almost by default."
  - Krieglstein et al. / Springer (2023) hand-insertion study — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-023-00258-6 — index: hand *drawing* vs hand *pushing in* finished content vs auto-appear: drawing hand → higher intrinsic motivation only; no differences in learning, cognitive load, or instructor perception; hand did NOT add extraneous load.
  - Segmentation study (ResearchGate https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366107637) — index: splitting whiteboard animations into 1–4 segments did NOT improve learning; metacognitive recap prompts raised motivation only. So "more pauses/chunks" is not automatically better — pauses must contain a *task* (predict/recap), not just a break.
  - Transient-information risk (discussed in Schneider 2023): animation that disappears must be held in working memory; whiteboard mitigates this only if drawings *remain visible* (accumulation). Erase-happy designs lose the benefit.
- **Language-specific / Asian EFL:**
  - Syafrizal et al. (2021), J Language & Linguistic Studies — https://www.jlls.org/index.php/jlls/article/view/2337 — index: Indonesian 8th-graders, experimental vs conventional: vocabulary improvement 58.9% vs 37%; grammar >100% vs 50.5%; effect sizes R²=.321 (vocab), .485 (grammar). Small classroom quasi-experiment — direction positive, magnitude not generalisable.
  - Suhroh, Cahyono & Astuti (2020), Arab World English Journal, CALL special issue — whiteboard animation in project-based learning, Indonesian vocational EFL: students *making* whiteboard animations presented better; interaction with creativity level was non-significant in the reported ANOVA table (p=.059 for the whiteboard-in-PjBL factor) — making-it benefits are plausible, not proven.
  - Schneider 2023's review also cites Syafrizal for vocab/grammar gains — the Indonesian literature is the main body of *language-learning-specific* whiteboard evidence; no controlled study was found that tests whiteboard animation *for prepositions specifically*. That is a genuine gap: preposition use is an extrapolation from grammar/vocab results + proto-scene studies (§4).

## 4. Preposition-specific treatments & cognitive-linguistics diagrams

- **Tyler & Evans (2003) The Semantics of English Prepositions; Evans & Tyler (2005) "Applying cognitive linguistics to pedagogical grammar: the English prepositions of verticality"** — PDF https://www.vyvevans.net/ar/_files/ugd/603cc2_1f736e63b2fd4a819fc7d26581654104.pdf — index. Core claims: a preposition encodes an abstract, embodied *proto-scene* (primary sense) + a functional element; other senses extend from it "in systematic, constrained ways" via (i) different ways of viewing the same scene and (ii) experiential correlation. Traditional list-definitions are "inaccurate" and force memorisation of apparent arbitrariness.
- **Proto-scene diagram grammar (the drawable bit):** a trajector (TR, the thing located/moving) and a landmark (LM, the reference thing). For *over*: TR higher than LM, with a dashed line marking a *potential-contact zone* below it; for *in*: TR inside LM's containment zone. Evidence these spare diagrams are psychologically real: Cambridge Language & Cognition study — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-and-cognition/article/are-schematic-diagrams-valid-visual-representations-of-concepts-evidence-from-mental-imagery-in-online-processing-of-english-prepositions/6F16E235B9F6C8E67E3E21B469058D1F — index: researchers used Tyler & Evans' over/in diagrams (enhancing TR with a red dot, thickening LM) as stimuli for mental-imagery/online-processing tasks.
- **Tyler's own slide deck, "It's Not Over When It's Over"** — https://Www.slideserve.com/johana/vyvyan-evans-yiyoung-kim-and-dasha-shakhova-made-invaluable-contributions-to-this-project — index. The sense inventory for *over*, each a one-line scene: flag *over* the capitol = higher than; Arlington is *over* the river = on the other side; negotiations are *over* = finished; ship took troops *over* = transfer; mask *over* her face = covering; *over* 300,000 = more than; chose intervention *over* negotiation = preference; picture *over* the mantel ≈ above. And the key drawable mechanism, the **A–B–C trajectory**: a moving TR starts at A, is higher than the LM only at B (top of the arc, e.g. horse jumping *over* the hurdle), and lands at C. Completion ("it's over") and across/other-side are the *endpoints of that same arc, profiled* — which is exactly how to animate them: draw the arc, then spotlight A→C as a whole.
- **Gezer & Nural (2026), Int J Language Academy (over/by networks)** — https://ijla.net/api/articles/6449e698-6a53-4960-aa51-999e6beba250/pdf?download=1 — index: over = higher level (umbrella over head), movement across (boat over the lake), covering (all over his face), time (over the summer holidays), beyond (over the age limit), more (over 40), completion (it is over). Confirms the mission's example chain (above → covering → finished) is attested, and adds time/more/beyond as later branches — do NOT teach them in the same first pass.
- **Pedagogy evidence for the CL approach:** "Teaching English locative prepositions: a cognitive perspective" — https://www.academia.edu/30850129/Teaching_English_locative_prepositions_a_cognitive_perspective — index, and Soochow "Making Meaning through Prepositions" model — http://lass.suda.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/32/40/8cef3ada46b6a0d7657ff1fef693/ebc747de-a891-434e-b6be-399b89fc92d7.pdf — index: studies (Tyler, Mueller & Ho 2011; Hung et al. 2018) found presenting polysemy as *radiating from spatial scenes* beat list teaching. Design rule that follows: **one proto-scene per preposition, senses as spokes, each spoke = one visible change to the scene.**

## 5. Chinese-market explainer animation (not literal whiteboard, transferable grammar)

- **洋葱学园 (Yangcong / Onion Academy)** — deep report http://www.duozhi.com/industry/insight/2023121415727.shtml — index; design philosophy http://www.duozhi.com/opentalk/2023071815358.shtml — index. Facts: 5–8-min fully-animated lesson per knowledge point; average production ~2 months and >¥100,000 per lesson; stated principle "重理解" (understanding over procedure) — "how the student thinks about the problem matters more than how the problem is solved." Worked example (negative numbers, 7 min): opens with 4 animated characters chatting about summer, poses a question with 4 options and **the video cannot continue until the learner answers**, then explains one idea through *multiple everyday scenes in sequence* (temperature above/below zero, mountain height vs sea depth, game HP loss/gain, lift going up/down) — 7 questions in 7 minutes. Their quality standard for every lesson: 跟得上 (can follow), 听得完 (can finish), 学得懂 (can understand), 做得对 (can do it right); design triangle = 目标感/共情感/逻辑性 (goal/empathy/logic) + 画面感/趣味性/启发性 (visual feel / fun / prompting). **Transferable grammar:** (1) character dialogue carries the story (narrative embedding, §3); (2) scenes accumulate as *variations on one schema*, never as unrelated clips; (3) a forced prediction/answer beat every ~60 s; (4) one knowledge point per short video, ruthlessly.
- **斑马 (Zebra AI) / 伴鱼 (Banyu)** — comparison (vendor, treat ratings as biased) https://www.banyu.com/blog/english/149101/ — index; industry roundup https://gj.eol.cn/gxdt/202508/t20250808_2684926.shtml — index. Zebra: AI interactive recorded lessons for low-age beginners — animation + picture-book input, short light units, 学–练–测 (learn–practise–test) loop, IP character, 15–20-min sessions matched to young attention spans; acknowledged limit: recorded AI lessons cannot replace live interaction/correction. Banyu: picture-book reading + 25-min live foreign-teacher lessons, AR props and star rewards. **Transferable grammar:** recurring character the child follows across senses; every explainer segment is immediately followed by a tap/speak practise item using the *same* picture; rewards mark *sense completions*, not time watched. Neither product uses literal whiteboard drawing — their lesson is that for ages 3–8, character + story + forced response beats stroke aesthetics; whiteboard should borrow their pacing, not compete with their polish.

## 6. Production practicalities — single-file offline HTML app

Essential vs decorative (ranked by the evidence in §3):
- **ESSENTIAL:** (1) progressive stroke reveal synced to a beat/narration line; (2) accumulation — finished elements stay; (3) one-change-at-a-time transformation of the same scene; (4) a signalling pointer at the locus of change (pen tip / hand / cursor — Zhang Study 3 says cursor suffices); (5) prediction/recap pause beats with a task; (6) handwritten-style labels added *after* the picture they name (contiguity).
- **DECORATIVE (cut first):** photorealistic hand image, paper texture, background music, marker squeaks (use at most one subtle tick on a meaning-change), colour fills beyond a 2-colour code (TR colour vs LM colour), camera pans (use only to follow an arc across the board; every needless pan is extraneous load + motion sickness in miniature).

Techniques, all offline/single-file capable:
- **SVG stroke-draw (the core trick):** for each `<path>`, set `stroke-dasharray = path.getTotalLength()`, `stroke-dashoffset = length`, then animate offset → 0 (CSS transition / Web Animations API / requestAnimationFrame). Strokes play in DOM order = drawing order. Canonical tutorial: https://webdesign.tutsplus.com/sign-on-the-dotted-line-animating-your-own-svg-signature--cms-23846t — index (dasharray = path length, sequence strokes one after another, per-stroke durations). No library needed; ~20 lines of JS.
- **Rough.js for the hand-drawn look:** https://roughjs.com/ — verified live. "<9kB gzipped… draw in a sketchy, hand-drawn-like style… works with both Canvas and SVG", primitives (line/curve/arc/polygon/circle/ellipse) + arbitrary SVG paths, `roughness`/`bowing` options, hachure/solid/zigzag fills, MIT licence. Inline the bundled file into the single HTML file (no CDN — offline requirement). Use it to *generate* wobbly paths, then stroke-animate the resulting `<path>` elements as above. Warning: Rough.js draws finished shapes instantly by itself; the *reveal* is your dash animation on top.
- **Hand/pen:** a small pen-nib or hand PNG/SVG positioned at the current path's `getPointAtLength(animatedLength)` each frame — this gives a true "hand follows the stroke" effect with no video. Hide it during pause beats (a hovering hand over the scene is a seductive detail).
- **Erase/redraw:** animate dashoffset back up (reverse draw) for the *one* element being replaced, or use an SVG mask "wipe" left-to-right for a whole-scene clear — reserve full wipes for a new preposition, never between senses of the same word.
- **Zoom/pan:** one `<svg viewBox>` change with a CSS transition on a wrapper `<g transform>` — implement as 2–3 named camera states only (whole board / left scene / right scene).
- **Narration sync without audio files:** if no recorded voice, drive beats from tap/"next" + on-screen caption text appearing word-group by word-group in sync with strokes (karaoke caption). If audio is used, embed as base64 or a single compressed file and use `audio.currentTime` cue points per beat. Keep every beat scrubbable/replayable — replayability is what defeats the transient-information effect.
- **Fonts:** a handwriting-style font must be embedded (woff2 base64) or labels fall back to a typed look that breaks the illusion; alternatively hand-write labels as SVG paths (best fidelity, most authoring cost — do it only for the preposition word itself and TR/LM labels).

## (b) Technique library — mapped to teaching jobs

| Teaching job | Technique | How it looks on screen |
|---|---|---|
| Build a core scene | **Progressive build in thinking order** | Ground/landmark first (table, box, hill), trajector second (cat, ball), relation last (arc, contact line). Nothing appears that narration hasn't reached. |
| Show a transformation | **Morph the same drawing** | Keep every stroke; animate only the differing element: cat slides along a drawn arc; blanket *grows* (extra strokes) until it covers the box. Reverse-draw (dashoffset up) only the element being undone. |
| Contrast two neighbours | **Side-by-side twin scenes + one difference highlighted** | Duplicate the scene (same LM, same TR), change one variable (gap vs contact for above vs over/on; inside vs on-top), then circle the differing zone in TR-colour and draw a ✗/✓. BBC-Sam variant: one scene, three labels (at the front / on the stage / in the crowd). |
| Move to time / abstract senses | **Re-label the same picture** | Keep the spatial drawing; fade its caption out, draw a clock/calendar *around the same geometry* (container → month box; surface → day square; point → clock dot; arc A→C → timeline with a "finished" flag at C). Say/show "same picture, new world." (Kevin's place→time move.) |
| Recap | **Zoom out to the accumulated board + spoke diagram** | Camera pulls back to show all senses as small scenes arranged as spokes around the proto-scene in the centre; pen taps each spoke as its word/sentence is re-read. Ends as a static summary chart (Woodward ending) the learner can screenshot/revisit. |
| Check understanding | **Prediction pause** | Freeze with the scene *half-changed* (blanket halfway across the box); learner taps/guesses the preposition or next position; only then complete the stroke. (Yangcong's answer-to-continue, ~1/min.) |
| Signal what matters | **Pen-tip + colour code** | Pen/cursor always at the locus of change; TR always one colour (e.g. red, as in the Cambridge proto-scene stimuli), LM always another (black/blue). No other colours compete. |
| Add personality | **Character cast + one joke per sense** | Same animal/character is the TR in every scene (Woodward's dogs; Yangcong's cast); humour lives in the *scene* (thermometer in dog's mouth), never in extra decoration. |

## (c) Quality checklist — good vs cheap whiteboard lesson

Good (observable):
- [ ] Every picture is *drawn* stroke-by-stroke, in the order the narration thinks it; you can predict the finished picture halfway (visual suspense).
- [ ] Drawing and speech are beat-synced: a stroke starts when its word is spoken, finishes before the next idea.
- [ ] Finished drawings *stay on screen*; the board accumulates; comparisons are visible simultaneously.
- [ ] Meaning changes are shown by *changing the drawing* (morph/extend/erase-one-element), never by cutting to a new slide.
- [ ] A pen/hand/cursor is present at the drawing point while drawing, absent while the learner is meant to look/think.
- [ ] Labels are handwritten (or hand-path) and appear *after* the thing they label; ≤ ~7 words on screen at once; no paragraphs.
- [ ] Colour is a code (TR vs LM vs highlight), used identically in every scene.
- [ ] Stroke speed varies with importance: fast for scenery, slow + pause for the meaning-bearing stroke; a beat of silence after each new sense.
- [ ] Segments are short (scribe norm ≤2.5 min per concept; Yangcong ≤8 min with interaction every ~60 s) and end on a summary view of the whole board.
- [ ] Custom drawings of *this* lesson's scenes; a recurring character; a voice with a personality saying things the pictures don't (complementary, not read-aloud).

Cheap (any three = cheap):
- [ ] Stock clipart/photos *slide, fade or pop in* whole, undrawn (or "drawn" by a hand waving over them — the push-in style that scored lowest on motivation).
- [ ] The same default VideoScribe/Doodly hand, font and library characters as a thousand other videos.
- [ ] Walls of typed text being slowly "hand-drawn" letter by letter, especially as the opening shot.
- [ ] Constant robotic stroke speed; narration a monotone/AI voice reading the on-screen text verbatim.
- [ ] Board wiped clean between every point (nothing accumulates; learner must hold everything in memory).
- [ ] Camera pans/zooms constantly with no change in meaning; background music louder than the ideas; marker-squeak on every stroke.
- [ ] Scenes unrelated to each other (new clip-art world per sentence) — no recurring TR/LM, so no schema can form.
- [ ] Length >5 min with no prediction, question, or recap beat.

## (d) Direct answer — teaching a preposition's sense extension in an offline single-file HTML app, beat by beat

Example used: **over** (above → across/finished → covering). Same script works for in/on/at with Kevin's dimensionality schema. Total runtime ~90–150 s, or tap-to-advance beats (preferred for children — learner-paced segmenting). One SVG board, TR = red cat, LM = black fence/table, pen-tip follows every stroke.

1. **Hook + prediction (0–8 s).** Board empty except a hand-written title "over ?". Narration/caption: "The cat and the fence. Where will the cat go?" Nothing drawn yet — anticipation first (VideoScribe hook rule; Wiseman's anticipation mechanism).
2. **Build the landmark (8–18 s).** Draw the ground line, then the fence (LM), stroke by stroke, pen visible. Caption names it only after it exists: "a fence". Pause 1 s.
3. **Build the trajector (18–26 s).** Draw the cat (TR, red) sitting at point A, left of the fence. Label "the cat". The scene is now a proto-scene *without* the preposition — learner sees TR and LM separately first (Tyler & Evans order).
4. **First sense — the arc / above-across (26–45 s).** Draw a dashed arc A→B→C over the fence *slowly* (this is the meaning-bearing stroke — slowest in the lesson). Then animate the cat *travelling along the already-drawn arc* to C. Handwrite "over" under the arc, then the sentence, word-groups in sync: "The cat jumped **over** the fence." Spotlight B (top of arc) briefly: "Only here is the cat *higher* than the fence." Freeze 2 s.
5. **Name the core (45–52 s).** Circle the arc in red. Caption: "over = go from one side, high, to the other side." This one sentence is the proto-scene; every later sense points back to this circled arc, which **stays on screen, shrunk to a corner "core card"** for the rest of the lesson.
6. **Prediction beat (52–60 s).** New identical fence scene begins at right (camera pan once, or board split). Cat at A again. Pause with arc *half-drawn*: "The cat jumped… and now it has landed. The jump is — ?" Learner taps: *over / under / in*. 
7. **Extension 1 — finished (60–78 s).** Complete the arc, cat lands at C, then draw a small flag/check at C and *re-draw the same arc as a timeline* beneath the scene (same geometry, new label). Sentence: "The jump is **over**." → second example in the same frame: a race/game icon finishing: "The game is **over**." Explicit caption bridge: "Finished = we reached the end of the arc." One change only: arc-as-path → arc-as-time.
8. **Extension 2 — covering (78–100 s).** Wipe *only the right scene* (keep core card + finished scene visible for comparison). Draw a table (LM), then a cloth (TR) — first draped *above* it (re-draw the proto-scene: TR higher than LM, dashed contact-zone line visible, per Tyler & Evans' diagram), then let the cloth *grow stroke-by-stroke* down over the sides until it touches/covers. Sentence: "The cloth is **over** the table." Caption bridge: "Same picture — higher than — now touching and hiding it." The single visible change is contact + coverage.
9. **Contrast beat (100–115 s).** Split screen: left = cloth hovering with a gap (label it **above**), right = cloth covering (label it **over**). Pen circles the *gap* on the left and the *contact* on the right. This is the neighbour-discrimination Woodward's "above = over" listing fails to teach.
10. **Recap — spoke board (115–135 s).** Zoom out: core arc card in the centre, three spokes to the three scenes (jump / game finished / cloth), pen taps each as its sentence is re-read once. End on this static board (screenshot-able summary chart). Optional final prediction item with a *new* TR/LM (bird over a roof) before the answer is drawn.

Implementation notes for this script: beats = an array of {paths[], caption, audioCue, pause, task} objects; a "next" tap or audio timecode advances one beat; every path is dash-animated with duration proportional to its teaching weight (arc slowest); the pen element follows `getPointAtLength()`; reverse-dash is the only "eraser"; replay button re-runs a single beat, not the whole lesson.
