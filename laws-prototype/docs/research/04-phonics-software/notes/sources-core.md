# Core sources — read 2026-11-01 (index unless noted)

## Verified live (research window)
- Teach Your Monster to Read overview — https://www.teachyourmonster.org/teach-your-monster-to-read-overview/ — VERIFIED LIVE 2026-11-01. 3 games: First Steps (31 GPCs s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f ff l ll ss..., CVC blending/segmenting, 6 tricky words), Fun With Words (+18 GPCs ch sh th ng ai ee igh oa oo ar or ur ow oi ear air ure er; CVC/CVCC/CCV/CCVC; ~30 tricky; sentences), Champion Reader (alternative spellings e.g. /ai/ in eight/they; alternative pronunciations e.g. i in fin/find; little books). Adaptive: struggling graphemes repeated. Practice Mode for specific sounds. Free on computer, Usborne Foundation, Roehampton academics.

## Index — flagship sequences
- Jolly Phonics: 42 sounds, 7 groups: 1 s a t i p n; 2 c k e h r m d; 3 g o u l f b; 4 ai j oa ie ee or; 5 z w ng v oo oo; 6 y x ch sh th th; 7 qu ou oi ue er ar. Rationale: maximise early CVC word-building, separate confusables (p/b apart). Each sound: story+action+song. App (Google Play https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gilbertjolly.teachphonics.teachers&hl=en_CA): audio all sounds, all songs, animated letter formation, action image/instructions, word bank+flashcards, tests. phonics.org review https://www.phonics.org/literacy-review-jolly-phonics-program/ : app routes = Letter Sounds / Alternatives / Tricky Words; DfE-validated (one of 45); not a complete literacy programme (no vocab/comprehension focus).
- Read Write Inc: Set1 m a s d t i n p g o c k u b f e l h sh r j v y w th z ch qu x ng nk (order varies slightly by school doc); Set2 ay ee igh ow oo oo ar or air ir ou oy (12, each with rhyme e.g. ay 'may I play'); Set3 ea oi a-e i-e o-e u-e aw are ur er ow ai oa ew ire ear ure tion tious/cious — alternative spellings for same sounds. Fred Talk: frog speaks only in sounds m-a-t; Fred Fingers for spelling (one finger per phoneme); Green words (decodable) vs Red words (irregular); mantra 'Special Friends, Fred Talk, Read the Word' → 'Fred in Your Head' → Speedy Reading. Source: http://www.thevictoryprimary.co.uk/our-school/curriculum/phonics and school PDFs.
- Letters and Sounds phases: P1 listening/PA; P2 19 letters, VC/CVC blending; P3 +25 graphemes (ch sh ng, ai ee oa, igh air ear) = 42 phonemes; P4 no new GPCs, adjacent consonants; P5 alternative spellings AND alternative pronunciations; P6 spelling/fluency. DfE validated list: 45 programmes after 3 rounds — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/choosing-a-phonics-teaching-programme/list-of-phonics-teaching-programmes
- Little Wandle (L&S Revised exemplar): Phase2 order s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f l ff ll ss j v w x y z zz qu ch sh th ng nk; tricky is I the as and has his her go no to into she he of we me be. Phase3 ai ee igh oa oo oo ar or ur ow oi ear air er; tricky was you they my by all are sure pure. 4 new GPCs/day Mon-Thu + Friday review; review daily/weekly/termly; Big Cat decodables matched to secure knowledge, read 3x (decoding/prosody/comprehension). Notably: pronunciation phrase/mnemonic per grapheme, 'Actions and songs are NOT used to avoid cognitive overload' (Sacred Heart school page) — direct counterpoint to Jolly.
- Sounds-Write (linguistic, speech-to-print): Initial Code Unit1 a i m s t, U2 n o p, U3 b c g h, U4 d f e v, U5 k l r u, U6 j w z, U7 x y ff ll ss zz; then Units 8-10 same code in CVCC/CCVC/CCVCC + sound-swap with pseudowords; Extended Code organised BY PHONEME: e.g. /ae/ spellings ea(steak) ai ay a-e; /ee/ e y ee ea ie; alternative-pronunciation units e.g. ea = head/steam/steak. 4 concepts: letters symbolise sounds; a sound = 1-4 letters; same sound, many spellings; same spelling, many sounds. ~175 sound-spelling correspondences, 44 sounds. Sources: https://highcliffeprimary.org/reading/ ; https://files.schudio.com/anthony-curton-cofe-primary-school/files/documents/Phonics_Screening_Guidance.pdf
- Phonics International (Hepplewhite): Alphabetic Code Chart organised by phoneme (rows = sounds), each row lists ALL graphemes for that sound with key picture+word, simple→complex left to right; reverse complexity explicit: 'ough' = /oa/ though, /or/ thought, /oo/ through, /ou/ plough, /u/ thorough. Teaching point for ea→/ai/: only break/steak/great. Closest existing thing to a complete per-sound inventory. https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/b1-dh-alph-code-overview-with-teaching-points-colour/66606177
- UFLI Foundations: 128 lessons, 8-step 2-day routine: 1 PA, 2 Visual Drill (see grapheme→say sound), 3 Auditory Drill (hear sound→write grapheme), 4 Blending Drill, 5 New Concept, 6 Word Work, 7 Irregular Words, 8 Connected Text. Blending starts after only 4 letters (m t s a, Lesson 5 words/sentences, Lesson 8 decodable stories). FREE digital apps: Virtual Blending Board (teacher projects, builds words from word chains, students read) + Virtual Word Work Mats Beginner (to L43) / Intermediate (L44+ digraphs) — drag/manipulate grapheme tiles to encode+decode word chains (one phoneme changes at a time). Sound wall cards, grapheme cards, heart word cards free in Toolbox. https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/toolbox-aus/apps2/ ; https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/toolbox/
- Lexia Core5: now described as 21 levels (older docs 18 levels/89 activities, 5 activities/level); auto-placement; 6 strands incl phonics + structural analysis (morphology); 3-step branching per unit: Practice → Standard → Instruction step (explicit re-teach + scaffolding); repeated branching flags 'struggling' in myLexia → teacher delivers scripted Lexia Lesson; level completion generates paper Skill Builder. 'Assessment Without Testing' = embedded assessment. EEF effectiveness trial: +2 months reading for struggling readers, 4-padlock security; EAL subgroup +3 months (small n, caution). NI RCT (O'Callaghan et al 2016, n=98, 8 wks): significant gains blending (partial eta2 .064) + nonword reading (.070), small effects, maintained at 2 mo; 35% made no gains (per paper excerpt). https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/promising-programmes/lexia-reading-core5
- Reading Eggs: core = 120 lessons, 12 maps x 10 lessons + end-of-map quiz, placement test, 6-11 activities/lesson; each letter own lesson; onset-rime + word families prominent (analytic flavour alongside synthetic claims). Fast Phonics = separate purer SSP strand: 20 'Peaks', up to 24 parts each, Letters-and-Sounds-aligned. Spelling Skills Bank 96 lessons. Evidence weak: Lowery 2017 action research n=31, 6 wks, Fountas&Pinnell measure (criticised measure) — ESSA III claim rests on thin base; phonics.org review flags pace/accuracy issues. https://readingeggs.com.au/articles/reading-eggs-or-fast-phonics-which-reading-program-to-use-for-students/ ; https://www.phonics.org/reading-eggs-app-review-for-phonics/
- Hooked on Phonics: Learn-Practice-Read-Celebrate; Pre-reader (alphabet/sounds via songs) → Learn to Read; each step 3 lessons; video introduces sound/pattern (-at, -an, sh) → game → decodable book using only taught sounds, after EVERY lesson; monthly physical Practice Pack (workbook+2-3 books+stickers). CSM: must complete level to unlock next BUT songs/games/books also freely browsable in separate section — hybrid gating. https://www.hookedonphonics.com/access-resources/faqs/ ; https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/hooked-on-phonics-learn-read
- Khan Academy Kids: phonics is one strand in whole-child app, free/no ads; Letters tab (letter sounds with Ollo), Reading tab 'Reading Foundational Skills' (Blend Sounds 1, Beginning Sounds 1, End Sounds...); tracing/word building; decodable + read-aloud library with word highlighting. No published GPC sequence/mastery gating comparable to SSP programmes; browse-heavy library model. https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403698893965-Phonics-The-building-blocks-of-literacy ; https://www.khanacademy.org/kids/ela
- IXL: not a teaching programme but a skill-index: hundreds of micro-skills (Blend onset and rime QQR; Blend each sound 6SJ; Find short a word...), SmartScore mastery, diagnostic-driven recommendations; scope doc cites Scarborough/Ehri/NRP; K: letter-sounds→short vowels→digraphs ch sh th→blends; HFW 'irregular' words only AFTER CVC decoding solid. Best-in-class for granular skill taxonomy + assessment-as-practice, weakest for explicit teaching (short videos only). https://www.ixl.com/materials/Reading_Foundations_Scope_and_Sequence.pdf
- Alphablocks (BBC/CBeebies): 26 living letters; holding hands = blending made visible (c-a-t, CAT! + word 'comes to life' if real word = instant semantic feedback); digraphs = characters FUSE into one body; Magic E fires a beam to partner vowel (split digraph visualised); levels: Red (letters), Yellow (blending, s a t p start), Green (digraph starter set ch sh th ng ai ee oa oo ar or ur ow oi + trigraphs igh ear air ure), Blue (blends/clusters, no new sound), next: alternative spellings (ai sail vs ay say; ee bee vs ea beat) + exception words introduced gradually. Free browse (any episode any time) — no gating/assessment at all. British pronunciation. Guide: https://www.bbc.co.uk/cbeebies/grownups/the-alphablocks-guide-to-phonics (via mirror https://meleleh.pages.dev/cbeebies/grownups/the-alphablocks-guide-to-phonics)
- Orton-Gillingham apps: Nessy (Bristol Dyslexia Centre) — OG in game world, videos/games, mnemonics (necessary = one collar two sleeves), b/d stories; Reading Horizons = faithful OG digital tutor, dated/school-like. Evidence: Stevens et al 2021 meta-analysis (24 studies): OG interventions NOT statistically significant for word reading/phonics/fluency/vocab/comprehension (ES .32 foundational, ns); Schlesinger & Gray 2017: multisensory version = no added benefit over structured literacy alone. Sources: https://tatyanaelleseff.com/og-limitations/ ; https://www.structural-learning.com/post/nessy
