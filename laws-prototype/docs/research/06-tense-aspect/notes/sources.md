- Lingolia interactive timeline https://english.lingolia.com/en/grammar/tenses/timeline — click tense/aspect; same cake event across 12; wording explicitly viewpoint: progressive 'you view an action as ongoing, incomplete or temporary', perfect 'you view a completed action as connected to a later time', result emphasised.
- Perfect English Grammar (Seonaid) use https://www.perfect-english-grammar.com/present-perfect-use.html — taxonomy: unfinished actions (since/for, stative), finished: life experience (ever/never, alive), unfinished time word (this week/today, can't with finished word), finished action with present result (lost keys so can't get in), recent news (just/yet/already); been=visited and came back, gone=there now.
- EnglishPage present perfect https://www.englishpage.com/verbpage/presentperfect.html — diagrams: Xs at unspecified time before now, limiting the window (in the last year vs last year), duration arrow to now for non-continuous verbs; topics Experience/Change/Accomplishments/Uncompleted expected/Multiple actions.
- AJAL 2026 compositional study http://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1511004.pdf — teaching tense (present/past/future) distinct from aspect (progressive, perfective/perfect), combining one at a time, vs traditional 12-tense model: benefits early EFL learners, reduced cognitive overload; advanced learners already exposed to 12 showed less improvement; authors argue shift away from 12-tense model, gradual for experienced. Also notes tense = Tr vs To (orientation time) relation — learner-facing Reichenbach-lite.
- CBI Iranian school https://ilt.atu.ac.ir/?_action=article&au=64455&_au=Rezvan++Harsij — Fazilatfar et al 2017: 28 pupils age 14 CBI (Gal'perin, Langacker CG concepts, SCOBA diagram Fig.1 orienting tense/lexical aspect/grammatical aspect under 'voice') 8 weeks vs 30 traditional; both improved, CBI significantly better on definitions, written discourse, grammar questions.
