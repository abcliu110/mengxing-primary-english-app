==English==

===Etymology===
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:bef}}
From {{inh|en|enm|bef|beef, bef, beof}}, borrowed from {{der|en|xno|beof}}, {{der|en|fro|buef}}, {{m|fro|boef||ox}} (modern {{m+|fr|bœuf}}); from {{der|en|la|bōs|bovem|ox}}, from {{der|en|itc-pro|*gʷōs}}, ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*gʷṓws}}. {{doublet|en|cow}}.

Beef in the sense of “a grudge, argument” was originally an American slang expression:<ref>{{cite-web|year=2016–2019|title=Origin of the Slang AmE and BrE Usage of “Beef”|titleurl=https://english.stackexchange.com/questions/336475/origin-of-the-slang-ame-and-bre-usage-of-beef|work=StackExchange|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20260206174321/https://english.stackexchange.com/questions/336475/origin-of-the-slang-ame-and-bre-usage-of-beef|archivedate=6 February 2026}}.</ref>
* attested as a verb “to complain” in 1888: “He'll beef an' kick like a steer an' let on he won't never wear 'em.”— ''New York World'', 13 May;
* attested as a noun “complaint, protest, grievance, sim.” in 1899: “He made a Horrible Beef because he couldn't get Loaf Sugar for his Coffee.”—''Fables in Slang'' (1900) by George Ade, page 80.

As to the possible origin of this American usage, it has been suggested that it can be traced back to a British expression for “alarm”, first recorded in 1725:<ref>''The New Canting Dictionary: Comprehending All the Terms, Ancient and Modern, Used in the Several Tribes of Gypsies, Beggars, Shoplifters, Highwaymen, Foot-pads'' etc. London.</ref>  "BEEF 'to alarm, as ''To cry beef  upon us''; they have discover'd us, and are in Pursuit of us". The term "beef" in this context would be a Cockney rhyming slang of {{m|en|thief}}. However, the continuous use of a similar expression, including its assumed semantic shift to 'complaint' in the United States from the 1880s onwards, needs further clarification.<ref>{{R:World Wide Words|entry=Beefing|url=qa/qa-bee2}}</ref>

====Usage notes====
* A [[calf]] is a bovine, so, technically, the flesh of a calf, used for food, is beef. However, it is not common to use this term for the flesh of a calf; instead, it is referred to with the more specific term {{m|en|veal}}.
