==English==

===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*gʷem-}}
A compound of the sources of {{af|en|be-|come}}.
From {{inh|en|enm|becomen}}, {{m|enm|bicumen}}, from {{inh|en|ang|becuman|t=to come (to), approach, arrive, enter, meet with, fall in with; happen, befall; befit}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*bikwemaną||to come around, come about, come across, come by}}, equivalent to {{prefix|en|be|t1=about, around|come}}. Cognate with {{cog|sco|becum||to come, arrive, reach a destination}}, {{cog|frr|bekommen}}, {{m|frr|bykommen||to come by, obtain, receive}}, {{cog|fy|bikomme||to come by, obtain, receive}}, {{cog|nl|bekomen||to come by, obtain, receive}}, {{cog|de|bekommen||to get, receive, obtain}}, {{cog|sv|bekomma||to receive, concern}}, {{cog|got|𐌱𐌹𐌵𐌹𐌼𐌰𐌽||to come upon one, befall}}. Sense of "befit, suit" due to influence from {{ncog|enm|cweme}}, {{m|enm|icweme}}, see {{m|en|queem}}. Displaced Old English {{lang|ang|''[[weorþan]]''}}.

====Usage notes====
* In Early Modern English, the second-person informal singular indicative verb forms used with {{m|en|thou}} were {{m|en|becomest}} in the present and {{m|en|becamest}} in the past tenses. Similarly, {{m|en|becometh}} was used as a third-person singular indicative present form.
* Also in Early Modern English, {{m|en||become}} (and certain other intransitive verbs, such as  {{m|en|come}} and {{m|en|go}}) used the auxiliary {{m|en|be}} rather than {{m|en|have}} for perfect aspect constructions. In current usage, {{m|en|have}} is standard, though ''be'' is still occasionally used poetically.
