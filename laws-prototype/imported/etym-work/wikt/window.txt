===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*h₂weh₁-|*h₃ekʷ-}}
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:wyndowe|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|wyndowe}}, {{m|enm|wyndown}}, from {{der|en|non|vindauga|t=window|lit=wind-eye; wind-hole}}, equivalent to {{com|en|wind|eye|nocat=1}}. Cognate with {{cog|sco|windae}} and {{m|sco|windock}}, {{cog|fo|vindeyga}}, {{cog|nb|vindu}}, {{cog|nn|vindauge}}, {{cog|da|vindue}}, archaic {{cog|sv|vindöga}}, {{cog|ovd|windog}}. Displaced native {{ncog|ang|ēagþȳrel|lit=eye hole}} (the rare direct descendant is {{m|en|eyethurl||window, pupil, etc.}}). The “windows” among early Germanic peoples were just unglazed holes (eyes) in the wall or roof that permitted wind to pass through {{rfv-etym|en}}.

For semantic parallels, compare {{cog|got|𐌰𐌿𐌲𐌰𐌳𐌰𐌿𐍂𐍉|t=window|lit=eye-door}}; {{cog|la-vul|*ventāna}}, from {{m|la|ventus||wind}}; and {{cog|sla-pro|*okъnò||window}}, from {{m|sla-pro|*ȍko||eye}}.
