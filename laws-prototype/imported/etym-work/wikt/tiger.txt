===Etymology 1===
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:tygre|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|tygre}}, in part from {{inh|en|ang|tigras}} (pl.), in part from {{der|en|xno|tigre}}, both from {{der|en|la|tigris}}, from {{der|en|grc|τίγρις}}, from {{der|en|ira|-}} (compare {{cog|ae|𐬙𐬌𐬔𐬭𐬌|t=arrow}}, {{m|ae|𐬙𐬌𐬖𐬭𐬀|t=pointed}}). Ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*(s)teyg-|t=to pierce, prick, be sharp}}. Compare English {{m|en|stick}}.

=====Usage notes=====
In heraldry, many writers use spellings such as {{m|en|tyger}} or {{m|en|tygre}} to distinguish the mythological beast from the natural tiger (also blazoned Bengal tiger), which also occurs in heraldry.
