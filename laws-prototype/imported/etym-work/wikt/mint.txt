===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*men- (think)}}
From {{inh|en|enm|mynt}}, {{m|enm|munet|münet|money, coin}}, from {{inh|en|ang|mynet||coin}}, from late {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*munit}}, from {{der|en|la|monēta||place for making coins, coined money}}, from the temple of {{w|Juno Moneta}} (named for {{m|la|Monēta}} mother of the Muses), where coins were made. {{doublet|en|money|manat}}. The verb is from the noun; {{cog|ang|mynetian||to mint}} is a parallel formation.

===Etymology 2===
[[Image:Mint.jpg|thumb|right|A mint plant.]]
{{etymon|en|id=plant|:inh|enm:mynte<id:plant>|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|mynte}}, from {{inh|en|ang|minte|t=mint plant}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*mintā|t=mint}}, from {{der|en|la|menta}}, probably from a lost Mediterranean language either through {{der|en|grc|μίνθη}}, {{m|grc|μίνθα}} or directly. Akin to {{cog|non|minta||mint}}. {{doublet|en|mentha}}.

===Etymology 2===
{{bor+|nb|en|mint}}, the adjective originally shortened from {{m|en|mint condition}}, referring to the condition of a newly minted coin. The underlying noun comes from {{der|nb|enm|mynt}}, from {{der|nb|ang|mynet|t=coin}}, from late {{der|nb|gmw-pro|*munit}}, borrowed from {{der|nb|la|monēta|t=mint; coined money}}. The Latin noun derives from {{m|la|Monēta}}, an epithet of {{w|Juno (mythology)|Juno}}, whose temple in Rome was associated with the mint. Its traditional connection with {{m|la|moneō|t=to remind; warn}} is disputed. {{unk|nb|title=The further origin of the divine name is uncertain}}. {{doublet|nb|mynt}}.

For the underlying coin-word family, compare {{cog|da|mønt}}, {{cog|sv|mynt}}, {{cog|de|Münze}}, {{cog|nl|munt|t=coin}} and {{cog|en|mint}}.
