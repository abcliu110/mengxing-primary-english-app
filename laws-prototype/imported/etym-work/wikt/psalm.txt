==English==

===Etymology===
From {{der|en|enm|salm}} or {{m|enm|psalme}}, from {{der|en|ang|psealm}}, later reinforced from {{der|en|fro|psalme}} (modern French {{m|fr|psaume}}), both from {{der|en|la|psalmus}}, from {{der|en|grc|ψαλμός||the sound emanating from twitching or twanging perhaps with the hands or fingers, mostly of musical strings}} (from {{m|grc|ψάλλω||to make a sound by striking, touching, plucking, rubbing, twanging, or vibrating}}), but later in New Testament times the meaning of {{m|grc|ψαλμός}} evolved from its Classical meaning of "a tune played to the harp" to a more general tune that could be played with any instrument; even a song sung with or without musical accompaniment. By the [[Byzantine]] Period, it lost all of its instrumental nuances.

==Swedish==

===Etymology===
From {{der|sv|gmq-osw|salmber}}, {{m|gmq-osw|psalmber}}, from {{der|sv|non|salmr}}, {{m|non|psalmr}}, from {{der|sv|la|psalmus}}, from {{der|sv|grc|ψαλμός||the sound emenating from twitching or twanging perhaps with the hands or fingers, mostly of musical strings}}.

===Noun===
{{sv-noun|c}}

# a [[hymn]], a church [[song]]
# a {{l|en|psalm}} of David, one of the chapters of the book of [[Psalms]]

====Usage notes====
* Denominations in Sweden outside of the Church of Sweden (e.g. baptists) have traditionally not used the word ''psalm'', but rather ''[[visa]], [[sång]]'' (song). Recent integrated hymnbooks are titled ''Psalmer och visor'' (1976) and ''Psalmer och Sånger'' (1987) to indicated that they cover both the Church of Sweden and other denominations.
