===Etymology 1===
{{root|nb|ine-pro|*Hrewp-}}
{{inh+|nb|da|rode|t=file; district}}, from {{der|nb|gmq-oda|rode|t=troop, group}} and {{m|gmq-oda|ruthe}}, via {{der|nb|gml|rote|t=troop, military unit}}, from {{der|nb|fro|rote|t=troop, group of armed men}}, from Medieval Latin {{m|la-med|rota}}, {{m|la-med|rotta}}, {{m|la-med|ruta}} and {{m|la-med|rutta|t=defeat; routed military unit}}, variants ultimately derived from Latin {{m|la|rupta|t=broken, routed}}, the feminine singular of {{m|la|ruptus|t=broken}}, the perfect passive participle of {{m|la|rumpō|t=to break, burst, tear}}. The Latin verb continues {{der|nb|itc-pro|*rumpō|t=to break}}, from Proto-Indo-European {{m|ine-pro|*Hrunépti|t=to be breaking}}, a nasal-infixed present formation of {{m|ine-pro|*Hrewp-|t=to break, tear up}}. The participle {{m|la|ruptus}} continues Proto-Italic {{m|itc-pro|*ruptos}}, ultimately from Proto-Indo-European {{m|ine-pro|*Hrup-tó-s|t=broken}}, another formation from the same root.

{{doublet|nb|rute|t1=route; timetable}}. Compare the parallel Scandinavian form {{cog|non|roti|t=crowd, troop}} (whence Nynorsk {{m|nn|rode}}), {{cog|sv|rote|t=district; military file}}, {{cog|de|Rotte|t=group, mob}} and {{cog|en|rout}}.

===Etymology 3===
[[File:Hanging crucifix.jpg|thumb|A hanging rood (crucifix).]]
{{lbor|nb|non|róða|t=cross, crucifix}}, revived as an archaizing literary word. The Old Norse term belongs to the {{m|gem-pro|*rōdō|t=rod, pole}} family. Its Christian sense “cross, crucifix” is closely associated with Old English {{m|ang|rōd|t=cross, crucifix}}. The deeper origin of Proto-Germanic {{m|gem-pro|*rōdō}} is uncertain; it has been compared with Proto-Indo-European {{m|ine-pro|*reh₁t-|t=post, beam, pole}}.

Compare {{cog|osx|rōda|t=rod, pole}}, {{cog|odt|ruoda|t=rod, pole}}, {{cog|goh|ruota|t=rod, staff}} and {{cog|en|rood|t=cross, crucifix}}.
