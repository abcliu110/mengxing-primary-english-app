=====Usage notes=====
In British English, the word ''noodle'' ''(sense 1)'' is chiefly used to describe Asian or northern-European food items comprising long, thin strands of dough. In American English, ''noodle'' can also refer to Italian pasta which in British English would only be referred to as {{m|en|pasta}}.<ref name="OED"/><ref>{{cite-web|author=Lynne Murphy|authorlink=Lynne Murphy|title=Noodles|work=Separated by a Common Language: Observations on British and American English by an American Linguist in the UK|titleurl=https://separatedbyacommonlanguage.blogspot.com/2015/08/noodles.html|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20220120141120/https://separatedbyacommonlanguage.blogspot.com/2015/08/noodles.html|archivedate=20 January 2022|date=12 August 2015}}.</ref>

===Etymology 4===
The origin of the {{glossary|verb}} is uncertain; it is possibly derived:<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''v.''{{sup|5}}|noformat=1|id=128176|date=March 2022|nodot=1}}; {{R:Lexico|alt=noodle{{sup|3}}|pos=v}}</ref>

* from {{m|en|[[noodle#Verb 2|noodle]]|t=to hum or sing (a tune) at a low pitch or volume}} {{qualifier|Shetland}} (see [[#Etymology 3|etymology 3]]); or
* from {{der|en|de|nudeln|t=to make music or sing listlessly; to make music or sing at a low pitch or volume, or in an improvisatory manner}} {{qualifier|Leipzig}}, apparently from {{m|de|Nudel|t=piece of pasta, noodle}} (see [[#Etymology 1|etymology 1]]) + {{m|de|-n}} (a variant of {{m|de|-en|pos={{glossary|suffix}} forming the {{glossary|infinitive}}s of verbs}}).

The word was probably also influenced by {{m|en|doodle|t=to draw or scribble aimlessly|pos=verb}}.

The {{glossary|noun}} is probably derived from the verb, though the verb is first attested later. The noun was probably also influenced by {{m|en|doodle|t=small mindless sketch|pos=noun}}.<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''n.''{{sup|3}}|noformat=1|id=128174|date=March 2022}}</ref>
