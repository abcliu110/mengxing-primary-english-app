==English==

===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|forfait}} from ca. 1300, from {{der|en|fro|forfait||crime}}, originally the past participle of {{m|fro|forfaire||to transgress}}, and {{der|en|la-med|foris factum}}. During the 15th century, the sense shifted from the crime to the penalty for the crime.
{{root|en|ine-pro|*dʰeh₁-}}

===Verb===
{{en-verb|+|+|+,~<l:rare>}}

# To [[suffer]] the loss of something by wrongdoing or non-compliance
#: {{ux|en|He '''forfeited''' his last chance of an early release from jail by repeatedly attacking another inmate.}}
# To lose a [[contest]], [[game]], [[match]], or other form of competition by voluntary withdrawal, by failing to attend or participate, or by violation of the rules
#: {{syn|en|capitulate|surrender|disqualify|forgo|withgo}}
#: {{ux|en|Because only nine players were present, the football team was forced to '''forfeit''' the game.}}
# To be guilty of a misdeed; to be criminal; to transgress.
# To fail to keep an obligation.
#* {{RQ:Shakespeare Merchant of Venice|III|i|passage=I will have the heart of him if he '''forfeit'''.}}
# {{lb|en|law}} Of government officials: to legally remove property from its previous owners.
#* {{quote-book|en|date=23 January 2024|author=Judge Milan D. Smith, Jr.|title=Snitko v. United States|url=https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/01/23/22-56050.pdf|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20240124033957/https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/01/23/22-56050.pdf|passage=After the raid on USPV, Plaintiffs filed claims with the FBI seeking return of their seized property. The government did not return the property in response to these claims; instead, it indicated that it was seeking to '''forfeit''' the property.}}
