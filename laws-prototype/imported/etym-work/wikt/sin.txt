=====Usage notes=====
* {{m|gml||Wēsen}} is a verb with a [[suppletive]] conjugation based on multiple Proto-Germanic stems. For many verb forms, authors freely chose between forms based on the stems ''wēs-'' and ''sî-'', without [[semantic]] impact. This is also true for modern Low German and Dutch. For the forms based on the ''sî-'' stem, see the respective entry at {{m|gml|wesen|wēsen}}.

===Etymology 1===
{{PIE word|nb|swé}}
{{inh+|nb|gmq-mno|sin|t=one's own}}, attested in a document from 1418,<ref>Endre Mørck (2015), “[https://ojs.novus.no/index.php/MOM/article/download/1209/1198 Hovedtrekk ved akkusativ med infinitiv i mellomnorsk]”, in ''Maal og Minne'', number 2, page 60, example 24a, citing ''Diplomatarium Norvegicum'' I, 657 (1418).</ref> from {{inh|nb|non|sínn|t=one's own}}, from {{inh|nb|gem-pro|*sīnaz|t=one's own}}. =====Usage notes=====
* {{m|nb|sin|Sin}} agrees in gender and number with the possessed noun, not with its owner: masculine {{m|nb|sin}}, feminine {{m|nb|si}}, neuter {{m|nb|sitt}}, plural {{m|nb|sine}}. The same forms are used with singular and plural possessors.
*: {{uxi|nb|Hun tok [[sekk|sekken]] '''sin''', [[bok|boka]] '''si''', [[pass|passet]] '''sitt''' og [[nøkkel|nøklene]] '''sine'''.|She took her bag, her book, her passport and her keys.}}
* A possessive normally precedes a noun in its indefinite form or follows a noun in its definite form: ''sin [[bil]]'' versus ''[[bil|bilen]] sin''. Some kinship expressions allow an indefinite noun before the possessive, as in ''[[bror]] sin'' (“his/her/their brother”).
* In ordinary reflexive use, {{m|nb|sin}} normally refers to the subject of its own clause. Compare:
*: {{uxi|nb|[[hun|Hun]] [[besøke|besøkte]] [[mor|moren]] '''sin'''.|She visited her own mother.}}
*: {{uxi|nb|[[hun|Hun]] [[besøke|besøkte]] [[mor|moren]] '''[[hennes]]'''.|She visited another woman's mother.}}
* Reference from a subordinate clause to the subject of a higher clause occurs in many dialects, but standard written usage generally requires {{m|nb|hans}}, {{m|nb|hennes}} or {{m|nb|deres}} instead.<ref>{{cite-web|url=https://sprakradet.no/spraksporsmal-og-svar/hvordan-syklene-sine-deres-ser-ut/|title=Hvordan syklene sine/deres ser ut|work=Språkrådet}}</ref>
* Established expressions can refer to another constituent rather than the grammatical subject. Examples include ''i sin [[helhet]]'' (“in its entirety”), ''i sin [[alminnelighet]]'' (“in general”) and ''[[overlate]] noen til sin [[skjebne]]'' (“to leave someone to their fate”).<ref>{{cite-web|url=https://sprakradet.no/spraksporsmal-og-svar/i-sin-alminnelighet-eller-i-dens-dets-alminnelighet/|title=I SIN alminnelighet eller i DENS/DETS alminnelighet?|work=Språkrådet}}</ref>
*: {{uxi|nb|Hun [[lese|leste]] [[rapport|rapporten]] i '''sin''' [[helhet]].|She read the report in its entirety.}}
* In the {{w|lang=no|Garpegenitiv|garpegenitiv}} construction, the preceding noun phrase identifies the possessor. This construction is common in speech and informal writing; the possessor need not be the subject of the clause.
*: {{uxi|nb|Hun [[låne|lånte]] [[Per]] '''sin''' [[bil]].|She borrowed Per's car.}}
