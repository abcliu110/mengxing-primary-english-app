===Etymology===
Alteration (by [[folk etymology|folk etymological]] influence of {{m|en|fish}}) of {{der|en|enm|crevis}} (whence modern dialectal {{m|en|crevis}}), from {{der|en|fro|crevice}} {{gloss|"crayfish"; > Modern French: {{m|fr|écrevisse}}}}, from {{der|en|frk|*krabitjō|*krebitja|t=crayfish}}, diminutive of {{der|en|frk|*krabit|*krebit|t=crab}}, from {{der|en|gem-pro|*krabitaz||crab, cancer}}, possibly from {{der|en|ine-pro|*gerbʰ-}}, {{m|ine-pro|*grebʰ-||to scratch, crawl}}, or from a {{der|en|qfa-sub}} word folk-etymologically influenced by this root.

Akin to {{cog|goh|krebiz}} (Modern {{cog|de|Krebs||crustacean, crab, crayfish}}), {{cog|gml|krēvet||crab, crayfish}}, {{cog|nl|kreeft||crayfish, lobster}}, {{cog|ang|crabba||crab}}. More at {{l|en|crab}}.

===Noun===
{{en-noun|+|*}}

# Any of numerous [[freshwater]] [[decapod]] [[crustacean]]s in [[superfamily]] {{taxlink|Astacoidea|superfamily}} or {{taxlink|Parastacoidea|superfamily}}, resembling the related [[lobster]] but usually much smaller.
## {{lb|en|New England|Pennsylvania|Upper Midwest US}} A freshwater [[crustacean]] (family {{taxlink|Cambaridae|family}}), sometimes used as an inexpensive [[seafood]] or as fish [[bait]].
# {{lb|en|AU|NZ|South Africa}} A [[rock lobster]] (family {{taxfmt|Palinuridae|family}}).
# {{lb|en|AU}} A [[freshwater crayfish]] (family {{taxfmt|Parastacidae|family}}), such as the [[gilgie]], [[marron]], or [[yabby]].
# {{lb|en|Singapore}} The species {{taxlink|Thenus orientalis|species}} of the [[slipper lobster]] family ({{taxfmt|Scyllaridae|family}}).

====Usage notes====
* Within the US, the term ''crayfish'' predominated in the region of New England and in New York, Pennsylvania, Michigan, Wisconsin{{,}} and Minnesota. In much of the United States—in the South, especially in Louisiana and Texas; in the Midwest and in the West—''crawfish'' predominated. In a belt stretching across Kentucky through Missouri, Arkansas, Kansas{{,}} and Oklahoma, and in Oregon and northern California, the term ''crawdad'' predominated. Over the course of the 21st century, crawdad and crayfish have declined in the US in favor of crawfish.<ref>{{cite-web |title=Archived copy |url=http://spark-1590165977.us-west-2.elb.amazonaws.com/jkatz/SurveyMaps/ |accessdate=29 July 2013 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130606184957/http://spark-1590165977.us-west-2.elb.amazonaws.com/jkatz/SurveyMaps/ |archivedate=6 June 2013 }}</ref>
