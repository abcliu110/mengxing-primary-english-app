===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*(s)mey-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|mirour}}, from {{der|en|fro|mireor|t=mirror|lit=looker, watcher}}, from {{m|fro|mirer|t=look at}}, from {{der|en|la|mīror||wonder at}}, from {{m|la|mīrus||wonderful}}, from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*smey-|t=to laugh, to be glad}}. Displaced native {{ncog|enm|schewere}}, {{m|enm|schawere}}, from {{ncog|ang|sċēawere|t=mirror|lit=watcher}}, which was also the word for "spy".

===Noun===
{{en-noun}}

# {{senseid|en|Q35197}} A smooth surface, usually made of [[glass]] with [[reflective]] material painted on the underside, that reflects light so as to give an [[image]] of what is in front of it.
#: {{syn|en|glass|looking-glass}}
#: {{ux|en|I had a look in the '''mirror''' to see if the blood had come off my face.}}
#: {{ux|en| He broke the '''mirror''' with the pickaxe.}}
#: {{ux|en|We could see the lorry in the '''mirror''', so decided to change lanes.}}
# {{lb|en|figuratively}} An object, person, or event that reflects or gives a picture of another.
#: {{ux|en|His story is a '''mirror''' into the life of orphans growing up.}}
#* {{RQ:Spenser Faerie Queene|passage=O goddess, heavenly bright, / '''Mirror''' of grace and majesty divine.}}
# {{senseid|en|Q1938536}} {{lb|en|computing|Internet}} A [[website]] or [[server]] that contains [[replicate]]d data from another site.
#: {{ux|en|Although the content had been deleted from his blog, it was still found on some '''mirrors'''.}}
# A [[mirror carp]].
# {{lb|en|historical}} An ethical-didactic [[treatise]] that details [[exemplary]] conduct through models, advising kings, princes, clergy, etc. on how to behave. <!--see e.g. "Mirrors for princes" on Wikipedia, or "The Mirror for Magistrates"-->
