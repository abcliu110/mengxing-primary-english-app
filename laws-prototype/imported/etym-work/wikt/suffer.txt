====Usage notes====
Many people advise against using this word in the context of disabilities. Instead of saying that someone ''suffers'' from [[blindness]] or some other disability, it is better to use more neutral wording, such as saying that the person ''has'' the disability.<ref>[https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-communication/inclusive-language-words-to-use-and-avoid-when-writing-about-disability Inclusive language: words to use and avoid when writing about disability]</ref><ref>{{cite-web |url=https://service-manual.nhs.uk/content/inclusive-language |accessdate=12 July 2020 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20200628051812/https://service-manual.nhs.uk/content/inclusive-language |archivedate=28 June 2020 }}</ref><ref>{{cite-web |url=https://www.remploy.co.uk/employers/resources/z-disabilities/disability-etiquette |title=Remploy: Disability etiquette |accessdate=12 July 2020 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20200625211059/https://www.remploy.co.uk/employers/resources/z-disabilities/disability-etiquette |archivedate=25 June 2020 }}</ref>

For example ‘to suffer from drought’ is neutral. But this collocation ‘to suffer from asthma or Down syndrome’ can be offensive to some people so it is better to say that a person has asthma.
