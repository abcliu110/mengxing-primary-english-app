===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=adjective|:inh|enm:fast<ety:inh<ang:fæst>>|tree=1}}
{{root|en|ine-pro|*peh₂ǵ-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|fast}}, {{m|enm|fest}}, from {{inh|en|ang|fæst|t=firm, secure}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*fast}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*fastuz}}; see it for cognates and further etymology.

The development of “rapid” from an original sense of “secure” apparently happened first in the adverb and then transferred to the adjective; compare {{m|en|hard}} in expressions like “to run hard”. The original sense of “secure, firm” is now slightly archaic, but retained in the related {{m|en|fasten|t=make secure}}. Also compare close meaning change from {{noncog|la|rapiō|t=to snatch}} to {{noncog|la|rapidus|t=rapid, quick}}, from {{noncog|ga|sciob|t=to snatch}} to {{noncog|ga|sciobtha|t=quick}}.

=====Usage notes=====
* In the context of nuclear reactors or weaponry, fission-spectrum neutrons (neutrons with the spectrum of energies produced by nuclear fission) are frequently referred to as {{m|en|fast}} neutrons, even though the majority of fission-spectrum neutrons have energies below the 1-million-electron-volt cutoff.

===Etymology 3===
From {{inh|en|enm|fasten}}, from {{inh|en|ang|fæstan|t=to fast}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*fastēn}}, from
{{inh|en|gem-pro|*fastāną|t=to fast}}, from the same root as {{inh|en|gem-pro|*fastijaną|t=to fasten}}, derived from {{m|gem-pro|*fastuz}}, and thereby related to Etymology 1.
The religious sense is presumably introduced in the Gothic church, from {{cog|got|𐍆𐌰𐍃𐍄𐌰𐌽||hold fast (viz. to the rule of abstinence)}}. This semantic development is unique to Gothic, the term glosses Greek {{m|grc|νηστεύω}}, Latin {{m|la|ieiuno}} which do not have similar connotations of "holding fast".
The feminine noun {{cog|goh|fasta}} likely existed in the 8th century (shift to neuter {{cog|goh|fasten}} from the 9th century, whence modern {{cog|de|Fasten}}).
The Old English noun originally had the sense "fortress, enclosure" and takes the religious sense only in late Old English, perhaps influenced by {{der|en|non|fasta}}.
The use for reduced nutrition intake for medical reasons or for weight reduction develops by the mid-1970s, back-formed from the use of the verbal noun {{m|en|fasting}} in this sense (1960s).
