===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*dʰeh₁-}}
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:effect<ety:bor<fro:effect>>|id=noun|tree=1}}
Of the noun: from {{inh|en|enm|effect}}, from {{der|en|fro|effect}} (modern {{cog|fr|effet}}), from {{der|en|la|effectus||an effect, tendency, purpose}}, from {{m|la|efficiō||accomplish, complete, effect}}; see ''effect'' as a verb. Displaced {{inh|en|ang|fremming}}, {{m|ang|fremednes}} from {{m|ang|fremman}}.

{{ety|en|:inh|enm:effecten<ety:der<la-med:effectuō>>|id=verb|tree=1}}
Of the verb: from {{inh|en|enm|effecten}}, partly from {{der|en|la-med|effectuō}}, from {{der|en|la|effectus}}, {{glossary|perfect}} {{glossary|passive}} {{glossary|participle}} of {{m|la|efficiō||accomplish, complete, do, effect}}, from {{m|la|ex||out}} + {{m|la|faciō||do, make}} (see {{m|en|fact}} and compare {{m|en|affect}}, {{m|en|infect}}) and partly from the noun ''effect''.

====Usage notes====
''Effect'' is often confused with {{m|en|affect}}. “Effect” indicates the manifestation of new ideas or entities, while “affect” usually suggests influence over existing ideas, emotions, and entities:
* “New governing coalitions have '''effected''' major changes” indicates that major changes were made as a result of new governing coalitions.
* “New governing coalitions have '''affected''' major changes” indicates that before new governing coalitions, major changes were in place, and that the new governing coalitions had some influence over those existing changes.
