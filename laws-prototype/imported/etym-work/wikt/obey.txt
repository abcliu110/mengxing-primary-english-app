==English==

===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|obeyen}}, from {{der|en|xno|obeir}}, {{m|fro|obeier}} et al., {{der|en|fro|obeir}}, from {{der|en|la|oboediō}} (also {{m|la|obēdiō|t=to listen to, harken, usually in extended sense, obey, be subject to, serve}}), from {{m|la|ob-||before, near}} + {{m|la|audiō|t=to hear}}. Compare {{m|en|audient}}. In Latin, ''ob'' + ''audire'' would have been expected to become Classical Latin *''obūdiō'' (compare ''in'' + ''claudō'' becoming ''inclūdō''), but it has been theorized that the usual law court associations of the word for obeying encouraged a false [[archaism]] from ''ū'' to ''oe'', to ''oboediō'' (compare Old Latin ''oinos'' → Classical Latin ''ūnus'').<ref>page 220, ''The Latin Language'' by L.R.Palmer ({{ISBN|080612136X}}, {{ISBN|9780806121369}}), and [https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=O7z4Sl-SgFEC&pg=PA220&lpg=PA220&dq=obudio+AND+oboedio&source=bl&ots=rMMoXv_x7s&sig=JuKhRGXa0GspnWvah8G3ZvUMbLA&hl=en&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwjM0Y6cmIDUAhUBD8AKHWKnAV0Q6AEIIzAA#v=onepage&q=obudio%20AND%20oboedio&f=false online at this link].</ref>
