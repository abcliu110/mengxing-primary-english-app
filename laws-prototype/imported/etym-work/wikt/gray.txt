===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*gʰreh₁-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|gray}}, from {{inh|en|ang|grǣġ}} (West Saxon). The spelling {{m|en|gray}} reflects the West Saxon vowel development, whereas the variant {{m|en|grey}} stems from the Anglian form {{m|ang|grēġ}} (through Middle English {{m|enm|grey}}). Further derived from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*grāu}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*grēwaz|t=grey}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*ǵʰreh₁-|t=to green, to grow}}.

Cognate with {{cog|fy|grau|t=grey}}, {{cog|nl|grauw|t=grey}}, {{cog|nds-de|grau}}, {{m|nds-de|graag|t=grey}}, {{cog|de|grau|t=grey}}, {{cog|sv|grå|t=grey}}, {{cog|is|grár|t=grey}}, {{cog|la|rāvus|t=tawny, grey}}, {{cog|cu|зьрѭ|t=to see, to glance}}, archaic {{cog|ru|зреть|t=to watch, to look at}}, {{cog|lt|žeriù|t=to shine}}.

=====Usage notes=====
* In the early 20th century, an attempt was made to introduce an artificial distinction between ''gray'' and {{m|en|grey}}, with the former being used for a "mixture of white and blue", and the latter for a "mixture of white and black";<ref>{{R:Jespersen MEGoHP|I|3.61|96}}</ref> this has not been generally adopted.
