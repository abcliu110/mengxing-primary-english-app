===Etymology 1===
{{inh+|en|enm|aken|pos=verb}}, and {{m|enm|ache|pos=noun}}, from {{inh|en|ang|acan|pos=verb}} (from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*akan}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*akaną|id=ache|t=to ache}}) and {{m|ang|æċe|pos=noun}} (from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*aki}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*akiz}}), traditionally explained as from a {{inh|en|ine-pro|*h₂égos|nocat=1}}.

Cognate with {{cog|stq|eeke}}, {{m|stq|ääke|t=to ache, fester}}, {{cog|nds|aken}}, {{m|nds|achen}}, {{m|nds|äken|t=to hurt, ache}}, {{cog|nds-de|Eek|t=inflammation}}, {{cog|frr|akelig}}, {{m|frr|æklig|t=terrible, miserable, sharp, intense}}, {{cog|fy|aaklik|t=nasty, horrible, dismal, dreary}}, {{cog|nl|akelig|t=nasty, horrible}}.

The verb was originally strong, conjugating for tense like ''[[take]]'' (e.g. ''I '''ake''''', ''I '''ook''''', ''I have '''aken'''''), but gradually became weak during Middle English; the noun was originally pronounced as {{IPAchar|/eɪt͡ʃ/}} as spelled (compare {{m|en|breach}}, from {{m|en|break}}). Historically the verb was spelled '''[[ake]]''', and the noun '''ache''' (even after the form {{IPAchar|/eɪk/}} started to become common for the noun; compare again {{m|en|break}} which is now also a noun). The verb came to be spelled like the noun when lexicographer [[:w:Samuel Johnson|Samuel Johnson]] mistakenly assumed that it derived from {{noncog|grc|ἄχος|t=pain}} due to the similarity in form and meaning of the two words.
