=====Usage notes=====
* {{m|en|my|My}} and {{m|en||mine}} are essentially two forms of the same word, with {{m|en||my}} being used [[attributive]]ly before the noun, and {{m|en||mine}} being used in all other cases, as may be seen in most of the usage examples and quotations above. In this respect, this word is analogous to most of the other possessive pronouns (e.g. {{m|en|your}} vs. {{m|en|yours}}), as well as a number of other noun modifiers, such as {{m|en|lone}}/{{m|en|alone}}.
* Historically, {{m|en||my}} came to be used only before a consonant sound, and later came to be used regardless of the following sound. Nonetheless, {{m|en||mine}} still sees archaic pre-vocalic use, as may be seen in the 1862 quotation above, and in the most formal of writing even into the 20th century.  In later archaic use, the restriction against {{m|en||mine}} being used before nouns starting with a consonant is no longer reliably respected, as may be seen in the 1936 and 2023 quotations above.

#: {{uxi|nb|Beløpet tilsvarte ti '''miner''' sølv.|The amount was equivalent to ten minas of silver.}}
#* {{quote-book|nb|year=2024|title=Bibelen|chapter=Lukas 19:16|publisher=Bibelselskapet|url=https://bibel.no/nettbibelen/les/nb-2024/LUK/LUK.19|passage=Herre, den ene '''minen''' sølv du ga meg, har kastet av seg ti.|translation=Lord, the one mina of silver you gave me has yielded ten.}}

=====Usage notes=====
An ancient Greek ''mine'' comprised 100 drachmas; a Syrian or Hebrew ''mine'' comprised 50 shekels. It was commonly one sixtieth of a talent. The later Greek royal ''mine'' was 1.5 kilograms, and the name was also used for the kilogram.<ref>{{R:SNL|mine_-_enhet}}</ref>
