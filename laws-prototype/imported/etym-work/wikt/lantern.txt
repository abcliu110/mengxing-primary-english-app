===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|lanterne}} (13th century), via {{der|en|fro|lanterne}} from {{der|en|la|lanterna||lantern}}, itself a corruption of {{der|en|grc|λαμπτήρ||torch}} (see {{m|en|lamp}}, {{m|grc|λάμπω}}) by influence of {{der|en|la|lucerna||lamp}}. The spelling ''[[lanthorn]]'' was current during the 16th to 19th centuries and originates with a folk etymology associating the word with the use of [[horn]] as translucent cover. For the verb, compare {{cog|fr|lanterner}} to hang at the lamppost. Displaced native {{ncog|ang|lēohtfæt|lit=light-container}}.

===Noun===
{{en-noun}}

# A case of [[translucent]] or [[transparent]] material made to protect a flame, or light, used to [[illuminate]] its surroundings.
# {{lb|en|theatre}} Especially, a metal casing with lens used to illuminate a stage (e.g. spotlight, floodlight).
# {{lb|en|architectural element}} An open structure of light material set upon a roof, to give light and air to the interior.
#* {{RQ:Dickens Bleak House|text=On such an afternoon, if ever, the Lord High Chancellor ought to be sitting here—as here he is—with a foggy glory round his head, softly fenced in with crimson cloth and curtains, addressed by a large advocate with great whiskers, a little voice, and an interminable brief, and outwardly directing his contemplation to the '''lantern''' in the roof, where he can see nothing but fog.|1}}
#* {{quote-journal|en|date=2022 January 12|author=Paul Bigland|title=Fab Four: the nation's finest stations: Eastbourne|journal=RAIL|issue=948|page=27|text=The station also boasts a large semi-domed French pavilion roof with fish-scale tiles and iron cresting, plus a rectangular hall with arcaded upper storey and wooden '''lantern'''.}}
# {{lb|en|architecture}} A cage or open chamber of rich architecture, open below into the building or tower which it crowns.
# {{lb|en|architecture}} A smaller and secondary [[cupola]] crowning a larger one, for ornament, or to admit light.
#: {{ux|en|the '''lantern''' of the cupola of the Capitol at Washington, or that of the Florence cathedral}}
# {{lb|en|engineering}} A [[lantern pinion]] or [[trundle wheel]].
# {{lb|en|steam engines}} A kind of cage inserted in a stuffing box and surrounding a piston rod, to separate the packing into two parts and form a chamber between for the reception of steam, etc.; a [[lantern brass]].
# {{lb|en|rail transport}} A [[light]] formerly used as a [[signal]] by a railway [[guard]] or [[conductor]] at night.
# {{lb|en|metalworking}} A [[perforated]] [[barrel]] to form a [[core]] upon.
# {{lb|en|zoology}} [[Aristotle's lantern]]
