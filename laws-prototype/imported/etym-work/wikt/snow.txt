The {{glossary|noun}} is derived from {{inh|en|enm|snaw}}, {{m|enm|snou}}, {{m|enm|snow|t=snow; accumulation of snow; snowfall; snowstorm; whiteness}},<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=snou|pos=n|id=MED41264}}</ref> from {{inh|en|ang|snāw|t=snow}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*snaiw|t=snow}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*snaiwaz|t=snow}},<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''n.''{{sup|1}}|noformat=1|id=3247599631|date=December 2024|nodot=1}}; {{R:Lexico|pos=n|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220702143727/https://www.lexico.com/definition/snow}}</ref> from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*snóygʷʰos|t=snow}}, from {{m|ine-pro|*sneygʷʰ-|t=to snow}}.

The {{glossary|verb}} is derived from {{inh|en|enm|snouen|t=to snow; (''figurative'') to shower}}, from {{m|enm|snou}}, {{m|enm|snow|pos=noun}} (see above)<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=snouen|pos=''v.''(1)|noformat=1|id=MED41266}}</ref><ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=v|id=1008597063|date=March 2024|nodot=1}}; {{R:Lexico|pos=v|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220702143727/https://www.lexico.com/definition/snow}}</ref> + {{m|enm|-en|pos={{glossary|suffix}} forming the {{glossary|infinitive}} of verbs}}.<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=-en|pos=''suf.''(3)|noformat=1|id=MED13518}}</ref> {{displaced|ang|snīwan}}, whence {{cog|en|snew}} {{qualifier|obsolete}}.

Verb {{senseno|en|hoodwink}} (“to convince or hoodwink (someone)”) probably refers to a person being blinded or confused by a snowstorm.

The adjective comes from the phrase {{m|en|a snowball's chance in hell}}, also see {{w|Wikipedia:Snowball clause|snowball clause}} at Wikipedia.
