===Etymology 1===
From {{inh|en|enm|nere}}, {{m|enm|ner|}}, from {{inh|en|ang|nēar|t=nearer|pos=comparative of {{m|ang|nēah|t=nigh}}, the superlative would become {{m|en|next}}}}, influenced by {{der|en|non|nær|t=near}}, both originating from {{der|en|gem-pro|*nēhwiz|t=nearer}}, comparative of the adverb {{m|gem-pro|*nēhw|t=near}}, from the adjective {{m|gem-pro|*nēhwaz}}, ultimately from Pre-Proto-Germanic {{m|ine-pro||*h₂nḗḱwos}}, a lengthened-grade adjective derived from {{der|en|ine-pro|*h₂neḱ-|t=to reach}}.
{{col-top|2|cog}}
Cognate with {{cog|frr|nai}}, {{m|frr|noi}}, {{m|frr|näi|t=close, near}}, {{cog|stq|nai|t=close, near}}, {{cog|nl|na|t=close}}, {{m|nl|naar|t=to, towards}}, {{cog|nds-nl|nao|t=after}}, {{cog|de|nach|t=after}}, {{m|de|nahe|t=near}}, {{m|de|näher|t=nearer}}, {{cog|nds-de|nao}}, {{m|nds-de|nå|t=towards}}, {{cog|lb|no|t=after}}, {{cog|da,fo,is,nb,nn|nær|t=close, near}}, {{cog|sv|när}}, {{m|sv|nära|t=close, near}}, {{cog|got|𐌽𐌴𐍈|t=close, near}}. See also {{m|en|nigh}}.

''Near'' appears to be derived from (or at the very least influenced by) the North Germanic languages; as opposed to {{m|en|nigh}}, which continues the inherited West Germanic adjective. Both, however, are ultimately derived from the same Proto-Germanic root: {{m|gem-pro|*nēhw|t=near, close}}.

=====Usage notes=====
The sense of ''nearly'' or ''almost'' is dialect, colloquial, old-fashioned or poetic in certain uses, such as, in many cases, when ''near'' is used to directly modify a verb.

=====Usage notes=====
Joan Maling (1983) shows that ''near'' is best analysed as an adjective with which the use of ''[[to]]'' is optional, rather than a preposition. It has the comparative and the superlative, and it can be followed by ''[[enough]]''. The use of ''to'' however is usually British.

===Etymology 2===
{{etymid|en|kidney}}{{dercat|en|ine-pro|inh=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|nēre}}, {{m|enm|neere||kidney, abdomen; (''pl.'') seat of emotions}}, probably from unattested {{inh|en|ang|*nēora}}, {{m|ang|*nīora}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*neurō}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*neurô}}; but alternatively borrowed from {{der|en|gml|nêre}}.<ref>{{R:MED Online|nẹ̄r(e|n|MED29326}}</ref> {{etystub|en}} Cognate with {{cog|sco|nere}}, {{m|sco|neir}}, {{cog|de|Niere}}, {{cog|nl|nier}}, {{cog|no|nyre}}, {{cog|is|nýra}}, and more distantly {{cog|la|nefronēs|g=p|qq=Praeneste dialect}}, {{cog|grc|νεφρός}}, all meaning both {{m-g|kidney}} and {{m-g|testicle}}.

In most English dialects, this word survived only as a {{glossary|fossilized|fossil}} in the second syllable of {{m|en|kidney}} (earlier {{m|en|kidenēre|kide-nēre}}), wherein the original ''r'' was apparently replaced with ''y''.
