===Etymology 1===
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:ketel}}
From {{inh|en|enm|ketel}}, also {{m|enm|chetel}}, from {{inh|en|ang|ċietel||kettle, cauldron}} and in [[Middle English]] possibly influenced by {{der|en|non|ketill}} and both from {{der|en|gem-pro|*katilaz||kettle, bucket, vessel}}, of uncertain origin and formation. Usually regarded as a borrowing of {{der|en|la-lat|catīllus||small bowl}}, diminutive of {{der|en|la|catinus||deep bowl, vessel for cooking up or serving food}}, however, the word may be Germanic confused with the Latin: compare {{cog|ang|cete||cooking pot}}, {{cog|goh|chezzi||a kettle, dish, bowl}}, {{cog|is|kati}}, {{m|is|ketla||a small boat}}. Cognate with {{cog|fy|tsjettel||kettle}}, {{cog|nl|ketel||kettle}}, {{cog|de|Kessel||kettle}}, {{cog|sv|kittel||cauldron, kettle}}, {{cog|got|𐌺𐌰𐍄𐌹𐌻𐍃||kettle}}, {{cog|fi|kattila}}, {{cog|pl|kocioł||cauldron}}, {{cog|cs|kotel||boiler}}, {{cog|ru|котёл||boiler, cauldron}}.<ref> Oxford English Dictionary 2ed. "kettle" </ref>

{{sense|watch}} Cockney rhyming slang from 'kettle and hob' to '[[fob]]' ([[fob watch]]).

{{sense|[[kettle hole]]}} Compare typologically {{m+|ru|котлови́на}} (< {{m|ru|котёл}}).

=====Usage notes=====
In most varieties of English outside the United States (UK, Irish, Australian, New Zealand, Canadian), if not specified otherwise, ''kettle'' usually refers to a vessel or appliance used to boil water.
