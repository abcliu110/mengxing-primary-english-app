# {{lb|en|UK|Ireland|Commonwealth|less common|_|in North America}} A [[line#English:_queue, especially for waiting|line]] of people, vehicles or other objects, usually one to be [[deal#Verb|dealt]] with in [[sequence]] (i.e., the one at the front end is dealt with first, the one behind is dealt with next, and so on), and which newcomers [[join]] at the opposite [[end]] (the [[back]]). {{defdate|from 19th c.}}
#* {{RQ:Buchan Greenmantle|chapter=[[s:Greenmantle/Chapter 5|Chapter 5]]
|passage=I was absent-minded at the moment and was last in the '''queue'''.}}

#* {{RQ:Guardian|title=‘Geldof started flicking Vs at Farage’: the story of the Brexit campaign, told by those with a front-row seat|date=2026-06-23|url=https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/jun/23/brexit-campaign-oral-history-front-row-seat|passage=George Osborne said: “In terms of getting an international trade deal with the United States, we would have to get to the back of the '''queue'''.” And Obama said: “Would it be helpful if I said that?”{{...|, and there was a mood that broadly it would}}. # A [[waiting list]] or other means of [[organize|organizing]] people or objects into a first-come-first-served order.
