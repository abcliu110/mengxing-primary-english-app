==English==

===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*kap-|id=seize}}
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:cacchen|tree=+}}
From {{inh|en|enm|cacchen}}, from {{der|en|xno|cachier}}, variant of Old French {{m|fro|chacier}}, from {{der|en|la-lat|captio|captiāre}}, from {{der|en|la|captō|captāre}}, frequentative of {{m|la|capiō|capere}}. Akin to Modern {{cog|fr|chasser}} (from {{cog|fro|chacier}}) and {{cog|es|cazar}}, and thus a {{doublet|en|chase|nocap=1}}. Compare {{dbt|en|ketch|notext=1}}. Via PIE cognate with {{m|en|have}}. Displaced {{cog|enm|fangen}} {{gloss|"to catch"; > Modern English {{l|en|fang|pos=verb}}}}, from {{cog|ang|fōn||to seize, take}}; {{cog|enm|lacchen}} {{gloss|"to catch" and heavily displaced Modern English {{l|en|latch}}}}, from {{cog|ang|læċċan}}.

The verb became irregular, possibly under the influence of the semantically similar {{m|en|latch}} (from {{noncog|ang|læċċan}}), whose past tense was {{m|enm|lahte}}, {{m|enm|lauhte}}, {{m|en|laught}} ({{noncog|ang|læhte}}), until becoming regularised in Modern English.
