==English==

===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|difficult}} (ca. 1400), a {{back-form|en||nocap=1}} from {{m|enm|difficulte}} (whence modern {{m|en|difficulty}}), from {{uder|en|fro|difficulté}}, from {{uder|en|la|difficultas}}, from {{m|la|difficul}}, older form of {{m|la|difficilis||hard to do, difficult}}, from {{m|la|dis-}} + {{m|la|facilis||easy}}; see [[difficile]]. Replaced native {{cog|enm|earveþ||difficult, hard}}, from {{cog|ang|earfoþe||difficult, laborious, full of hardship}}, cognate to {{cog|de|Arbeit||work}}.

The verb is from the adjective, partly after {{der|en|frm|difficulter}} and its etymon {{der|en|la|difficultō}}. Compare {{m|en|difficilitate}}, {{m|en|difficultate}}, and {{cog|it|difficoltare}}.<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=v|id=7735089898}}</ref>

====Usage notes====
''Difficult'' implies that considerable mental effort or physical skill is required, or that obstacles are to be overcome which call for sagacity and skill in the doer; as, a ''difficult'' task. Thus, “hard” is not always synonymous with difficult. Examples include ''a ''difficult'' operation in surgery'' and ''a ''difficult'' passage by an author'' (that is, a passage which is hard to understand).
