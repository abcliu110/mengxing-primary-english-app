===Etymology===
18th century, from {{bor|de|fr|loyal}}, from {{der|de|la|legalis}}. {{doublet|de|legal}}.

===Pronunciation===

===Adjective===
{{de-adj|comp}}

# {{l|en|loyal}}
#: {{ant|de|illoyal}}

====Usage notes====
* {{m|de||Loyal}} usually means a formal, outward kind of loyalty, whereas {{m|de|treu}} tends to mean loyalty that is based on emotion or deep-rooted conviction. Therefore {{m|de||loyal}} is used especially with institutions, business partners, colleagues etc., while {{m|de||treu}} is used especially with lovers, friends, pets and suchlike. {{m|de||Ein loyaler Ehemann}} would be a husband who is reliable, supports his wife, does not speak ill of her, etc., whereas {{m|de||ein treuer Ehemann}} would put more emphasis on intimate affection and monogamy. Similarly, {{m|de||ein loyaler Anhänger}} is a follower who is reliable and performs their duty, while the expression {{m|de||ein treuer Anhänger}} may add an emotional level.
