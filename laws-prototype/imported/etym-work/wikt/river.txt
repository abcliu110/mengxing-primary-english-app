=====Usage notes=====
* As with the names of {{l|en|lake|lakes}} and {{l|en|mountain|mountains}}, the names of rivers are typically formed by adding the word before or after the unique term: the {{m|en|River Thames}} or the {{m|en|Yangtze River}}. Generally speaking, names formed using adjectives or attributives see ''river'' added to the end, as with the {{m|en|Yellow River}}. It is less common to add ''river'' before names than it is with lakes, but many of the rivers of Britain are written that way, as with the {{m|en|River Severn}}; indeed, British English tends to use "River X" in such cases while American, South African, Australian and New Zealand English use "X River". The former derives from the earlier but now uncommon form ''river of ~'': the 19th century ''River of Jordan'' is now usually simply the ''River Jordan''.
* It is common to preface the proper names of rivers with the article {{m|en|the}}.
* Concerning the reference of its coordinate terms, some people say:<ref>[http://worldrivers.net/2018/11/29/distinction-of-rivers-stream-brooks-creeks-and-other-terms/ Definitions of rivers, stream, brooks, creeks and other terms] (Goran Šafarek, ''WorldRivers.net,'' November 29, 2018)</ref> you can step over a {{m|en|brook}}, jump over a {{m|en|creek}}, wade across a {{m|en|stream}}, and swim across a {{m|en||river}}.
