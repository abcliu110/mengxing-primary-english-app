===Etymology 1===
From {{inh|en|enm|shark}} (used by {{w|Thomas Beckington}} in 1442 to refer to a kind of fish),<ref>{{R:MED Online|shark|n|MED39794}}</ref> of {{unc|en|nocap=1}} origin. Most likely from a semantic extension of the {{der|en|de|-}}-derived {{m|en||shark|scoundrel}}, see [[shark#Etymology 2|below]]. The fish was originally called a {{m|en|dogfish}} or {{m|en|haye}} in English and Middle English. Its name in Old English is unknown, although some uses of the word {{m|ang|hranfisċ|}} that do not appear to carry the sense of "{{l|en|whale}}" may have been referencing it.

{{col-top|2|alternative theories}}
Some older dictionaries derived the word from {{cog|la|carcharias|c(h)archarias}}, {{m|la|carcharus|c(h)acharus}} (from {{cog|grc|-}}), but admit that "the requisite [Old French] forms intermediate between E. ''shark'' and L. ''carcharus'' are not found, and it is not certain that the name [shark] was orig. applied to the fish; it may have been first used of a greedy man".<ref>{{R:Century}}</ref>

Other older authorities speculated that the word might derive from {{noncog|yua|xok||fish}} ({{IPAchar|/ʃok/}}), as [[w:John Hawkins (naval commander)|John Hawkins]] brought a specimen from the area where Mayan was spoken to England in the 1560s. However, the 1442 use rules out a New World origin for the word.
{{col-bottom}}

===Etymology 1===
{{unc|sq|Disputed}}. {{etydate|1904}}.

{{box-top|title=Hypotheses etymologies}}
* {{R|sq|Kristoforidhi:1904}}, and later independently {{R|sq|Jokl:1926}}, derive from {{der|sq|grc|σάρξ|σάρξ, σαρκός|t=flesh; flesh of a fruit}}, also later followed by {{R|sq|Sandfeld:1930}} and {{R|sq|Orel:1998|-}}.
* {{R|sq|Çabej:SEFSH|-}} connected it to {{m|sq|shakull}}, {{m|sq|rrëshiq}}, {{m|sq|rrëshek}}, {{m|sq|shirk}}, all meaning {{m-g|bag made of sheepskin or goatskin}}, as swell as {{m|sq|zharg|t=snake moult; tree bark}}, {{m|sq|zherk|t=thin animal skin used in lutes}}, and derived it from {{inh|sq|ine-pro|*(s)ker-|*sker-|t=to cut off}}, whence {{cog|sla-pro|*skorà|t=skin}}, {{cog|gmw-pro|*skirmi|t=hide, pelt}} and {{cog|la|scortum|t=hide, pelt}}, assuming a suffixation with {{m|sq||-k}}. From the same root he also derives {{m|sq|shqerr|t=to tear}}, {{m|sq|korr|t=to mow, reap}} and {{m|sq|çart|t=to break}}, and calls this last one the closest.
* {{R|sq|Topalli:2017|-}} calls the connections to {{m|sq|shakull}}, etc. dubious but does not reject the derivation from {{inh|sq|ine-pro|*(s)ker-|*sker-}}.
