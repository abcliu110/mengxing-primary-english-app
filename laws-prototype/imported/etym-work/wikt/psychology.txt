===Etymology===
From {{bor|en|fr|psychologie}}, from {{der|en|la-ren|psychologia}}, from {{der|en|grc|ψυχή|t=soul}} + {{m|grc|-λογία||study of}}, equivalent to {{confix|en|psycho-|-logy}}. The Latin term is believed by some to have been coined in a lost treatise by Croatian humanist {{w|Marko Marulić}} (1450–1524), but this is disputed by other scholars. It is {{etydate|1570s|nocap=1}}, at which time it was apparently already current, and may be a Hellenization of the established expression {{m|la|[[dē]] [[animā]]||on the soul}} in titles.<ref>{{cite-book|pageurl=https://books.google.com/books?id=4SS0fcbm3xMC&pg=PA25&lpg=PA25|title=The Sciences of the Soul: The Early Modern Origins of Psychology|last=Vidal|first=Fernando|year=2011|publisher=University of Chicago Press|pages=25–26}}</ref>
