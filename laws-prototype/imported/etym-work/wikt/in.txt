===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=preposition|:inh|enm:in<id:preposition>}}
{{PIE word|en|h₁en}}
Preposition and verb from {{inh|en|enm|in}}, from {{inh|en|ang|in}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*in}}.{{dercat|en|ine-pro|inh=1}}

Adverb, noun and adjective from {{inh|en|enm|in}}, from {{inh|en|ang|inn}} and {{m|ang|inne}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*innai}}.

Sense 1/2 "in"/"into" are from the original PIE prefix, with locative/accusative case respectively. Sense 3/4 "qualification"/"means" are from the PIE metaphor of all infinitives coming from locatives.

====Usage notes====
This variant of {{m|ga|i}} is used before vowel-initial words, before {{m|ga|bhur||your {{g|p}}}}, before {{m|ga|dhá||two}}, before titles of books, films, and the like, and before foreign words that resist mutation.

In older texts, the ''n'' is spelled together with a vowel-initial word (e.g. {{m|ga|i n-aice le}} instead of modern {{m|ga|in aice le||beside}} and {{m|ga|[[i]] [[nÉirinn]]}} or {{m|ga|[[i]] [[n-Éirinn]]}} instead of modern {{m|ga|in [[Éirinn]]||in Ireland}}. =====Usage notes=====
In the West Saxon dialect, this word was replaced by {{m|ang|on}} during the separate prehistory of Old English. However, it was still used as a prefix to form many words such as {{m|ang|ingang||entrance}}, {{m|ang|inġewinn||civil war}}, {{m|ang|inġeþanc||inner thoughts}}, {{m|ang|inlendisċ||native}}, {{m|ang|intinga||cause}}, and {{m|ang|inweorc||indoor work}}, and it was still implied by derived adverbs such as {{m|ang|inne||inside}}, {{m|ang|innan||from the inside}}, and {{m|ang|inn}} (“in,” adverb).
