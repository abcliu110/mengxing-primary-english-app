===Etymology 1===
From {{der|en|fro|succeder}}, from {{uder|en|la|succedo|succedere|to go under, go from under, come under, approach, follow, take the place of, receive by succession, prosper, be successful}}.

Partly {{displaced|en|speed|nocap=1}}, of which the original sense “to succeed” only survives in a few {{lg|fossilized}} expressions such as {{m|en|Godspeed}}.

====Verb====
{{en-verb}}

# {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[follow]] something in [[sequence]] or [[time]].
#: {{ux|en|Autumn '''succeeds''' summer.}}
#* {{RQ:Browne Pseudodoxia Epidemica|edition=2nd|passage=Destructive effects {{...}} '''succeeded''' the curse.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[replace]] or [[supplant]] someone in order [[vis-à-vis]] an office, position,  or title.
#: {{synonyms|en|[[take]] the [[place]] of}}
#: {{ux|en|The king's eldest son '''succeeds''' his father on the throne.}}
#: {{ux|en|After a contentious election, Jones '''succeeded''' Smith as president of the republic.}}
# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To come after or follow; to be [[subsequent]] or [[consequent]]; {{q|often with ''to''}}.
#* {{RQ:Maugham Moon and Sixpence|[[s:The Moon and Sixpence/Chapter XLIX|chapter 49]]||Her arms were like legs of mutton, her breasts like giant cabbages; her face, broad and fleshy, gave you an impression of almost indecent nakedness, and [[vast]] chin '''succeeded''' to vast chin.}}

#: {{syn|en|supersede|q1=formal}}
