===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=hog|:inh|enm:pigge}}
From {{inh|en|enm|pigge|t=pig, piglet}} (originally a term for a young pig, with adult pigs being {{m|enm|swyn|t=swine}}), from {{inh|en|ang|*picga}}, {{m|ang|*pycga}} (attested in {{m|ang|picgbrēad|t=mast, pig-fodder}}), perhaps a diminutive of {{der|en|gmw-pro|*puk}}, {{m|gmw-pro|*pūk|t=pig}}, which also gave rise to {{cog|gml|pûke}}, {{m|gml|puyke|t=pig, piglet}}. Pokorny suggests this root might be somehow related to {{m|ine-pro|*bū-}}, {{m|ine-pro|*bew-||to blow; swell}}, which could account for the alternation between "pig" and "big".

Compare {{cog|dum|pogge}}, {{m|dum|puggen}}, {{m|dum|pigge}}, {{m|dum|pegsken}} (> dialectal {{cog|nl|pogge|t=piglet}}), {{cog|gml|pugge}} (> Westphalian {{cog|nds-de|Pogge}}, {{m|nds-de|Pugge}}, {{m|nds-de|Püggsken|t=pig, piglet}}). 

A connection to early modern {{cog|nl|bigge}} (modern {{m+|nl|big|t=piglet}}), {{cog|fy|bigge|t=piglet}}, {{cog|nds-de|Bigge}}, {{m|nds-de|Bigg|t=piglet}}, and {{cog|stq|Bikkie|t=piggy}} is sometimes proposed, "but the phonology is difficult".<ref name="NED">''A new English dictionary on historical principles''</ref> Some sources say the words are "almost certainly not" related,<ref>{{R:Dictionary.com}}</ref> others consider a relation "probable, but not certain".<ref>M. Philippa: "Big". In: Etymologisch Woordenboek van het Nederlands, 2003-09.</ref>

The slang sense of "police officer" is attested since at least 1785.<ref>'''2003''', Victoria Fromkin, Robert Rodman, Nina M. Hyams, ''An Introduction to Language'', [https://books.google.com.au/books?id=7PftAAAAMAAJ&q=%22pig%22|%22pigs%22+police+-intitle:%22%22+-inauthor:%22%22&dq=%22pig%22|%22pigs%22+police+-intitle:%22%22+-inauthor:%22%22&hl=en&sa=X&ei=6jLXT4-XMsHYigfxvJS4Aw&redir_esc=y page 474] — Similarly, the use of the word '''''pig''''' for “policeman” goes back at least as far as 1785, when a writer of the time called a Bow Street police officer a “China Street '''pig'''.”</ref>
