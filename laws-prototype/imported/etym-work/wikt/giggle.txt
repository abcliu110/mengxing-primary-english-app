==English==

===Etymology===
Early 16th century, probably of {{onomatopoeic|en|title=imitative}} origin.<ref>{{R:AHD}}</ref>

Or, perhaps a frequentative based on dialectal English {{m|en|gig|t=to creak}}, from {{der|en|enm|gigen|t=to make a creaking sound}} {{suf|en||le}}; or perhaps of {{der|en|nl|-}} or {{der|en|nds|-}} origin: compare {{cog|stq|güüchelje|t=to giggle}}, {{cog|fy|giechelje|t=to giggle}}, {{cog|nl|giechelen|t=to giggle}}, {{cog|nds-de|giecheln|t=to giggle}}, dialectal {{cog|de|giggln}}, {{m|de|gigglen|t=to giggle}}, {{cog|de|gickeln|t=to giggle}}. All of these words are likely onomatopoeic as well. Also compare {{cog|gsw|Guege|t=fiddle}}.
