===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:scho<id:shoe>|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|scho}}, {{m|enm|sho}}, from {{inh|en|ang|sċōh||shoe}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*skōh}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*skōhaz||shoe}}, of unclear etymology; possibly a derivation from {{m|gem-pro|*skehaną||to move quickly}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*skek-||to move quickly, jump}}. Eclipsed non-native {{noncog|enm|sabatine}}, {{m|enm|sabatoun|t=shoe}} from {{noncog|la-med|sabatēnum}}, {{m|la|sabatum|t=shoe, slipper}} (compare {{noncog|pro|sabatō}}, {{noncog|es|zapato|t=shoe}}, {{noncog|fr|sabot|t=wooden shoe, clog}}, {{noncog|it|ciabatta}}). The archaic plural ''[[shoon]]'' is from {{inh|en|enm|shon}}, from {{der|en|ang|scōn}}, {{m|ang|scōum|t=shoes|pos=dative plural}} and {{m|ang|scōna|t=shoes'|pos=genitive plural}}; it is cognate with {{cog|sco|shuin|t=shoes}}. See also {{cog|sco|shae}}, {{cog|fy|skoech}}, {{cog|nds|Schoh}}, {{cog|nl|schoen}}, {{cog|de|Schuh}}, {{cog|bar|Schuach}}, {{cog|da,nb,nn,sv|sko}}, {{cog|txb|skak|skāk|balcony}}.

===Noun===
{{en-noun|s|shoon<l:archaic,dialectal>|shoen<l:obsolete>}}

# {{senseid|en|Q22676}}{{lb|en|footwear}} A protective covering for the [[foot]], with a bottom part composed of thick [[leather]] or [[plastic]] sole and often a thicker heel, and a softer upper part made of leather or [[synthetic]] material. Shoes generally do not extend above the [[ankle]], as opposed to [[boot|boots]], which do.
#: {{ux|en|Get your '''shoes''' on now, or you'll be late for school.}}
# A piece of metal designed to be attached to a horse's foot as a means of protection; a [[horseshoe]].
#: {{ux|en|Throw the '''shoe''' from behind the line, and try to get it to land circling (a ringer) or touching the far stake.}}
# {{lb|en|card games}} A device for holding multiple decks of playing cards, allowing more games to be played by reducing the time between [[shuffle]]s.
# Something resembling a shoe in form, position, or function, such as a ''[[brake shoe]]''.
#: {{ux|en|Remember to turn the rotors when replacing the brake '''shoes''', or they will wear out unevenly.}}
## A band of iron or steel, or a ship of wood, fastened to the bottom of the runner of a sleigh, or any vehicle which slides on the snow.
## A drag, or sliding piece of wood or iron, placed under the wheel of a loaded vehicle, to retard its motion in going down a hill.
## The part of a brake for a wheeled vehicle which presses upon the wheel to retard its motion.
## {{lb|en|architecture}} A trough-shaped or spout-shaped member, put at the bottom of the water leader coming from the eaves gutter, so as to throw the water off from the building.
## A trough or spout for conveying grain from the hopper to the eye of the millstone.
## An inclined trough in an ore-crushing mill.
## An iron socket or plate to take the thrust of a strut or rafter.
## An iron socket to protect the point of a wooden pile.
## {{lb|en|engineering}} A plate, or notched piece, interposed between a moving part and the stationary part on which it bears, to take the wear and afford means of adjustment; called also [[slipper]] and [[gib]].
## Part of a current collector on electric trains which provides contact either with a live rail or an overhead wire (fitted to a pantograph in the latter case).
## {{lb|en|historical}} An ingot of gold or silver shaped somewhat like a traditional Chinese shoe, formerly used in trade in the Far East.
##: {{syn|en|boat money|q1=uncountable|shoe money|q2=uncountable}}
##* {{quote-journal|en|year=1806|author=Lawrence Dundas Campbell; E. Samuel|journal=The Asiatic Annual Register|page=56
|passage=The finest gold among them is 100 touch, called Sycee, i. e. pure gold without alloy: so that if a '''shoe''' of gold touch 93, then it hath 93 parts of fine gold and 7 parts alloy.}}
# The outer cover or [[tread]] of a [[pneumatic]] [[tire]], especially for an [[automobile]].
# {{lb|en|by extension|slang}} A pneumatic tire, especially for an automobile.
# {{lb|en|slang}} A [[fake]] [[passport]].
