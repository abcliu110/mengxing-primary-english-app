===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:bor|id=Mangifera|pt:manga<id:mango>|tree=+}}
{{bor+|en|pt|manga}}, from {{der|en|ml|മാങ്ങ}} / {{der|en|ta|மாங்காய்}}, possibly via {{der|en|ms|mangga}}, ultimately from {{der|en|dra-sou-pro|*mām|*mām-kāy|t=unripe mango}}, a compound of {{m|dra-sou-pro|*mām|t=mango tree}} + {{m|dra-pro|*kāy|t=unripe fruit}}.<ref>{{R:dra:DL|pages=526, 530}}</ref> First used for the fruit as early as the 1580s and the tree by the 1670s.<ref name="OnlineED">{{R:Online Etymology Dictionary|entry=mango}}</ref><ref name="OxfordED">{{R:OED Online|entry=mango|pos=''n.''{{sup|1}}|noformat=1|id=113430|date=June 2021}}</ref> The etymology of the ''-o'' ending is not certain.<ref name="OxfordED"/>

==Latin==

===Etymology===
{{unc|la}}; but perhaps an agent noun related to {{cog|grc|μαγγανεύω|t=enchant, use charms}} and secondarily {{m-g|trick out, dress artificially}},<ref>Bodel, John. 2005. Caveat emptor: Towards a study of Roman slave-traders. ''Journal of Roman Archaeology'' 18. 192.</ref><ref>{{cite-journal|year=1896|author={{w|Augustus Samuel Wilkins|Wilkins, A.S.}}|chapter=A proposito dell’origine della parola mango vedi|journal=Q. Horati Flacci Opera|location=London; New York|publisher=MacMillan|page=136}}</ref> from the noun {{m|grc|μάγγανον|t=philtre, charm, means for bewitching others}}. Buck suggests that Latin {{m|la||mangō}} is a loanword based (ultimately or otherwise) on the Greek noun.<ref>{{cite-book|last=Buck |first=Carl Darling |year=1949, 1988 reprint |title=A Dictionary of Selected Synonyms in the Principal Indo-European Languages |url=https://archive.org/details/adictionaryofselectedsynonymsinprincipalindoeuropeanlanguagescarldarlingbuck_664_G/page/820/mode/2up |location=Chicago |publisher=University of Chicago |isbn=0-226-07937-6 |page=820 |text=ON {{m|non|manga}}, OE {{m|ang|mangian}}, ME {{m|enm|mange}}, OS {{m|osx|mangōn}}, fr. Lat. {{m|la|mangō}} ‘dealer, monger’ (who adorns his wares to give them an appearance of greater value), beside {{m|la||mangōnium}} ‘displaying of wares’, prob. loanwords based on Grk. [[μάγγανον#Ancient Greek|μάγγανον]] ‘means of charming or bewitching’. Walde-P. 2.233. Ernout-M. 588. Walde-H. 2.28 f. NED s.v. ''mong'', vb.<sup>1</sup>.}}</ref>

Alternatively, derived from {{m|la|manus|t=hand}} via an unattested verb such as {{m|la||*manicō}} or {{m|la||*manigō|t=handle, manage; trade, deal?}} (both requiring an unusual syncope of the verb suffix, the former also requiring an unusual voicing of /k/) {{suf|la||-ō|pos2=agent noun suffix|id2=noun}}. This would make it related to {{m|la|manceps|t=purchaser; contractor}} and {{m|la|mancipium|t=property, slave}}, whence perhaps the sense of {{m-g|slave-trader}}. The semantic trajectory would be similar to that of {{noncog|de|handeln|t=to handle; to trade, deal}}, ultimately from {{noncog|gem-pro|*handuz|t=hand}}.
