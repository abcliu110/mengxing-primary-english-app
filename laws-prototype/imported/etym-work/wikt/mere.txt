===Etymology 1===
{{ety|en|id=only|:inh|enm:mere<ety:bor<xno:meer<ety:inh<fro:mier>>>>}}
From {{inh|en|enm|mere}}, {{m|enm|mer}}, from {{der|en|xno|meer}}, from {{der|en|fro|mier}}, from {{der|en|la|merus|t=pure, unmixed, undiluted}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*mer-|t=to sparkle, gleam}}.

Cognate with {{cog|ang|āmerian}}, {{m|ang|āmyrian|t=to purify, examine, revise}}. The Middle English word was perhaps influenced by or conflated with sound-alike {{der|en|enm|mere|id=famous|t=glorious, noble, splendid, fine, pure}}, from {{der|en|ang|mǣre|id=famous|t=famous, great, excellent, sublime, splendid, pure, sterling}}, from {{der|en|gmw-pro|*mārī|id=famous}}, from {{der|en|gem-pro|*mērijaz}}.

As such, if in a cell there are 2 forms, the first one is the way this verb would normally be conjugated, and the 2nd form is what is more common in usage. This does cause it, however, to become irregular.
* The gerund form, {{m|ro||merând}}, is rarely, if ever, said, and as such isn’t listed.
