===Etymology 1===
{{ety|en|id=collection of items|:inh|enm:kyt<ety:der<dum:kitte>>}}

From {{inh|en|enm|kyt,kytt,kytte}}, from {{der|en|dum|kitte||a wooden vessel made of hooped staves}}. Related to {{cog|nl|kit|t=tankard}} {{attention|nl|rather than to an entry on Dutch "tankard" this link refers to an entry on Dutch "kit"}} (see below). The further etymology is unknown. Perhaps from {{der|en|gem-pro|*kitjō-,*kut-}}, which would be related to the root of {{cog|nl|kot|t=ramshackle house}}, itself of non-Indo-European origin.<ref>{{R:Kluge 1891|Kotze}}</ref>

The transfer of meaning to the contents of a soldier's knapsack dates to the late 18th&nbsp;century, extended use of any collection of necessaries used for travelling dates to the first half of the 19th century.
The further widening of the sense to a collection of parts sold for the buyer to assemble emerges in American English in the mid 20th century.

===Etymology 2===
[[File:Red fox kit 3 (Vulpes vulpes).jpg|thumb|alt=A young red fox emerging from a burrow, looking at the camera|A red fox '''kit''' ({{senseno|en|young fox}})]]

A short form of {{m|en|kitten}} or {{m|en|kitling}}. From the 16th century, spelled {{m|en|kytte}}, {{m|en|kitt}}. From the 19th century also extended to other young animals (beaver, fox, mink, muskrat, etc.), and to a species of small fox ([[kit fox]]). Later usage (for other animals) perhaps influenced by {{m|en|chit}}.
