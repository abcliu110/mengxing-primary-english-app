===Etymology 1===
{{bor+|nb|en|hint}}.<ref name="hint-NAOB">{{R:nb:NAOB}}</ref> The English noun originally meant “occasion, opportunity” and is probably an alteration of obsolete {{m|en|hent||a grasp; act of seizing}}, from the verb {{der|nb|enm|henten||to seize; grasp|nocat=1}} (also {{m|enm|hinten}}), from {{der|nb|ang|hentan||to seize; pursue|nocat=1}}, from {{der|nb|gem-pro|*hantijaną||to pursue; follow|nocat=1}}.<ref>“[https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/hint_1 hint]”, ''Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary''.</ref><ref>“[https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/hint hint]”, ''Merriam-Webster.com Dictionary''.</ref><ref>Vladimir Orel (2003), ''A Handbook of Germanic Etymology'', page 161, s.v. “*xantjanan”.</ref> This explanation connects the notion of seizing something with taking advantage of an opportunity.

The deeper formation is uncertain. Kroonen connects the Germanic verb with {{m|gem-pro|*huntōną||to hunt; chase}} and {{m|gem-pro|*hinþaną||to reach for; catch}}, ultimately associated with a proposed {{der|nb|ine-pro|*ḱent-||to reach; sting|nocat=1}} root; compare {{cog|en|hunt}}.<ref>Guus Kroonen (2013), ''[https://archive.org/details/etymological-dictionary-of-proto-germanic Etymological Dictionary of Proto-Germanic]'', pages 227 and 257, s.vv. “*hinþan-” and “*huntōn-”.</ref>

Compare the parallel borrowings {{cog|nn|hint}}, {{cog|da|hint}}, {{cog|sv|hint}}, {{cog|ja|ヒント|tr=hinto}} and {{cog|ko|힌트|tr=hinteu|t=hint; clue}}.
