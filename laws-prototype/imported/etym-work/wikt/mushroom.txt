===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:muscheron|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|muscheron}}, {{m|enm|musseron|}}, from {{der|en|xno|musherum}}, {{m|fro|moscheron}}, from {{der|en|fro|moisseron}}, of {{unk|en|title=obscure}} origin: probably derived from {{der|en|fro|mosse}}, {{m|fro|moise}} {{gl|"moss"; whence also {{m+|fr|mousse}}}}, as the use first applied to a type of fungus which grows in moss, from {{der|en|frk|*mosu|t=moss}} or {{der|en|odt|*mosa|t=moss}}, akin to {{cog|goh|mosa|t=moor, swamp}}, {{cog|goh|mos|t=moss, bog}}, {{cog|goh|mios|t=moss, mire}}, {{cog|ang|mēos|t=moss}}, {{cog|ang|mōs|t=bog, marsh}}, {{cog|non|mosi|t=moss}}, {{cog|non|myrr|t=bog, mire}}, from {{der|en|gem-pro|*musą}}, {{m|gem-pro|*musô}}, {{m|gem-pro|*miuziz|t=mosses, bog}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*mews-|t=mosses, mold, mildew}}. Displaced native {{ncog|ang|swamm}}. More at [[mire]]. Alternatively, the Old French may be of pre-Roman origin. See {{cog|grc|μύκης||mushroom}}. {{doublet|en|moss|mousse}}.
