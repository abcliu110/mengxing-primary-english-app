===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|id=mingling|:inh|enm:among<id:mingling>|tree=1}}
{{root|en|ine-pro|*h₂en-|*menk-}}
The {{lg|preposition}} is derived from {{inh|en|enm|among}}, {{m|enm|amang}}, {{m|enm|amange}}, {{m|enm|amonge|t=in the presence of, amid, among; in, within; between; during}},<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=among(es|pos=prep|id=MED1466}}</ref> from {{inh|en|ang|amang}},<ref>{{R:Anglo-Saxon Dictionary|entry=a-mang|pos=prep. c. dat|page=36|column=1}}</ref>  {{m|ang|onġemang|pos=prep}},<ref name="ASD">{{R:Anglo-Saxon Dictionary|entry=on-gemang|pos=prep. with dat.'' and ''adv|page=752|column=1}}</ref> from {{m|ang|on|t=on, among, in}}<ref>{{R:Anglo-Saxon Dictionary|entry=on|pos=prep.'', ''adv|page=744|column=1}}</ref> (probably ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*h₂en-|t=on, onto}}) + {{m|ang|ġemang|t=crowd; mixture|pos=n}}<ref>{{R:Anglo-Saxon Dictionary|part=Supp|entry=ge-mang|pages=367–368|pageref=367}}</ref><ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=prep.'' and ''adv|id=6191608973|date=December 2025|nodot=1}}; {{R:Lexico|pos=prep}}</ref> (from {{m|ang|ġe-|pos={{lg|prefix}} forming {{lg|noun}}s denoting association or similarity}} (ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*ḱóm|t=beside, by; near; with}}) + {{m|ang||mang-}} (from {{m|ang|mængan}}, {{m|ang|mengan|t=to mingle, mix}};<ref>{{R:Anglo-Saxon Dictionary|entry=mengan|page=678|column=1}}</ref> ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*menk-|t=to knead; to press}})).<ref>Compare {{R:OED Online|entry=† ymong|pos=n|id=4288554025|date=July 2023}}</ref> {{surf|en|a-|pos1=prefix meaning ‘at; in; on’|mong|t2=crowd, throng; group}}.

The {{lg|adverb}} is derived from {{inh|en|enm|among}}, {{m|enm|amang|t=accompanied by, along with, by the side of, in association with, together; all the while, continually; also, besides; at the same time; from time to time, occasionally; meanwhile; within}},<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=among(es|pos=adv|id=MED1465}}</ref> from {{inh|en|ang|onġemang|pos=adv}}:<ref name="ASD"/> see further above.

The raising of {{IPAchar|/ɔ/}} (which would have yielded {{IPAchar|/ɒ/}}) to {{IPAchar|/u/}} (hence modern {{IPAchar|/ʌ/}}) within Middle English is regular between {{IPAchar|/m/}} and {{IPAchar|/ŋ/}}; compare {{m|en|monger}}, {{m|en|mongrel}}.
