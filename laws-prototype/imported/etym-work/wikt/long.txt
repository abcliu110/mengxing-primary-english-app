The word shows the regular historical change of ''a'' to ''o'' before certain consonant clusters such as ''ng'' (compare with other examples in Middle and Modern English such as {{m|en|bond}}, {{m|en|song}}, {{m|en|throng}}, and {{m|en|wrong}}). The ''o''-form may have also been reinforced by {{der|en|fro|long}}, from {{der|en|la|longus}}, from the same Indo-European word. {{doublet|en|lungo}} and {{m|en|lunge}}.

=====Usage notes=====
* ''[[wide|Wide]]'' may be used instead of ''long'' when referring to a left-to-right horizontal dimension, or to a horizontal dimension shorter than that described as ''long'' (for example, a river that is ''wide'' and ''long''). ''[[deep|Deep]]'' may be used for a horizontal front-to-back dimension.
* ''[[tall|Tall]]'' or ''[[high]]'' are usually used instead of ''long'' when referring to positive [[vertical]] dimension (upwards), and ''[[deep]]'' when referring to negative vertical dimension (downwards).

=====Usage notes=====
The use of ''long'' with the sense ''a long time'' (found as a complement of verbs like {{m|en|take}}, {{m|en|have}} ({{m|en|got}}), {{m|en|need}}, {{m|en|spend}}, {{m|en|give}}, {{m|en|be}} or of the prepositions {{m|en|for}} or {{m|en|before}}) is normally restricted to questions and negative statements.<ref>{{cite-book|last1=Pullum|first1=Geoffrey K.|last2=Huddleston|first2=Rodney|year=2002|chapter=Adjectives and adverbs|page=569|title=The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language}}</ref> In other situations, the phrase ''a long time'' is used instead:
: ''Does it take '''long'''?''
:: ''&mdash; No, it doesn't take '''long'''.''
:: {{q|nonstandard}} ''&mdash; Yes, it takes '''long'''.''
:: ''&mdash; Yes, it takes '''a long time'''.''
:: ''&mdash; Yes, it takes far too '''long'''.''

This restriction does not apply when "long" is modified by an adverb such as ''too'' or ''enough''. It also does not apply when "long" is used as a premodifier of a verb, participle, adjective, preposition or adverb with the sense "for a long time".
