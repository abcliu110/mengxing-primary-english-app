===Etymology 1===
{{ety|en|id=noun|:inh|enm:chek|tree=1}}

From {{inh|en|enm|chek,chekke}}, borrowed from {{der|en|fro|eschec,eschek,eschac}}, from {{der|en|la-med|scaccus}}, borrowed from {{der|en|ar|شَاه||king or check at chess, shah}}, borrowed from {{der|en|fa-cls|شَاه||king}}, from {{der|en|pal|𐭬𐭫𐭪𐭠|ts=šāh}}, from {{der|en|peo|𐏋|ts=xšāyaθiya||king}}, from {{der|en|iir-pro|*kšáyati||he rules, he has power over}}, ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*tek-||to gain power over, gain control over}}.

Compare {{cog|stq|Schak}}, {{m|stq|Schach}}, {{cog|nl|schaak}}, {{cog|de|Schach}}, {{cog|da|skak}}, {{cog|sv|schack}}, {{cog|is|skák}}, {{cog|fr|échec}}, {{cog|it|scacco}}. {{dbt|en|chess|shah<t:king of Persia or Iran>}}, from the same source, as well as {{m|en|thig}}, which derives from the Germanic cognate.

All of the English senses developed from the chess sense. The sense "financial instrument" derives from the sense of "means of verification" (i.e. against forgery), likely influenced by the already related word {{m|en|exchequer}}.
