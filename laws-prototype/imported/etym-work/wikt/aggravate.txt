==English==

===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*gʷreh₂-}}
The {{glossary|adjective}} is {{etydate|1471|nocap=1}} in {{w|Middle English}}, the {{glossary|verb}} in 1530; from {{lbor|en|la|aggravātus|notext=1}}, {{glossary|perfect}} {{glossary|passive}} {{glossary|participle}} of {{m|la|aggravō||to add to the weight of, make worse, oppress, annoy}} (see {{af|en|-ate|id1=verb|pos1=verb-forming suffix}} and {{af|en|-ate|id1=adjective|pos1=adjective-forming suffix}}), from {{m|la|ad-||to}} + {{m|la|gravō||to make heavy}}, from {{m|la|gravis||heavy}} + {{m|la|-ō|id=denominative|pos=verb-forming suffix}}. See {{m|en|grave}} and compare {{m|en|aggrieve}} and {{m|en|aggrege}}. Participial usage of the adjective up until {{w|Early Modern English}}. {{surf|en|ag-|grave|t2=heavy|-ate|t3=verb suffix}}.

====Usage notes====
* Although the meaning "to exasperate, to annoy" has been in continuous usage since the 16th century, a large number of usage [[maven]]s have contested it since the 1870s. Opinions have swayed from this proscription since 1965, but it still garners disapproval in ''Garner's Modern American Usage'' (2009), at least for formal writing.
