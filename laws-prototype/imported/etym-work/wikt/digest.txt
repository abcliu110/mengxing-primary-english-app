===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*h₂eǵ-|id=drive}}
From {{inh|en|enm|digesten|}}, from {{der|en|la|dīgestus|}}, past participle of {{m|la|dīgerō||carry apart}}, from {{m|la|dī-}} (for {{m|la|dis-||apart}}) + {{m|la|gerō||to carry}}, influenced by {{cog|frm|digestion|}}. Partly displaced native {{ncog|ang|meltan}} (intransitive) and {{m|ang|mieltan}} (transitive), both “to melt, to digest,” whence {{w|Modern English}} {{m|en|melt}}.

===Etymology 2===

From {{der|en|la|dīgesta}}, neuter plural of {{m|la|dīgestus}}, past participle of {{m|la|dīgerō||separate}}.

====Pronunciation====

====Noun====
{{en-noun}}

# That which is digested; especially, that which is worked over, classified, and arranged under proper heads or titles.
#* {{quote-journal|en|date=21 Dec 86|volume=14|number=23|journal=Gay Community News|page=8|author=Donald Stone|title=Gay Male Fiction Comes of Age in Anthology|text=By also relating the tales included in the anthology to various facts of that development, he leaves no doubt that this volume constitutes a veritable '''digest''' of the remarkable strides made by the genre in recent years.}}
# A compilation of statutes or decisions analytically arranged; a summary of laws.
#: {{ux|en|Comyn's '''Digest'''}}
#: {{ux|en|the United States '''Digest'''}}
# Any collection of articles, as an Internet mailing list including a week's postings, or a magazine arranging a collection of writings.
#: {{ux|en|Reader's '''Digest''' is published monthly.}}
#: {{ux|en|The weekly email '''digest''' contains all the messages exchanged during the past week.}}
# {{lb|en|cryptography}} The result of applying a [[hash function]] to a [[message]].

=====Usage notes=====
* {{sense|compilation of statutes or decisions analytically arranged}} The term is applied in a general sense to the [[w:Pandects|Pandects]] of Justinian, but is also specially given by authors to compilations of laws on particular topics.
