===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:north|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|north}}, from {{inh|en|ang|norþ}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*norþr}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*nurþrą}}, ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*nér|t=below (the surface)}}. The meaning developed either from "region where the sun is below (the earth)" or from "left side of someone who turns to the east when praying".
{{col-top|2|cog}}
Cognate with various Germanic counterparts such as {{cog|yol|noardth}}, {{m|yol|nordh|t=north}}, {{cog|frr|noor}}, {{m|frr|nord}}, {{m|frr|nuurd}}, {{m|frr|Nuurđ|t=north}}, {{cog|stq|Noude}}, {{m|stq|Nudde|t=north}}, {{cog|fy|noard|t=north}}, {{cog|nl|noord|t=north}}, {{cog|de|Nord|t=north}}, {{cog|da,nb,nn|nord|t=north}}, {{cog|fo,is|norður|t=north}}, {{cog|sv|nord}}, {{m|sv|norr|t=north}}; also with {{cog|grc|νέρτερος}}, {{m|grc|ἐνέρτερος|t=below}}, {{cog|ru|нора|t=hole}}, {{cog|lt|nėrõvė|t=mermaid, nymph}}, {{cog|osc|𐌍𐌄𐌓𐌕𐌓𐌀𐌊|t=left}}, {{cog|xum|nertru|t=left}}, {{cog|sa|नरक|t=hell}}, {{cog|txb|ñor|t=below}}.
