===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:schoppe|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|shoppe}}, {{m|enm|schoppe}}, from {{inh|en|ang|sċoppa|t=shed; booth; stall; shop}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*skupp-}}, {{m|gem-pro|*skup-|t=barn, shed}}, from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*skub-}}, {{m|ine-pro|*skup-|t=to bend, bow, curve, vault}}. Cognate with {{cog|nl|schop|t=shed}}, {{cog|de|Schuppen|t=shed}}, {{cog|de|Schober|t=barn}}, {{cog|fr|échoppe|t=booth, shop}} (< Germanic). The verb is denominal. The noun senses “act of shopping”, “purchased items” are backformed from the verb.

===Usage notes===
In [[North America]] establishments that sell goods are mainly referred to as [[store]]s with "shop" only used for smaller establishments.

===Verb===
{{en-verb}}

# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To visit stores or shops to browse or explore merchandise, especially with the intention of buying such merchandise.
#: {{ux|en|Why don’t you come '''shop'''ping for gifts with me, early before the Christmas rush?}}
#: {{ux|en|He’s (gone) '''shopping''' for clothes.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To browse or purchase products from (a [[catalog]], an [[internet]] [[website]], etc.), mostly from home. 
#: {{ux|en|'''Shop''' our new arrivals.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1988|author=Sylvia Harney|title=Married beyond recognition: a humorous look at marriage|page=90
|passage=You fantasized about having unhurried afternoons before the baby arrived to leisurely '''shop''' your favorite boutiques. Then the first crash hits — you no longer have the money to shop your favorite boutiques.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive|slang|chiefly|UK}} To report the criminal activities or whereabouts of someone to an authority.
#: {{ux|en|He '''shopped''' his mates in to the police.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive|slang|chiefly|UK}} To [[imprison]].
# {{lb|en|transitive|internet slang}} To [[photoshop]]; to digitally edit a picture or photograph.
# {{lb|en|transitive|slang|archaic}} To [[dismiss]] from [[employment]].
#: {{syn|en|fire|sack}}
# {{lb|en|transitive|colloquial}} To investigate or evaluate as a [[mystery shopper]].
#* quoted in '''1970''', United States. Bureau of Employment Security, ''Benefit Series Service: Unemployment Insurance'' (page 221-7)
#*: A grocery clerk who, after he had been "'''shopped'''" on occasions, was discharged because he had admitted that on one occasion he had not rung up a sale immediately after the transaction, as required by the company rule, but had recorded it later when he had remembered it, ''held'' discharged, but not for misconduct connected with his work {{...}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=2001|author=Stuart Tannock|title=Youth at Work: The Unionized Fast-food and Grocery Workplace|page=50
|passage=Because mystery shoppers' reports are presented to employees some time (often several days) after they have "been '''shopped'''," employees may not be able to recall who the mystery shopper was, even after the fact — and, consequently, they may be unable to judge the accuracy and fairness of these reports.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[pitch#Verb|pitch]] (a book, script, etc.) to a publisher or studio.
