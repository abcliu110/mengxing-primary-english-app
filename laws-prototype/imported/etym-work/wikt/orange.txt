===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|id=fruit|:inh|enm:orenge<id:fruit>|tree=1}}
{{inh+|en|enm|orenge}}, {{m|enm|orange}}, from {{der|en|fro|pome orenge|t=fruit orange}}, influenced by the place name {{m|en|Orange}} (which is from Gaulish and unrelated to the word for the fruit and colour) and by {{der|en|pro|auranja}} and calqued from {{der|en|roa-oit|melarancio}}, {{m|it|melarancia}}, compound of {{m|it|mela|t=apple}} and {{m|it|[[un]]'[[arancia]]|t=an orange}}, from {{der|en|ar|نَارَنْج}}, from Early {{der|en|fa-cls|نَارَنْگْ}}, from {{der|en|sa|नारङ्ग|t=orange tree}},<ref name="OnlineED">{{R:Online Etymology Dictionary|entry=orange}}</ref> ultimately from {{der|en|dra}}. Compare {{cog|ta|நாரங்காய்}}, compound of {{m|ta|நாரம்|t=water}} and {{m|ta|காய்|t=fruit}}; also {{cog|te|నారంగము, నారింజ}}, {{cog|ml|നാരങ്ങ}}, {{cog|kn|ನಾರಂಗಿ}}). {{doublet|en|aranzada}}.

Originally borrowed as the surname (derived from the place name) in the 13th century, before the sense of the fruit was imported in the late 14th century and the colour in 1510.<ref name="OnlineED"/> In the colour sense, largely displaced {{m|ang|ġeolurēad}}, whence {{m|en|yellow-red}}.

====Usage notes====
* It is commonly stated that ''orange'' has no rhymes. While there are no commonly used English dictionary words that rhyme exactly with ''orange'' ({{m|en|door hinge}} comes close in US pronunciation), see [[Rhymes:English/ɒɹɪndʒ]] for some possibilities. See also [[w:Orange_(word)#Rhyme|the Wikipedia article about rhymes for the word ''orange'']].
* In most dialects, ''orange'' is pronounced with two syllables.  But in certain dialects of North American English, the vowel of the second syllable is deleted and the word is pronounced as one syllable.<ref>https://www.merriam-webster.com/words-at-play/what-rhymes-with-orange</ref> In such dialects, the two forms are generally {{l|en|allophony|allophonic}}.
