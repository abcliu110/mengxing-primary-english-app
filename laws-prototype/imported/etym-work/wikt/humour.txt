===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*wegʷ-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|humour}}, from {{der|en|fro|humor}}, {{m|fro|humour}}, from {{der|en|la|hūmor}}, correctly {{m|la|ūmor|t=liquid}}, from {{m|la|hūmeō}}, correctly {{m|la|ūmeō|t=to be moist}}. The ''h'' in these words, which was silent in late Classical Latin, is [[w:folk etymology|folk etymological]], due to the erroneous association with the word {{m|la|humus|t=soil}}.

The shift in meaning "liquid" > "mood" is attributed to the classical system of physiology, where human behaviour is regulated by [[w:Humorism#Four_humors|four bodily humours]] (fluids). The sense "mood" gave rise to the verb sense "to give in to someone's mood or whim" and, by narrowing of meaning, the sense "wit".<ref>{{R:Etymonline}}</ref>
