# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To [[traverse]] a [[slope]] or [[contour]], either [[upwards]] ([[ascending]]) or [[downwards]] ([[descending]]).
#* {{Q|en|{{w|Daniel Defoe}}|Robinson Crusoe|Chapter 4|year=1719|quote=after I had found (the goats') haunts a little, I laid wait in this manner for them: I observed if they saw me in the valleys, though they were upon the rocks, they would run away, as in a terrible fright; but if they were feeding in the valleys, and I was upon the rocks, they took no notice of me; from whence I concluded that, by the position of their optics, their sight was so directed downward that they did not readily see objects that were above them; so afterwards I took this method - ''I always '''climbed''' the rocks first, to get above them'', and then had frequently a fair mark.}}
#* {{Q|en|{{w|George Mallory}}|original quotation from Mallory's written manuscript notes for his 1923 United States fundraising lecture tour, found within "Climbing Everest: The Complete Writings of George Mallory"|year=1923|quote=People ask me, ''What is the use of '''climbing''' Mount Everest''? and my answer must at once be, "It is of no use."
