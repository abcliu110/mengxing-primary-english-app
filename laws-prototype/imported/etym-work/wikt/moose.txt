=====Usage notes=====
* The usual plural of {{m-self|en|moose}} is {{m-self|en|moose}}; compare the names of many animals, such as {{m|en|sheep}}, {{m|en|deer}} and {{m|en|fish}}, which are also invariant.
** However, keep in mind that {{m|en|deer}} and {{m|en|sheep}} are invariant due to a phonological change which happened before the Old English period (see {{m|ang|-u}} at Etymology 5), and that {{m|en|fish}} seemingly became reanalyzed according to that paradigm. It may be that {{m|en|elk}} became influenced by the same paradigm (resulting in its current possibility of pluralization with or without {{m|en|-s}}), which may have influenced {{m|en|moose}}.
** On the other hand, it might be that {{m|en|moose}} has an invariant plural because it is a foreign word (as a tentative example, consider {{m|en|ninja}} from {{m+|ja|忍者}}, a language typically without pluralizing suffixes), even though the Algonquin languages pluralize this noun with a suffix. For example, see {{m+|abe|moz}}.
* Other plurals are rare and non-standard: {{m|en|mooses}} (with the usual English plural-forming suffix {{m|en|-s}}) and {{m|en|meese}} (jocularly formed by analogy to {{m|en|goose}} → {{m|en|geese}}).
