=====Usage notes=====
{{rfref|en}}
{{m-lite|en|me|Me}} is traditionally described as the [[objective]] pronoun, meaning it is used as the [[object]] of verbs and prepositions, while the [[subjective]] pronoun {{m-lite|en|I}} should be used as the [[subject]] of verbs. However, “objective” pronouns are widely used as the subject of verbs in colloquial speech when they are accompanied by a conjunction, for example, ''me and her are friends''. This usage is traditionally considered incorrect, and ''she and I are friends'' is the prescriptive construction.

Using {{m-lite|en|me}} as the lone subject of a verb (without a conjunction, for example, ''me want'', ''me like'') is a feature of various types of both [[pidgin]] English and that of [[infant]] English-[[learners]], and is sometimes used by speakers of standard English for [[jocular]] effect (for example, ''me likee'', ''me wantee'').

In some dialects, ''me'' is also used as a [[possessive]] in speech (see below), or put another way (because in writing those dialects often write {{m-lite|en|my}}), those dialects pronounce ''[[my]]'' as /mi/, but in some of those dialects, ''my'' /maɪ/ may still be used when the word is emphasized, as in ''it’s '''my''' turn''.

Some prescriptivists object to the use of {{m-lite|en|me}} following the verb {{m-lite|en|be}}, as in ''it wasn’t me''; they prescribe phrase ''it was not I'' as the correct one, though this may be seen as extreme and used for jocular effect.
