===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*peth₂-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|fethere}}, from {{inh|en|ang|feþer}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*feþru}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*feþrō}}, from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*péth₂r̥||feather, wing}}, from {{m|ine-pro|*peth₂-||to fly}}.

Cognate with {{cog|fy|fear||feather}}, {{cog|cim|bèdara}}, {{m|cim|fòdara||pillowcase}}, {{m|cim|vèdara||feather}}, {{cog|nl|veder}}, {{m|nl|veer||feather}}, {{cog|de|Feder||feather}}, {{cog|nds-de|Fedder||feather}}, {{cog|lb|Fieder||feather}}, {{cog|wym|faoder||feather}}, {{cog|yi|פֿעדער||feather}}, {{cog|da|fjeder}}, {{m|da|fjer||feather}}, {{cog|fo|fjøður||feather}}, {{cog|is|fjöður||feather}}, {{cog|nb|fjær}}, {{m|nb|fjør||feather}}, {{cog|nn|fjøder}}, {{m|nn|fjør||feather}}, {{cog|sv|fjäder||feather}}. Also {{cog|grc|πέτομαι||to fly}}, {{cog|sq|shpend||bird}}, {{cog|la|penna||feather}}, {{cog|xcl|թիռ||flight}}.

The sense correlated with splines and keys ({{senseno|en|spline or key or counterpart thereof}}) probably reflects analogy with the fletching sense ({{senseno|en|arrow stabilizer applied by fletching}}).

===Noun===
{{en-noun}}

# A branching, hair-like structure that grows on the bodies of [[bird]]s, used for flight, swimming, protection and display.
#* {{quote-journal|en|year=1873|author=W. K. Brooks|title=A Feather|journal=Popular Science Monthly|volume=IV|page=687
|passage=Notice, too, that the shaft is not straight, but bent so that the upper surface of the '''feather''' is convex, and the lower concave.}}
#* {{RQ:Burroughs Beasts of Tarzan|chapter=V|passage=Big fellows they were, all of them, their barbaric headdresses and grotesquely painted faces, together with their many metal ornaments and gorgeously coloured '''feathers''', adding to their wild, fierce appearance.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=2000|author=C. J. Puotinen|title=The Encyclopedia of Natural Pet Care|page=362|passage=Nesting birds pluck some of their own '''feathers''' to line the nest, but '''feather''' plucking in pet birds is entirely different.}}
# Long hair on the lower legs of a [[dog]] or [[horse]], especially a [[draft horse]], notably the [[Clydesdale]] breed. Narrowly only the rear hair.
#: {{syn|en|feathers|feathering|horsefeathers}}
#: {{ant|en|spats}}
# {{senseid|en|arrow stabilizer applied by fletching}} One of the [[fin]]s or [[wing]]s on the [[shaft]] of an [[arrow]].
# {{senseid|en|spline or key or counterpart thereof}} A [[longitudinal]] [[strip]] projecting from an object to strengthen it, or to enter a channel in another object and thereby prevent displacement sideways or rotationally but permit motion lengthwise.
#: {{cot|en|key<id:Q1424692><!--sometimes construed as hypernymous-->|spline<id:Q581809>}}
#: {{nearsyn|en|spline<id:mechanical component, feather or featherlike or keylike>}}<!--in some instances wholly synonymous-->
# Kind; nature; species (from the proverbial phrase "[[birds of a feather]]").
#* {{RQ:Shakespeare Timon of Athens|I|i|passage=I am not of that '''feather''' to shake off / My friend when he must need me.}}
# One of the two [[shim|shims]] of the three-piece stone-splitting tool known as {{w|plug and feather}} or [[plug and feathers]]; the feathers are placed in a borehole and then a wedge is driven between them, causing the stone to split.<ref>{{R:Knight AMD}}</ref>
# The [[angular]] [[adjustment]] of an [[oar]] or paddle-wheel float, with reference to a [[horizontal]] [[axis]], as it leaves or enters the water.
# Anything petty or trifling; a [[whit]] or [[jot]].
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1823|title=An Ecclesiastical Memoir of Essex Street Religious Society
|passage=To some pew purchasers he gave deeds, to others he gave, none, but both were promised security, and both it seems were equally secure, for the pew deed as Mr. Melledge declared to Mr. G. was not worth a '''feather'''.}}
# {{lb|en|hunting|in the plural}} [[partridge|Partridges]] and [[pheasant]]s, as opposed to [[rabbit]]s and [[hare]]s (called ''fur'').
# {{lb|en|rail}} A junction indicator attached to a colour-light signal at an angle, which lights up, typically with four white lights in a row, when a diverging route is set up.
#* {{quote-journal|en|date=2020 December 30|author=David Allen|title=Unusual signals...: Morpeth Signal M123|journal=Rail|page=64|text=Signal M123 is a conventional 3-aspect colour light with three Junction Indicators - commonly known as ''''feathers''''.}}
# {{lb|en|cricket}} A [[faint]] [[edge]].
