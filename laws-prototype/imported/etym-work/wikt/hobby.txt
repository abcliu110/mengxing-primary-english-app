===Etymology 1===
From {{inh|en|enm|hoby|t=small horse, pony}}, a variant or alteration of {{m|enm|hobyn|t=a small ambling horse}}, of {{unc|en|title=uncertain and disputed origin}}. According to one theory the term is derived from ''Hobin'', a pet name of {{m|en|Robert}} or {{m|en|Robin}}, at the time frequently used as a [[proper name]] for a workhorse (compare {{m|en|dobbin}}).

Alternatively, possibly from {{der|en|xno|hobyn}}, {{der|en|fro|hobin}}, {{m|fro|obin}} {{gl|"a nag, hobby"; > Modern {{cog|fr|aubin}}, {{cog|it|ubino}}}}: either derived from {{der|en|fro|hober}}, {{m|fro|ober|t=to stir, move}}, from {{der|en|odt|*hobbon}} {{gl|"to toss, move up and down"; whence {{cog|dum|hobbelen|t=to bump along}}}}; or possibly from {{der|en|gmq|-}} origin related to {{cog|da|hoppe|t=a mare}}, {{cog|gmq-osw|hoppa|t=a young mare}}, {{cog|frr|hoppe|t=horse}}; both routes ultimately deriving from {{der|en|gem-pro|*huppōną|t=to  bob, hop}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*kewb-|t=to bend; a bend, joint}}. More at {{l|en|hop}}, {{l|en|hobble}}.

The modern sense of "pastime, activity of interest" is a {{clipping|en|hobby horse|nocap=1}}, which had shifted from "small horse, pony" to "child's toy riding horse" to "favorite pastime or avocation" with the connecting notion being "activity that doesn't go anywhere".
