==English==

===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|fonne}}, {{m|enm|fon||foolish, simple, silly}} or {{m|enm|fonnen||make a fool of}}, from {{der|en|enm|fonne|t=a fool, dupe}}, probably of {{der|en|gmq|-}} origin, related to {{cog|sv|fånig||foolish}}, {{cog|sv|fåne||a fool}}, from {{der|en|non|fáni|t=vain person, swaggerer}}, but of {{unk|en|title=unknown}} ultimate origin. Perhaps related to or influenced by {{m|sv|fjäll|t=rock, cliff, mountain}}.<ref>{{R:SAOB_online|unik=F_1761-0262.2nRV}}</ref> Compare also {{cog|en|fumble}}, {{cog|nn|fomme|t=clumsy fool}}.

Compare also {{cog|no|fomme}}, {{m|no|fume||a fool}}. More at {{m|en|fon}}, {{m|en|fond}}.

As a noun, ''fun'' is recorded from 1700, with a meaning “a cheat, trick, hoax”, from a verb ''fun'' meaning “to cheat, trick” (1680s). The meaning “diversion, amusement” dates to the 1720s. The older meaning is preserved in the phrase ''to'' {{m|en|make fun of}} (1737) and in usage of the adjective {{m|en|funny}}. The use of ''fun'' as adjective is newest and is due to reanalysis of the noun; this was incipient in the mid-19th century.

Alternative etymology connected {{cog|enm|fonne}} with {{der|en|ofs|fonna}}, {{m|ofs|fone}}, {{m|ofs|fomne}}, variant forms of {{m|ofs|fāmne}}, {{m|ofs|fēmne||young woman, virgin}}, from {{der|en|gmw-pro|*faimnijā}}, from {{der|en|gem-pro|*faimnijǭ||maiden}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*peymen-||girl}}, {{m|ine-pro|*poymen-||breast milk}}. If so, then cognate with {{cog|ang|fǣmne||maid, virgin, damsel, bride}}, {{cog|fy|famke||girl}}, {{cog|stq|fone}}, {{m|stq|fon||woman, maid, servant," also "weakling, simpleton}}.
