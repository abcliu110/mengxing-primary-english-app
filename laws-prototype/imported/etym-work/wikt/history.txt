===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*weyd-}}
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:historie|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|historie}}, from {{der|en|fro|estoire}}, {{m|fro|estorie||chronicle, history, story}} ({{cog|fr|histoire}}), from {{der|en|la|historia}}, from {{der|en|grc|ἱστορίᾱ||learning through research}}, from {{m|grc|ἱστορέω||to research, inquire (and) record}}, from {{m|grc|ἵστωρ||the knowing, wise one}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*weyd-||see, know}}. {{doublet|en|story|storey}}.

Attested in Middle English in 1393 by {{w|John Gower}}, ''{{w|Confessio Amantis}},''<ref>OED</ref> which was aimed at an educated audience familiar with French and Latin.

====Usage notes====
* The chief [[polysemy|polysemic]] ambiguity of the word ''history'' in [[natural language]] (in a nutshell, "the past" versus "that portion of the past for which written records exist") is handled with the help of a [[coordinate term]] pair ({{m|en|prehistory}} and ''history'') or a qualifier (''recorded history''), yielding clarifying phrases such as ''in all of human history and prehistory'' or ''in all recorded history''.
