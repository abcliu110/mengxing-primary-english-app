====Usage notes====
''Ago'' renders a sense of doing or making which is [[continuative]] or [[behavioral]]. For a sense of a specific [[instance]] or [[occasion]] of doing or making, see {{l|la|facio}}. For a sense of doing or making which is yet more [[continuative]], see {{l|la|agito}} and {{l|la|gero}}.

According to Döderlein, another difference between ''ago'' and ''facio'' when they mean "make" is that ''ago'' typically has to do with making something that does not continue after the "actor" stops doing the action; whereas with ''facio'', the object continues to exist after the maker has made the thing. In other words, ''ago'' is temporal, whereas ''facio'' is spatial.<ref>{{cite-book|en|year=1858|translator=Arnold, Henry Hamilton|entry=Agere; Facere; Gerere; Opus; Factum; Age; I nunc; Deger|title=Döderlein's Hand-book of Latin synonyms|location=Andover [Mass.]|publisher=W. F. Draper|original=Lateinische Synonymen und Etymologien|origlang=de|by=[[w:Johann Christoph Wilhelm Ludwig Döderlein|Ludwig Döderlein]]|page=9|pageurl=https://archive.org/details/dderleinshandboo00doed/page/9/mode/1up}}</ref>

====Reconstruction notes====
Weiss explains the perfect {{m|la||ēgī}} as an imperfect to an original Narten present {{l|ine-pro||*h₂ḗǵ-ti}}, under the assumption that {{IPAfont|*/h₂/}} has no colouring effect upon an adjacent {{l|ine-pro||*ē}} (as per so-called [[Eichner's law]]).{{ref|name=Weiss|{{cite-book|year=2009|author=Weiss, Michael|title=Outline of the Historical and Comparative Grammar of Latin|publisher=Beech Stave Press|page=412f}}}} Pronk, on top of finding the evidence for Eichner's law itself inconclusive, dismisses Weiss' explanation as involving too many unlikely hypotheses,{{ref|name=Pronk|{{R:ine:Pronk:2019b|140f}}}} and presents De Vaan's explanation of {{m|la||ēgī}} as analogical after {{m|la|faciō|faciō, fēcī|id=to do|t=to do}} as the most reasonable hypothesis.{{ref|name=EDL|{{R:itc:EDL|pages=30-31|head=agō, -ere}}}}
