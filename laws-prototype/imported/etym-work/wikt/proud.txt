===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|proud}}, {{m|enm|prout}}, {{m|enm|prut}}, from {{inh|en|ang|prūd}}, {{m|ang|prūt|t=proud, arrogant, haughty}} (compare {{cog|ang|prȳtung|t=pride}}; {{m|ang|prȳde}}, {{m|ang|prȳte|t=pride}}, see also {{ncog|ang|deal}}), probably from {{der|en|fro|prod}}, {{m|fro|prut|t=brave, gallant}} (modern {{cog|fr|preux}}), from {{der|en|la-lat|prode|prōde|useful}}, derived from {{cog|la|prosum|prōdesse|t=to be of value}}; however, the Old English [[umlaut]] derivatives {{m|and|prȳte}}, {{m|ang|prȳtian}}, etc. suggest the word may be older and possibly native. Compare {{cog|non|prýði|t=ornament; gallantry, bravery}}. See also {{l|en|pride}}.

Cognate with {{cog|nds-de|praud}}, {{cog|non|prúðr|t=gallant, brave, magnificent, stately, handsome, fine}} ({{cog|is|prúður}}, Middle {{cog|sv|prudh}}, {{cog|da|prud}}).
