===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*dʰegʷʰ-|*(s)tenh₂-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|Thursday}}, {{m|enm|Thuresday}}, from {{inh|en|ang|þursdæġ}}, {{m|ang|þuresdæġ||Thursday}}, possibly from a contraction of {{m|ang|þunresdæġ||Thursday|lit=[[Thor]]'s day}}, equivalent to {{af|en|Thor|-s-|day}}, or of {{der|en|gmq|-}} origin, from {{der|en|non|þórsdagr}}; all from {{der|en|gmw-pro|*Þunras dag||day of the thunder god}}. Compare {{cog|fy|tongersdei}}, {{cog|nds-de|Dunnersdag}}, {{cog|nl|donderdag}}, {{cog|de|Donnerstag}}, {{cog|da|torsdag}}. More at {{l|en|thunder}}, {{l|en|day}}.

A [[calque]] of Latin ''{{l|la|diēs Iovis}} ({{l|la|diēs Jovis}})'', via an association ({{m|la|interpretātiō germānica}}) of the god [[Thor]] with the Roman god of thunder [[Jove]] (Jupiter).
