==English==

===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|tall}}, {{m|enm|talle}}, {{m|enm|tal|t=seemly, becoming, handsome, good-looking, excellent, good, valiant, lively in speech, bold, great, large, big}}, from {{inh|en|ang|*tæl}}, {{m|ang|ġetæl|t=swift, ready, having mastery of}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*talaz|t=submissive, pliable, obedient}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*dol-}}, {{m|ine-pro|*del-|t=to aim, calculate, adjust, reckon}}.

Cognate with {{cog|sco|tal|t=high, lofty, tall}}, {{cog|ofs|tel|t=swift}}, {{cog|osx|gital|t=quick}}, {{cog|goh|gizal|t=active, agile}}, {{cog|got|𐌿𐌽𐍄𐌰𐌻𐍃|t=indocile, disobedient}}.

The ''Oxford English Dictionary'' notes: "The sense development [of ''tall''] is remarkable, but is paralleled more or less by that of other adjectives expressing estimation, such as ''[[buxom]]'', ''[[canny]]'', ''[[clean]]'', ''[[clever]]'', ''[[cunning]]'', ''[[deft]]'', ''[[elegant]]'', ''[[handsome]]'', ''[[pretty]]'', ''[[proper]]''; German {{m|de|klein}}, as compared with English ''[[clean]]'', presents the antithesis to modern ''tall'' as compared to ''tall'' in early Middle English. It has been conjectured that in the sense 'high of stature' it is a different word, adopted from the Welsh ''{{m|cy|tal}}'' in some sense; but the latter is, according to Professor Rhŷs, merely a 16th-century borrowing of the English word (in Owen Pughe's Dictionary erroneously mixed up with the genuine Welsh word {{m|cy|tal||end, brow, forehead}}, with which it has no possible connection.)"<ref>''Oxford English Dictionary'' '''T''', p. 57.</ref>
