===Etymology 1===
[[Image:Buxus sempervirens 2.jpg|thumb|right|A bush (woody plant)]]
From {{inh|en|enm|bush}}, probably continuing {{inh|en|ang|*busċ}}, {{m|ang|*bysċ}} (possibly reflected in place names), from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*busk}}. 

The Middle English variants {{m|enm|busk}} and {{m|enm|bosk}} may reflect Scandinavian/{{der|en|gmq|-}} and {{der|en|fro|-}} influence, respectively; {{m|enm|buish}} and {{m|enm|boish}} may have been influenced by {{cog|fro|bois|t=wood}}. These variant-level explanations are uncertain and do not by themselves establish Scandinavian or French origin for the regular form {{m|enm|bush}}.<ref>{{cite-web|title=bush|url=https://www.ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=bush|work=The American Heritage Dictionary of the English Language|publisher=HarperCollins|accessdate=2026-10-19}}</ref><ref>{{cite-web|author=Albert L. Lloyd; Rosemarie Lühr; Otto Springer|year=1998|title=busc, bosc|url=https://ewa.saw-leipzig.de/articles/24620/en|work=Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Althochdeutschen|volume=II|publisher=Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht|accessdate=2026-10-19}}</ref>

The Germanic forms go back to {{inh|en|gem-pro|*buskaz|t=bush, thicket}} (through {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*busk}} in West Germanic).<ref name="MW-bush">{{cite-web|title=bush|url=https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/bush|work=Merriam-Webster.com Dictionary|accessdate=2026-10-19}}</ref> {{doublet|en|bosque}}.

The senses referring to uncultivated land are an extension of the earlier senses, probably influenced by the colonial use of {{der|en|nl|bosch|t=wood, forest}} (modern {{m|nl|bos}}), a cognate of the English word.<ref name="MW-bush"/><ref name="DSAE-bush">{{cite-web|title=bush|url=https://dsae.co.za/entry/bush/e01377|work=A Dictionary of South African English on Historical Principles|accessdate=2026-10-19}}</ref>

The tavern sense is from the sign of a bush usually employed to indicate such places.

Compare also {{cog|la|boscus}} and the Romance forms {{cog|oc|bòsc}}, {{cog|fr|bois}}, {{m|fr|bûche}} and {{m|fr|buisson}}, {{cog|it|bosco}} and {{m|it|boscaglia}}, {{cog|es|bosque}}, {{cog|pt|bosque}}.
