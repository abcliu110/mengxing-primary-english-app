# One individual's [[personality]], [[character]], [[demeanor]], or [[disposition]].
#: {{ux|en|She remained her usual cheerful '''self''' despite recent setbacks}}
#* '''2003''', {{w|Roy_Porter|Roy Porter}}, ''Flesh in the Age of Reason'', Ch.1, at p.7:
#*: {{w|John Locke}} argued that the mind is not like a furnished flat, prestocked before occupation with innate ideas, but like a home put together piecemeal from mental acquisitions picked up bit by bit. The '''self''' is thus the bit-by-bit product of experience and education: we are what we become - or, in {{w|William Wordsworth|Wordworth's}} later phrase, the child is the father of the man. Particular parents, surroundings and stimuli produce individuated selves. Identity is thus unique because contingent, the cumulative product of ceaseless occurrences.
