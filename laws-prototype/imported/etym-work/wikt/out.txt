#: {{ux|en|Last night we slept '''out''' under the stars.}}
#: {{ux|en|It's cold '''out'''.}}

#: {{ux|en|Turn the lights '''out'''.}}

====Usage notes====
* The use of ''out'' as a preposition in particular contexts (as in ''look out the window'') is standard in American, Australian{{,}} and New Zealand English, and is common in speech and informal contexts in Britain, but is not accepted in formal British English.<ref>{{R:Lexico}}</ref><ref>{{R:MacMillan}}</ref>
* Omission of ''of'' in other contexts (for instance, ''Get out the way!'') is nonstandard – or at least informal – in all varieties.

{{col-top|2|Translation notes}}
In many languages there is no direct translation, as the idea expressed by the English ''adverb'' is expressed by a ''prefix'' in many languages. Other West Germanic languages are halfway in-between to some extent as it uses a ''prefix'' in the infinitive of its verbs, but often, though not always, separates the prefix into the same form as the English ''adverb'' when conjugating them.

* Dutch: usually expressed by the prefix [[uit-]]
* Esperanto: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|eo|el-}} and/or preposition {{m|eo|el}} (1), {{m|eo|ekstere}} (2)
* Finnish: [[ablative case]] (-lta, -ltä) or [[elative case]] (-sta, -stä)
* German: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|de|aus-}}
* Hungarian: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|hu|ki-}}
* Latin: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|la|ex-}}
* Latvian: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|lv|iz-}}
* Russian: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|ru|вы-}}, sometimes by {{m|ru|вз-}}, {{m|ru|воз-}}
* Slovak: usually expressed by the prefix {{m|sk|vy-}}, or sometimes {{m|sk|z-}}
* Swedish: sometimes expressed by the prefix {{m|sv|ut}}. In some cases considered somewhat formal.

====Usage notes====
* In cricket, the specific cause or rule under which a batsman is out appears after the word “out”, e.g., “out hit the ball twice”.
* In baseball, the cause is expressed as a verb with adverbial “out”, e.g., “he grounded out”.
