===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*pel-|id=fold}}
The verb is from {{inh|en|enm|folden}}, from {{inh|en|ang|fealdan}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*falþan}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*falþaną||to fold}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*pel-||to fold}}. 

Cognate with {{cog|nl|vouwen}}, {{cog|de|falten}}.

The noun is from {{inh|en|enm|folde}}, {{m|enm|falde}}, itself derived from the verb.

====Verb====
{{en-verb|+|+|+|+,~en<l:obsolete>}}

# {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[bend]] (any thin [[material]], such as paper) over so that it comes in contact with itself.
#: {{ux|en|Next, '''fold''' the paper over, so the right half is on top of the left half.}}
#: {{ux|en|The newspaper was '''folded''' under his arm.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To make the proper [[arrangement]] (in a thin material) by bending.
#: {{ux|en|If you '''fold''' the sheets, they'll fit more easily in the drawer.}}
#: {{ux|en|The clothes were '''folded''' by the housekeeper.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[draw]] or [[coil]] (one’s arms, a snake’s body, etc.) around something so as to [[enclose]] or [[embrace]] it.
# {{senseid|en|stir gently}} {{lb|en|transitive|cooking}} To [[stir]] (semisolid ingredients) gently, with an action as if folding over a solid.
#: {{ux|en|'''Fold''' the egg whites into the batter.}}
#* '''8 Jan 2020''', Felicity Cloake in ''The Guardian'', ''[https://www.theguardian.com/food/2020/jan/08/how-make-perfect-gluten-free-chocolate-brownies-recipe How to make the perfect gluten-free chocolate brownies – recipe]''
#*: if you want to make life really easy for yourself, may I point you in the direction of Sunflour’s recipe, which '''folds''' four eggs and 150g ground almonds into 500g chocolate spread.
# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To become folded; to form folds.
#: {{ux|en|Cardboard doesn't '''fold''' very easily.}}
# {{senseid|en|fall over or collapse}} {{lb|en|intransitive|informal}} To [[fall over]]; to [[collapse]] or [[give way]]; to be [[crush]]ed.
#: {{syn|en|buckle#English:_collapse or crumple physically|cave#Verb|cave in#Verb|crumple#Verb}}
#: {{ux|en|The chair '''folded''' under his enormous weight.}}
# {{senseid|en|give way or give up}} {{lb|en|transitive|or|intransitive}} To give way on a point or in an argument.
#: {{syn|en|buckle#English:_give way, give up, surrender|cave#Verb|cave in#Verb|crumple#Verb}}
#: {{ux|en|He '''folded''' that point and didn't bring it up again.}}
#: {{ux|en|He kept saying it was my fault, but when I brought up Sausalito, he '''folded'''.}}
# {{senseid|en|withdraw from betting}} {{lb|en|intransitive|poker}} To [[withdraw]] from [[bet]]ting.
# {{senseid|en|mahjong}} {{lb|en|intransitive|mahjong}} To [[abandon]] completing one's [[hand]] and play in a [[defensive]] manner to avoid [[deal in|dealing in]].
#: {{ux|en|With no hearts in the river and no chance to hit his straight, he '''folded'''.}}
# {{senseid|en|withdraw or quit generally}} {{lb|en|intransitive|by extension}} To [[withdraw]] or [[quit]] in general.
# {{senseid|en|fail}} {{lb|en|intransitive}} To [[fail]], to [[collapse]], to [[disband]].
# {{senseid|en|go out of business}} {{lb|en|intransitive|business}} Of a company, to cease to trade.
#: {{ux|en|The company '''folded''' after six quarters of negative growth.}}
# {{senseid|en|double or lay together}} {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[double]] or [[lay]] [[together]] (one’s arms, hands, wings, etc.) so as to [[overlap]] with each other.
#: {{ux|en|He '''folded''' his arms in defiance.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive|obsolete}} To [[plait]] or [[mat]] (hair) [[together]].
# {{senseid|en|cover or wrap up}} {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[enclose]] in a fold of material, to [[swathe]], [[wrap up]], [[cover]], [[enwrap]].
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To [[enclose]] within folded arms, to [[clasp]], to [[embrace]] (''see also'' [[enfold]]).
#* {{RQ:Stoker Dracula|21||He put out his arms and '''folded''' her to his breast. And for a while she lay there sobbing. He looked at us over her bowed head, with eyes that blinked damply above his quivering nostrils. His mouth was set as steel.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive|figuratively}} To [[cover up]], to [[conceal]].
#* {{RQ:Shakespeare Lucrece|passage=I will not poyſon thee with my attaint, / Nor '''fold''' my fault in cleanly coin’d excuſes, / My ſable ground of ſinne I will not paint, / To hide the truth of this falſe nights abuſes.}}

===Etymology 2===
The noun is from {{inh|en|enm|fold}}, {{m|enm|fald}}, from {{inh|en|ang|fald}}, {{m|ang|falæd}}, {{m|ang|falod||fold, stall, stable, cattle-pen}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*falud}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*faludaz||enclosure}}. 

Akin to {{cog|sco|fald}}, {{m|sco|fauld||an enclosure for livestock}}, Dutch {{m|nl|vaalt||dung heap}}, {{cog|gml|valt|valt, vālt|an inclosed space, a yard}}, {{cog|da|fold||pen for herbivorous livestock}}, {{cog|sv|fålla||corral, pen, pound}}.

The verb is from Late {{inh|en|enm|fooldyn}}, itself derived from the noun.
