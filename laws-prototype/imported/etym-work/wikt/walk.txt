===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*welH-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|walk}}, {{m|enm|walke}}, {{m|enm|walken}}, {{m|enm|walkyn}}, {{m|enm|wolken|t=to roll, toss, or turn; to walk; to move; to be living; to beat or full; to perform}}, a conflation of {{inh|en|ang|wealcan|t=to move around; to revolve; to roll; to fluctuate; to discuss}} ({{m|ang|ġewealcan|t=to go, traverse; to roll}}) and {{inh|en|ang|wealcian|t=to curl, roll up, twist; to wrinkle}}; both from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*walkan}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*walkaną|t=to roll, toss, turn, wind; to walk, wander, wend; to full, trample}}, {{m|gem-pro|*walkōną|t=to roll about; to full}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*walg-|t=to twist, turn, move}}.
{{col-top|2|cog}}
Cognate with {{cog|bar|woikn|t=to full, tan; to knead dough; to roll out dough}}, {{cog|cim|balchan|t=to beat, hit, strike}}, {{cog|nl|zwalken|t=to walk around}}, {{cog|de|walken|t=to full, tan, walk; to knead; to beat up}}, {{cog|da|valke|t=to full, walk, waulk}}, {{cog|fo|válka|t=make dirty; stir up dirt}}, {{cog|sv|valka|t=to full cloth, to waulk}}; also {{cog|la|valgus|t=bent out; bandy, bow-legged}}. More at {{l|en|vagrant}} and {{l|en|whelk}}. Doublet of {{l|en|waulk}}.
