===Etymology 1===
From {{inh|en|enm|hurten}}, {{m|enm|hirten}}, {{m|enm|hertan|t=to injure, scathe, knock together}}, from {{der|en|fro-nor|hurter}} {{gloss|"to ram into, strike, collide with"; > Modern {{cog|fr|heurter}}}}, perhaps from {{der|en|frk|*hūrt|t=a battering ram}}, cognate with {{cog|cy|hwrdd|t=ram}} and {{cog|kw|hordh|t=ram}}.  Compare {{cog|gem-pro|*hrūtaną}}, {{m|gem-pro|*hreutaną|t=to fall, beat}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*krew-|t=to fall, beat, smash, strike, break}}; however, the earliest instances of the verb in Middle English are as old as those found in Old French, which leads to the possibility that the Middle English word may instead be a reflex of an unrecorded {{inh|en|ang|*hyrtan}}, which later merged with the Old French verb. Germanic cognates include {{cog|nl|horten||to push against, strike}}, {{cog|gml|hurten||to run at, collide with}}, {{cog|gmh|hurten|t=to push, bump, attack, storm, invade}}, {{cog|non|hrútr||battering ram}}.

Alternate etymology traces {{cog|fro-nor|hurter}} rather to {{der|en|non|hrútr|t=ram (male sheep)}}, lengthened-grade variant of {{m|non|hjǫrtr||stag}},<ref>D.Q. Adams, ''Encyclopedia of Indo-European Culture'', s.v. "horn" (London: Fitzroy-Dearborn, 1999), 273.</ref> from {{der|en|gem-pro|*herutuz}}, {{m|gem-pro|*herutaz||hart, male deer}}, which would relate it to English {{m|en|hart||male deer}}. See {{l|en|hart}}.
