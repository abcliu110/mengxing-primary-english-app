===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*kelh₂-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|clerc}}, from {{inh|en|ang|clerc}}, from {{der|en|la-lat|clēricus||priest, clergyman, cleric”, also generally “learned man, clerk}}, from {{der|en|grc|κληρικός|t=of the clergy|pos=adj. in church jargon}}, from {{m|grc|κλῆρος|t=lot, inheritance”, originally “shard used in casting lots}}. {{doublet|en|cleric}}.

Compare typologically {{m+|ru|дьяк}} (akin to {{m|ru|дья́кон}}).

===Noun===
{{en-noun}}

# One who [[occupational]]ly provides [[assistance]] by [[work]]ing with [[record]]s, [[account]]s, [[letter]]s, etc.; an [[office]] [[worker]].
#* {{RQ:Gilbert and Sullivan HMS Pinafore|When I Was a Lad|10|As office boy I made such a mark<br>That they gave me the post of a junior '''clerk'''.}}
#* {{RQ:Besant Ivory Gate|chapter=Prologue|passage=Thus, when he drew up instructions in lawyer language, he expressed the important words by an initial, a medial, or a final consonant, and made scratches for all the words between; his '''clerks''', however, understood him very well.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|title=The Age of Revolution. 1789-1848|author=Eric Hobsbawm|year=1962|page=193|text='''clerks'''; a brand of humanity which, as the literature from Dickens to Gogol makes only too clear, was hardly to be envied except perhaps for the privilege of public service, the security which allowed them to starve at an even rhythm all their lives.}}
## A [[salesclerk]]; a person who serves [[customer]]s in a store or market.
## A [[law clerk]].
## An [[employee]] at a hotel who deals with guests.
## The chief [[legal]] [[advisor]] of a [[legislature]] or legislative [[chamber]], who is usually also responsible for keeping [[minutes]] of [[sitting]]s.
# {{lb|en|Quakerism}} A [[facilitator]] of a [[Quaker]] [[meeting]] for [[business]] [[affair]]s.
# {{lb|en|archaic}} In the Church of England, the layman that assists in the church service, especially in reading the responses (also called ''parish clerk'' and ''Bible clerk'').
#* {{RQ:Shakespeare Richard 2|4|1|passage=God save the King! Will no man say, amen? / Am I both priest and '''clerk'''? Well then, amen.}}
# {{lb|en|dated}} A cleric or clergyman (the legal title for clergy of the Church of England is "Clerk in Holy Orders", still used in legal documents and cherished by some of their number).
# {{lb|en|obsolete}} A [[scholar]].
#* '''13th century''', Traditional carol,
#*: And all was for an appel, an appel that he toke/As '''clerkès''' finden written in their boke.
