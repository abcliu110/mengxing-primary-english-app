==English==

===Etymology===
Attested since 1540, origin uncertain. Probably from {{bor|en|nds|-}} and {{der|en|gml|lasich|t=slack, feeble, lazy}},<ref>{{R:Dictionary.com}}</ref><ref>{{R:Merriam-Webster Online}}</ref> from {{m|gml|las}}, from {{der|en|osx|lask}}, from {{der|en|gem-pro|*lasiwaz}}, {{m|gem-pro|*laskaz|t=feeble, weak}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*las-|t=weak}}.

Akin to {{cog|nl|leuzig|t=lazy}}, {{cog|de|lässig|t=casual, nonchalant, laid-back, easy-going}}, {{cog|non|lasinn|t=limpy, tired, weak}}, {{cog|ang|lesu}}, {{m|ang|lysu|t=false, evil, base}}. More at {{m|en|lush}}.

An alternative etymology traces ''lazy'' to [[w:Early Modern English|Early Modern English]] {{m|en|laysy}}, a derivative of {{m|en|lay}} (plural {{af|en|[[lay]]s|-y|id2=adjectival}}) in the same way that {{m|en|tipsy}} is derived from {{m|en|tip}}. See {{m|en|lay}}.
