===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*bʰeydʰ-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|feith|faith}} (also {{m|enm|fay}}), borrowed from {{der|en|fro|foi|fei, feid}}, from {{der|en|la|fidēs|fidem}}. {{dbt|en|foy}}. Displaced native {{cog|ang|ġelēafa}}, which was also a word for {{m|en|belief}}.

* Old French had {{IPAchar|[θ]}} as a final devoiced allophone of {{IPAchar|/ð/}} from lenited Latin {{IPAchar|/d/}}; this eventually fell silent in the 12th century.  The ''-th'' of the Middle English forms is most straightforwardly accounted for as a direct borrowing of a French {{IPAchar|[θ]}}. However, it has also been seen as arising from alteration of a French form with ''-d'' under influence of English abstract nouns in the suffix {{m|en|-th}} (''e.g., truth, ruth, health, etc.''), or as a recharacterization of a French form like {{m|fro|fay}}, {{m|fro|fey}}, {{m|fro|fei}} with the same suffix. Compare {{cog|roa-cha|fiate}}, {{m|roa-cha|fiaite}}, showing the same preservation of the final consonant.
