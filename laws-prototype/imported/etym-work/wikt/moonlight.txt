===Etymology===
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:moonelight|:afeq|moon|light<id:illumination>|tree=1}}
The {{lg|noun}} is derived from {{inh|en|enm|moonelight,monelight,mone lyght|t=light of the moon; (''heraldry'') pattern of moons on the field of a heraldic banner}},<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=mọ̄ne-light|pos=n|id=MED28553}}</ref> from {{m|enm|mon}}, {{m|enm|mone|t=moon}} (from {{inh|en|ang|mōna|t=moon}},<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=mọ̄n(e|pos=''n.''(1)|noformat=1|id=MED28542}}</ref> ultimately from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*mḗh₁n̥s|t=moon; month}}) + {{m|enm|light|t=light}}<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=light|pos=n|id=MED25497}}</ref> (from {{inh|en|ang|lēoht|t=light}}, ultimately from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*lewk-|t=bright; to see; to shine}}). {{surf|en|moon|light|notext=1|type=exocentric}}.<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=n.'' and ''adj|id=1016343576|date=December 2025|nodot=1}}; {{R:Lexico|pos=n}}</ref>

The {{lg|verb}} is derived from the noun. {{senseno|en|to do a moonlight flit|uc=1}} (“to secretly leave premises without paying the rent”) is a {{back-formation|en|moonlight flit|nocap=1}}, while {{senseno|en|to make a night-time attack}} (“to make a night-time attack on a tenant farmer”) is probably a {{bf|en|moonlighter|nocap=1}}.<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=v|id=1143069831|date=March 2025|nodot=1}}; {{R:Lexico|pos=v}}</ref>

====Usage notes====
Regarding {{senseno|en|to work a secondary job}} (“to do work for pay which is in addition to a main job”), in US English the word means simply to work at secondary employment.<ref>{{cite-book|entry=moonlight ''vi''|editor=Frederick C. Mish|title=[[w:Webster's Dictionary|Merriam-Webster’s Collegiate Dictionary]]|edition=10th|location=Springfield, Mass.|publisher=w:Merriam-Webster|year=1993|page=755|pageurl=https://archive.org/details/merriamwebstersc0000unse_z9w8/page/755/mode/1up|column=2|isbn=978-0-87779-710-4|passage=to hold a second job in addition to a regular one}}.</ref><ref>{{R:Merriam-Webster Online|pos=v}}</ref> In UK English, it sometimes used to imply doing [[undeclared work]] (that is, work for which income tax was not paid on the extra money earned),<ref>See, for example, {{R:Collins English Dictionary|pos=v}}</ref><ref>{{cite-book|entry=moonlight ''vb''|editor=Diana Treffy|title=[[w:Collins English Dictionary|Collins English Paperback Dictionary]]|edition=4th|location=Glasgow|publisher=[[w:William Collins, Sons|Collins]]|year=1999|year_published=2001|page=526|pageurl=https://archive.org/details/collinspaperback0000unse_i1p8/page/526/mode/1up|column=2|isbn=978-0-00-472208-5|passage=to work at a secondary job, esp. illegally.}}</ref> but more recent editions of some UK dictionaries no longer differentiate between the two meanings.<ref>See, for example, {{R:Cambridge|pos=v}}</ref> In this case, {{lg|adjective}}s would be used to qualify the legality of the moonlighting.
