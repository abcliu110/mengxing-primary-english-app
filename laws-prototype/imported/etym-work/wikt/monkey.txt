===Etymology===
{{unc|en}}: 

* May be derived from {{suf|en|monk|-y|alt2=-ey|pos2=diminutive suffix|id2=diminutive}}, 
* or borrowed from {{bor|en|gml|Moneke}}, the name of the son of Martin the Ape in ''{{w|Reynard the Fox}}'' (which may represent an unattested colloquial {{der|en|gml|*moneke}}, {{m|gml|*moneken}}), itself of uncertain origin: 
** Possibly derived from a {{der|en|roa|-}} term represented by Late {{der|en|frm|monne}} {{gl|whence Modern {{cog|fr|mone|t=monkey}}}} or earlier {{cog|fro|monnekin|t=monkey}}, originally {{m|fro|Monnekin}}, the name of a monkey in ''Li Dis d'Entendement''. Compare also {{cog|fro|-}} and {{cog|frm|monin|t=monkey}}.  
*** The French terms may have been borrowed from {{der|en|it|monna|t=monkey}}, from {{der|en|osp|mona|t=female monkey}}, itself a shortening of {{m|osp|mamona}}, variant of {{m|osp|maimón}}, from {{der|en|ar|مَيْمُون||baboon}}). 
*** However, {{cog|fro|monnekin}} may alternatively be unrelated to the other terms, instead being a borrowing of Early {{der|en|dum|mannekin|pos=a diminutive of ''man''|lit=little human}}, and if so ''monkey'' is a doublet of {{m|en|mannequin}}; see modern {{m+|nl|manneken}}.
