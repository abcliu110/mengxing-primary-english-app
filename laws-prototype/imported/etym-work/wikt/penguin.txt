===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|id=bird|:af|cy:pen<id:head><unc:1>|cy:gwyn<id:white><unc:1>|:der|la:pinguis<id:fat><unc:1>|tree=1}}
{{unc|en}}.<ref>T.F. Hoad, ''Concise Dictionary of English Etymology'', {{ISBN|978-0-19-283098-2}}; headword ''penguin''</ref> {{etydate|16th century}} in reference to the [[auk]] of the Northern hemisphere; the word was later applied to the superficially similar birds of the Southern hemisphere (as was {{m|en|woggin}}). Possibly from {{der|en|cy|pen||head}} and {{m|cy|gwyn||white}}, or from {{der|en|la|pinguis||fat}}. See [[Citations:penguin|citations]] and the {{w|Penguin#Etymology|Wikipedia article}}.

{{senseno|en|nun|uc=1}} originates from the often black-and-white {{l|en|habit|id=religious attire}} worn by nuns, which resemble the bird's colors.

===Noun===
{{en-noun}}

# Any of several [[flightless]] [[sea bird]]s, of the family {{taxfmt|Spheniscidae|family}} within the [[order]] {{taxfmt|Sphenisciformes|order}}, found in the [[Southern Hemisphere]], marked by their usual upright stance, walking on short legs, and (generally) their stark black and white plumage. {{defdate|from 16th c.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1638|author=w:Thomas Herbert|title=Some Yeares Travels|section=I
|passage=Here are also birds cal'd '''''Pen-gwins''''' (white-head in ''Welch'') like Pigmies walking upright, their finns or wings hanging very orderly downe like sleeves {{...}}}}
# {{lb|en|obsolete|or|historical}} An [[auk]] (sometimes especially a [[great auk]]), a bird of the [[Northern Hemisphere]].
#* {{quote-journal|en|year=1772|month=March|title=Account of the Settlement of the Malouines|journal=The Gentleman's and London Magazine|page=166
|passage=This last species of '''penguin''', or auk, seems to be the same with the ''[[:w:Tufted puffin|alca cirrhata]]'' of Dr. Pallis, Spicileg. Zool. Fasc. v. p. 7. tab. i. & v. fig. 1–3. F.}}
#* {{quote-journal|en|year=1885|journal=Journal of the American Geographical Society of New York
|passage=More than a hundred years ago, for example, was seen the last of the great wingless '''penguins''' or auks, which early writers quaintly called " wobble-birds."}}
# {{senseid|en|nun}} {{lb|en|slang}} A [[nun]].
#* {{quote-av|en|writer={{w|Dan Aykroyd}}|title={{w|The Blues Brothers (film)|The Blues Brothers}}|text=''Jake ({{w|John Belushi}})'': What are we doing here? <br/> ''Elwood (Dan Aykroyd)'': You promised you'd visit the '''penguin''' the day you got out. <br/> ''Jake'': Yeah. So, I lied to her. <br/> ''Elwood'':You can't lie to a nun. We gotta go in and visit the '''penguin'''.|year=1980|medium=motion picture}}
# {{lb|en|juggling}} A type of catch where the palm of the hand is facing towards the leg with the arm stretched downward, resembling the flipper of a penguin.
# {{lb|en|UK|military|slang}} A member of the [[air force]] who does not [[fly]] [[aircraft]].
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1942|author=Hermann Hagedorn|title=Sunward I've Climbed|page=143
|passage=Although it is a permanent R.A.F. station (pre-war, that is), we meander about, even in the Mess, in battle dress and flying boots, sweaters, etc., much to the disgust of some of the more dignified ''''penguins'''' {{...}}}}
#* {{quote-book|en|url=https://archive.org/details/invasionswithout0000berg/page/n15/mode/2up?q=%22penguin%22|page=xi|title=Invasions without tears: the story of Canada's top-scoring Spitfire wing in Europe during the Second World War|isbn=9780394222776|lccn=94189547|author=Monty Berger|year=1994|passage=Notwithstanding his elevated status as the wing’s Senior Intelligence Officer, Monty, too, was a “'''penguin''',” equal in that respect to an armament assistant, airframe mechanic, pigeon loftsman or any of the seventy-odd ground trades listed by the Royal Canadian Air Force at the war’s end.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/In_the_Company_of_Women/LurcDAAAQBAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=%22a%20penguin%22%20air%20force&pg=PT55&printsec=frontcover&bsq=%22a%20penguin%22%20air%20force|title=In the Company of Women|author=Kate Christie|publisher=Bella Books|isbn=9781594937972|oclc=1025342370|date=2015-06-01|passage=She may have been a “'''penguin'''”—an Air Force service member who didn't fly—but it sure beat being at home, as Brady had pointed out.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Kxq7DwAAQBAJ&newbks=1&newbks_redir=0&lpg=PA46&dq=%22penguins%22%20ground%20air%20force&pg=PA46#v=onepage&q=%22penguins%22%20ground%20air%20force&f=false|title=An Adventure in Education: The Autobiography of an Accidental Career in Education|author=Derek Esp|publisher=Troubador Publishing Ltd|isbn=9781838590048|oclc=1091699582|date=2019-11-05|page=46|passage=This was to be my home for three months where ground officer cadets, referred to colloquially as ‘'''penguins'''’ were trained.}}
