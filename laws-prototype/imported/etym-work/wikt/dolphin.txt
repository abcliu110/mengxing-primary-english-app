===Etymology 1===
From {{inh|en|enm|delphyn}}, from {{der|en|la|delphīnus}}, from {{der|en|grc|δελφίς}}, from {{m|grc|δελφύς||womb}}; the modern form in ''-ol-'' is probably influenced by the pronunciation of {{cog|frm|dauphin}}. Compare {{cog|nl|dolfijn}} and {{cog|sv|delfin}}. {{doublet|en|dauphin}}. Displaced native {{ncog|ang|mereswīn|lit=sea pig}}, whence {{cog|en|mereswine}}.

# {{senseid|en|Q7369}} A [[carnivorous]] [[cetacean#Noun|cetacean]], typically with a [[beak]]-like [[snout]], famed for their intelligence and occasional willingness to approach humans.
#: {{syn|en|mereswine|sea goose|sea pig}}
#: {{coo|en|whale|porpoise}}
## {{taxfmt|Tursiops truncatus|species}}, ({{vern|Atlantic bottlenose dolphin}}) the most well-known species.
# A fish, the [[mahi-mahi]] or [[dorado]], {{taxfmt|Coryphaena hippurus|species}}, with a dorsal fin that runs the length of the body, also known for iridescent coloration.
# {{lb|en|heraldiccharge}} A [[depiction]] of a fish, with a broad indented [[fin]], usually [[embowed]].
# The [[dauphin]], eldest son of the kings of France.
# {{lb|en|history}} A mass of [[iron]] or [[lead]] hung from the [[yardarm]], in readiness to be dropped through the deck and the hull of an enemy's vessel to sink it.
# {{lb|en|nautical}} A kind of [[wreath]] or [[strap]] of [[plait]]ed [[cordage]].
# {{lb|en|nautical}} A [[spar]] or [[buoy]] held by an [[anchor]] and furnished with a [[ring]] to which ships may fasten their cables.<ref>'''1841''', {{w|Richard Henry Dana Jr.}}, ''The Seaman's Friend''</ref>
# {{lb|en|nautical}} A mooring post on a [[wharf]] or [[beach]]
#*{{quote-book|en|year=1842|author=John Timperley|title=s:An Account of the Harbour and Docks at Kingston-upon-Hull|text=there is a jetty or small pier with the necessary mooring posts, and two transport buoys a little to the south of the '''dolphins'''.}}.
# {{lb|en|nautical}} A permanent [[fender]] designed to protect a heavy boat, bridge, or coastal structure from the impact of large floating objects such as ice, floating logs, or vessels.
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1844| title=The Technical Educator, an encyclopædia |author=| page=107| passage=At each end of the piers in the water, in cases where several rows of pile are driven, a sort of cutwater should be formed, in order to ward off heavy bodies, such as floating trees, ice, etc. and prevent them from injuring the superstructure (called in German constructions, "Eisbrecher," or ice-breaker). This is usually done by driving one pile by itself in advance of the rest, or by forming what is called a "'''dolphin'''" at each end of the pier.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1852| title=An Account, with illustrations, of the Suspension Bridge across the River Danube, uniting Pesth with Buda and the adjacent country, in the Kingdom of Hungary, etc|author=William Tierney Clark| page=37| passage=An ice-breaker or '''dolphin''' was also constructed during the latter part of the autumn, a little above the site for No. 2 dam, or that for the twoer nearest the Pesth shore; this '''dolphin''', which served as a protection against the ice, rafts, &c. was constructed at this time, so that a means might be afforded of judging of the actual force of the ice during the winter ensuing; and the event proved that its construction was most fortunate, as the winter of 1840-41 was unusually severe.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1868| title=Seamanship|author=Stephen Bleecker Luce | page=28| passage=The '''Dolphin''' is now seldom used, but a rope treated in the same manner, with mousings raised on it at regular intervals, is sometimes put around a Launch, just below the gunwale outside and secured there as a sort of permanent fender; this also is called a '''Dolphin'''.}}
#* {{quote-book|en|year=1852| title=The Congressional Globe: Volume 32|author=| page=xxx| passage=For a '''dolphin''' or buoy to be placed on the south point of Goat Island, in the harbor of Newport, one hundred and fifty dollars.}}
#* {{quote-journal|en|date=March 31,1860|work=American Railroad Journal |title=Victoria Bridge|author=|volume=16|number=13|passage=Sometimes a stoppage would take place owing to the ''accumulation'' of ice between the '''Dolphin''' and the dam, which kept that above back until a mass of ice more resembling an island than anything else it can be compared to, would force the whole mass before it, breaking up the large blocks accumulated at the dam into a thousand pieces.}}
# {{lb|en|military|obsolete}} One of the [[handle]]s above the [[trunnion]]s by which a [[gun]] was lifted.
# {{lb|en|US|slang}} A person who buys shares on the primary market only to resell them immediately at a high profit.

===Etymology 2===
{{wp|+ (structure)}}
[[File:Dalben aus Holz Suederelbe.jpg|thumb|200 px|upright|Dolphin structures in Germany.]]
Ultimately from [[w:Fernando Álvarez de Toledo, 3rd Duke of Alba|3rd Duke of Alba]] ({{m|fr|duc-d'Albe}} in French), who was the first to build this type of structure in the Spanish Netherlands in the 16th century.  Possibly from {{bor|en|nl|dukdalf}}, or the plural {{m|nl|dukdalven}}, through elision of the initial ''duk-''.

====Noun====
{{en-noun}}

# {{lb|en|nautical}} A [[man-made]] semi submerged [[maritime]] structure, usually installed to provide a fixed structure for temporary mooring, to prevent ships from drifting to shallow water or to serve as base for navigational aids.
