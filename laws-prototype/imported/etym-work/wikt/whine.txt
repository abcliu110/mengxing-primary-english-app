==English==

===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*ḱwey-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|whynen}}, {{m|enm|hwinen}}, {{m|enm|whinen}}, from {{inh|en|ang|hwīnan|t=to rush, to whizz, to squeal, to whine}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*hwīnan}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*hwīnaną}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*ḱwey-|t=to hiss, whistle, whisper}}. Cognate with {{cog|non|hvína}}, whence {{cog|is|hvína}}, {{cog|nn|kvina}}, {{cog|sv|vina}}, and {{cog|da|hvine}}. Despite the strong similarity in sound and meaning, not related with {{noncog|de|weinen}}, {{noncog|nl|wenen}}, from {{m+|gem-pro|*wainōną}} (for which see dialectal {{m+|en|ween#Etymology 3|ween|to weep, lament}}).

===Verb===
{{en-verb}}

# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To utter a high-pitched cry.
# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To make a sound resembling such a cry.
#: {{ux|en|The jet engines '''whined''' at take off.}}
# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To complain or protest with a whine (compare [[whinge]]) or as if with a whine, i.e. in a [[peevish]] or self-pitying way.
#* {{quote-book|en|author=w:Catherine Jemmat|title=The Memoirs of Mrs. Catherine Jemmat, Daughter of the Late Admiral Yeo, of Plymouth. Written by Herself|edition=2nd|location=London|publisher=Printed for the author, at [[w:Charing Cross|Charing-Cross]]|year=1765|volume=I|page=145|pageurl=https://books.google.com/books?id=EHoEAAAAYAAJ&pg=RA1-PA145|oclc=316667080|passage=[S]he was one of your ſoft ſpoken, canting, '''whining''' hypocrites, who with a truly jeſuitical art, could wreſt evil out of the moſt inoffenſive thought, word, look or action; {{...}}}}
#* '''1936 Feb. 15''', {{w|Ernest Hemingway}}, [https://books.google.com.hk/books?id=XfPa5B2blv0C&pg=PA202 letter] to {{w|Maxwell Perkins}}:
#*: Feel awfully about {{w|F. Scott Fitzgerald|Scott}}... I always knew he couldn't think&mdash;he never could&mdash;but he had a marvelous talent and the thing is to use it&mdash;not '''whine''' in public.
# {{lb|en|intransitive}} To move with a whining sound.
#: {{ux|en|The jet '''whined''' into the air.}}
#: {{ux|en|The wind '''whined''' and moaned through the trees.}}
# {{lb|en|transitive}} To utter with the sound of a whine.
#: {{ux|en|The child '''whined''' all his complaints.}}
#: {{ux|en|Kelly Queen was '''whining''' that the boss made him put on his tie.}}

====Usage notes====
* The {{l|en|homophone|homophony}} of ''whine'' and ''whining'' with {{m|en|wine}} and {{m|en|wining}} for many speakers is sometimes a point of humor, as with ''[[cheese with that whine|would you like some cheese with your /waɪn/]]?'' or ''if you're going to be whining then I need to be wining''.
