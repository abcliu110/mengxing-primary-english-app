{{en-noun|~}}

# The condition of being held in a state between two or more [[forces]], which are acting in [[opposition]] to each other.
#: {{ux|en|My '''tensions''' with Eric over his alleged past actions have been fully resolved.}}
# A [[psychological]] state of being [[tense]].
# A [[feeling]] of nervousness, excitement, or fear that is created in a movie, book, etc.; [[suspense]].
#* {{quote-journal|en|year=1983|author={{w|Wendy Griswold}}|title=The Devil's Techniques: Cultural Legitimation and Social Change|volume=48|issue=5|work=American Sociological Review|url=https://www-jstor-org.wikipedialibrary.idm.oclc.org/stable/2094926|pages=668–680|page=670b|passage=This popular characterization of the Devil incorporates the Trickster archetype. Both are smart, energetic, lying, quick to take advantage of an opportunity. Both have latent narrative appeal in their generation of '''tension''' and conflict, their unpredictability, their defiance of hierarchy and authority.}}
# {{lb|en|physics|engineering}} The state of an elastic object which is stretched in a way which increases its length.
# {{lb|en|physics|engineering}} A force transmitted through a rope, string, cable, or similar object (used with prepositions ''on'', ''in'', or ''of'', e.g., "The tension in the cable is 1000 N", to convey that the same magnitude of force applies to objects attached to both ends).
#* {{quote-journal|en|date=1959 June|title=Clacton and Walton Electrification|journal=Railway Magazine|page=379|text=Most of the overhead system is of the weight-tensioned type, constant '''tension''' being automatically applied by balance weights.}}
# {{lb|en|physics|engineering}} [[voltage|Voltage]]. 

====Usage notes====
* Of the sixth sense, usually only the terms low tension, high tension, and extra-high tension, and the abbreviations [[LT]], [[HT]], and [[EHT]] are used. They are not precisely defined; LT is normally a few volts, HT a few hundreds of volts, and EHT thousands of volts.
