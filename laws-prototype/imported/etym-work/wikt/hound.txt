===Etymology 1===
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:hound|tree=1|text=++}} {{doublet|en|canine}}.

Cognate with {{cog|nl|hond||dog}}, {{cog|de|Hund||dog}}, {{cog|da,nb,nn,sv|hund||dog}}, {{cog|fo,is|hundur||dog}}.

In 14th-century England, ''hound'' was the general word for all domestic canines, and {{m|en|dog}} referred to a subtype resembling the modern mastiff and bulldog (much as the distinction between {{m|de|Hund}} and {{m|de|Dogge}} in contemporary German).<ref name="dog">[http://hrcak.srce.hr/index.php?show=clanak&id_clanak_jezik=48370&lang=en]</ref> By the 16th century, ''dog'' had become the general word, and ''hound'' had begun to refer only to breeds used for [[hunt]]ing.<ref>[https://books.google.com/?id=uZcs6poXkfEC&pg=PA215]</ref>
