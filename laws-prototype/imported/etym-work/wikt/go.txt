===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:gon|id=move|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|gon}}, {{m|enm|goon}}, from {{inh|en|ang|gān|t=to go}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*gān}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*gāną|t=to go}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*ǵʰeh₁-|t=to leave}}.

The inherited past tense form {{m|en|yode}} (compare {{cog|ang|ēode}}) was replaced through [[suppletion]] in the 15th century by {{m|en|went}}, from {{cog|ang|wendan|t=to go, depart, wend}}.

=====Usage notes=====
* Along with {{m|en|do}}, {{m|en|make}}, and to a lesser extent other English verbs, ''go'' is often used as a substitute for a verb that was used previously or that is implied, in the same way a [[pronoun]] substitutes for a [[noun]]. For example:
*: ''Chris: Then he '''goes''' like this:'' (Chris then waves his arms around, implying that the phrase means ''then he waves his arms'').
* Some speakers use {{m|en|went}} for the past participle, especially in informal contexts, though this is considered [[Appendix:Glossary#nonstandard|nonstandard]] and is [[Appendix:Glossary#proscribed|proscribed]].
* Like other English verbs, the verb ''go'' once had an alternative present participle formed with the suffix ''[[-and]]'', i.e. ''[[goand]]''. ''[[goand|Goand]]'' is now [[obsolete]], having been replaced by ''[[going]]'', except in a few rural dialects in Scotland and Northern England, where it is considered [[archaic]]. Even in such dialects, it is never used to form the continuous tenses. These examples are from the [[Highlands]]:
*: {{ux|inline=1|en|'''Goand''' [[snell]] [[athwart]] the [[houf]], hoo hent 'im be the [[swire|swyr]].|'''Going''' swiftly across the churchyard, she grabbed him by the neck.}}
*: {{ux|inline=1|en|'''Goand''' oot of the [[holt]], she saw a woundor baist.|'''Going''' out of the woods, she saw a magical creature.}}
* In certain contexts, ''been'' is idiomatically substituted for ''gone'' as the past participle. For example, one might ''go'' to London but later say that one has ''been'' to London. One might also say that one will ''go'' to the toilet but later say that one has ''been'' to the toilet. It is more common to say "I have never been sailing" than "I have never gone sailing", although the latter (while sometimes proscribed) is also used.

====Usage notes====
* In the meaning {{m-g|to}}, used with place names that do not start with the definite article. Place names that do start with the definite article use {{m|ga|go dtí}} instead. In a few fixed phrases, the archaic form {{m|ga|gos}} is used.
* The inflected forms that originally belonged to this preposition (that is, the prepositional pronouns meaning {{m-g|to me}} etc.) have been reanalyzed as the inflected forms of {{m|ga|chuig}}.
