===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*ǵneh₃-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|coude}}, {{m|enm|couthe}}, {{m|enm|cuthe}}, from {{inh|en|ang|cūþe}}, past indicative and past subjunctive form of {{m|ang|cunnan||to be able}} (compare related {{m|ang|cūþ}}, whence English {{m|en|couth}}).  Cognate with {{cog|de|konnte}}, {{cog|sv|kunde}}.

The ''-l-'' in the spelling was added in the early 16th century by analogy with {{m|en|should}} and {{m|en|would}}; this analogy formerly affected the pronunciation as well<ref name="Dobson">{{R:en:Dobson:1957|II|4|451|passage=''Could'': the orthoepists record four main pronunciations, {{IPAchar|[kʌʊld]}}, {{IPAchar|[kuːld]}}, {{IPAchar|[kud]}}, and {{IPAchar|[kʌd]}}.}}</ref> and was probably assisted by the tendency for {{IPAchar|/l/}} to be lost in those words (and so not written, leading to ''shudd'', ''wode'', etc).<ref>{{R:OED Online|entry=can|pos=v.1|id=26857|date=March 2017}}</ref><ref>Christopher Upward, George Davidson, ''The History of English Spelling'' (2011), section "Silent L"</ref>

====Usage notes====
* Some speakers and writers consider it wrong to use ''could'' to refer to permission. Such people favor replacing it with ''[[might]]'', just as they favor replacing ''can'' with ''[[may]]'' when referring to permission.
