===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*(s)ter-}}
The {{glossary|verb}} is derived from {{inh|en|enm|strouten}}, {{m|enm|struten|t=to bulge, swell; to protrude, stick out; to bluster, threaten; to object forcefully; to create a disturbance; to fight; to display one's clothes in a proud or vain manner}}{{nb...|strote, stroute, strouʒten, strowte, strut, strute|otherforms=1}},<ref>{{R:MED Online|entry=strǒuten|pos=v|id=MED43407}}</ref> from {{inh|en|ang|strūtian|t=to project out; stand out stiffly; to exert oneself, struggle}},<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''v.''{{sup|1}}|noformat=1|id=191974|date=1919}}</ref><ref name="Lexico v">{{R:Lexico|pos=v}}</ref> from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*strūtōną}}, {{m|gem-pro|*strūtijaną|t=to be puffed up, swell}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*streudʰ-|t=rigid, stiff}}, from {{m|ine-pro|*(s)ter-|t=firm; strong; rigid, stiff}}.

The English word is cognate with {{cog|da|strutte|t=to bulge, bristle}}, {{cog|nds|strutt|t=stiff}}, {{cog|gmh|striuzen|t=to bristle; to ruffle}} (modern {{cog|de|strotzen|t=to bristle up}}, {{m|de|sträußen}} {{qualifier|obsolete|except in [[w:Alemannic German|Alemannic]]}}); and compare {{cog|got|𐌸𐍂𐌿𐍄𐍃𐍆𐌹𐌻𐌻|t=leprosy}}, {{cog|non|þrútinn|t=swollen}}.

The {{glossary|noun}} is derived from the verb.<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''n.''{{sup|3}}|noformat=1|id=191971|date=1919}}</ref><ref name="Lexico n">{{R:Lexico|pos=n}}</ref> Noun sense 2 (“instrument for adjusting the pleats of a ruff”) appears to be due to a misreading of a 16th-century work which used the word ''stroout'' ({{m|en|strouted|t=caused (something) to bulge, protrude, or swell; strutted}}).<ref name="OED n2">{{R:OED Online|pos=''n.''{{sup|2}}|noformat=1|id=191970|date=1919}}</ref>

===Etymology 2===
[[File:Wing Brace strut.JPG|thumb|Struts [[support#Verb|supporting]] an [[airplane]]’s [[wing#Noun|wing]].]]

The origin of sense 1 of the {{glossary|noun}} (“beam or rod providing support”) is {{unknown|en|unknown}}; it is probably ultimately from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*strūtōną}}, {{m|gem-pro|*strūtijaną|t=to be puffed up, swell}}:<ref name="Lexico n"/><ref name="OED n2"/> see further at [[#Etymology 1|etymology 1]]. The English word is cognate with {{cog|is|strútur|t=hood jutting out like a horn}}, {{cog|nds|strutt|t=rigid, stiff}}, {{cog|no|strut|t=nozzle, spout}}, {{cog|sv|strut|t=paper cornet}}.

The {{glossary|verb}} is derived from sense 1 of the noun.<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''v.''{{sup|2}}|noformat=1|id=191975|date=1919}}</ref><ref name="Lexico v"/>

Sense 2 of the noun (“act of strutting”) is derived from the verb:<ref>{{R:OED Online|pos=''n.''{{sup|4}}|noformat=1|id=191972|date=1919}}</ref> see above.
