===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=piece|:inh|enm:chippe|tree=1}}
Noun from {{inh|en|enm|chip}}, {{m|enm|chippe}}, from {{inh|en|ang|ċipp|t=chip; small piece of wood, shaving}}, from {{der|en|ang|*ċippian||to cut; hew}}&nbsp;– attested in {{cog|ang|forċippian|t=to cut off}}&nbsp;–, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*kippōn|t=to cut; carve; hack; chop}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*kippōną|t=to chip, chop}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*ǵeyb-|t=to split; divide; germinate; sprout}}. Related to {{cog|nl|kip}}, {{m|nl|keep|t=notch; nick; score}}, {{cog|nl|kippen|t=to hatch}}, {{cog|nds-de|kippen|t=to cut; clip; trim; shorten}}, {{cog|de|kipfen|t=to chop off the tip; snip}}, {{cog|gmq-osw|kippa|t=to chop}}. Compare also {{m|en|chop}}.

The formally similar {{noncog|ang|ċipp}}, {{m|ang|ċypp}}, {{m|ang|ċyp|t=a beam; log; stock; post}}, from {{noncog|gem-pro|*kippaz|t=log; beam}} (whence {{noncog|osx|kip|t=post}}, {{noncog|goh|kipfa}}, {{m|goh|chipfa|t=axle, stave}}, {{noncog|non|keppr|t=cudgel, club}}) is a different, unrelated word either borrowed from {{noncog|la|cippus|t=stake; pale; post}} or borrowed from the same source language as the Latin.

Verb from {{inh|en|enm|chippen}}, from {{inh|en|ang|*ċippian|t=to cut; hew}}&nbsp;– attested in {{cog|ang|forċippian|t=to cut off}}&nbsp;– see above.

=====Usage notes=====
* In New Zealand and Australia, where the term ''chip(s)'' can refer to either French fried potatoes or deep-fried potato slices, the dishes are distinguished as "[[hot chips]]" (French fried potatoes) or, in New Zealand, "[[cold chips]]" (deep-fried potato slices) when clarity is needed.
* See notes at {{m|en|fry}} regarding the British usage of ''chips'' and ''fries''.
