==English==

===Etymology===
Recorded since 1580 in the sense “piece, hunk”. The word {{m|en|luncheon}} with the same meaning is presumably an extension on the pattern of {{m|en|puncheon|t=cask}} and {{m|en|truncheon|t=cudgel}}. But earliest found forms of {{m|en||luncheon}} include {{m|en|lunshin}} and {{m|en|lunching}}, which are equivalent to {{af|en||alt1=lunch|-ing}}, with the suffix {{m|en|-ing}} possibly later modified to imitate a French origin.

In contrast, the more common sense “light meal” is first attested for {{m|en||luncheon}} in 1652 and for {{m|en||lunch}} in 1829, so in this sense the latter is probably a shortening of the former.

''Lunch'' is possibly a derivative of {{m|en|lump}} (as {{m|en|hunch}} is from {{m|en|hump}}. See {{m|en|hunch}} for more), or represents an alteration of {{m|en|nuncheon}}, from {{der|en|enm|nonechenche|t=light midday meal}} (see {{l|en|nuncheon}}) and altered by northern English dialect {{m|en||lunch|t=hunk of bread or cheese}} (1590), which perhaps is from {{m|en|lump}} or from {{cog|es|lonja|t=a slice|lit=loin}}.<ref>{{R:OED Online|lunch|n.2|2063135329|September 2023}}</ref>
