===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=bicycle|:clip|bicycle|etydate=1882|tree=1|text=+}}
One explanation for the form with {{IPAchar|/k/}} is that {{m|en||bicycle}} was parsed to {{m|en||bi(cy)c(le)}}. An alternative explanation is that it was parsed to {{m|en||bic(ycle)}} but since speakers are aware of a general {{IPAchar|/k/}}~{{IPAchar|/s/}} alternation (as in {{m|en|[[electric]] ~ [[electricity]]}} etc.), the softened {{IPAchar|/s/}} was restored to a default {{IPAchar|/k/}} when the “ending” {{m|en||-ycle}} was dropped.<ref>''[http://www.jstor.org/stable/454976 An Etymological Brainteaser: The Shortening of Bicycle to Bike],'' Robert B. Hausmann, American Speech, Vol. 51, No. 3/4 (Autumn - Winter, 1976), pp. 272–274</ref> Similar cases are {{m|en|merc}} {{IPAchar|/mɜɹk/}}, {{m|en|spec}} {{IPAchar|/spɛk/}} for {{m|en|mercenary}}, {{m|en|specify}}. It seems unlikely, however, that this process is purely phonological and not at least partially based on the spelling ⟨c⟩.<!--If that were the case it should have the same likelihood of occurring in words spelt with <ss>-->

===Etymology 2===
From {{inh|en|enm|bike}}, {{m|enm|byke|t=a nest of wild bees or wasps; {{n-g|also,}} honeycomb}}, of {{unk|en|nocap=1}} origin. Perhaps a back-formation of {{cog|enm|*bykere|t=beekeeper}}, from {{inh|en|ang|bēocere|t=beekeeper}}; or from {{inh|en|ang|*bȳc}}, a byform of {{cog|ang|būc|t=belly; vessel; container}}. Compare also {{cog|sco|byke|t=beehive, anthill; home, dwelling}}, {{cog|non|bý|t=bee}}.
