===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:bileven|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|beleven}}, {{m|enm|bileven}}, from {{inh|en|ang|belīefan|t=to believe}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*bilaubijan|t=to believe}}, equivalent to {{af|en|be-|leave|t2=to give leave or permission to, permit, allow, grant}}. Cognate with {{cog|sco|beleve|t=to believe}}, {{cog|ofs|bilēva|t=to permit}}, {{cog|dum|beloven|t=to believe, entrust}}, {{cog|gml|belö̂ven|t=to believe}}, {{cog|gmh|belouben|t=to believe}}.

A related term in Old English was {{m|ang|ġelīefan|t=to be dear to; believe, trust}}, from {{cog|gmw-pro|*galaubijan|t=to have faith, believe}}, from {{cog|gem-pro|*galaubijaną}}. Compare also {{cog|ang|ġelēafa||belief, faith, confidence, trust}}, {{cog|ang|lēof}} {{gl|"dear, valued, beloved, pleasant, agreeable" > {{cog|en|lief}}}}. Related also to {{cog|frr|leauwjen|t=to believe}}, {{cog|stq|leeuwe|t=to believe}}, {{cog|fy|leauwe|t=to believe}}, {{cog|nl|geloven|t=to believe}}, {{cog|nds-de|glöven|t=to believe}}, {{cog|de|glauben|t=to believe}}, {{cog|got|𐌲𐌰𐌻𐌰𐌿𐌱𐌾𐌰𐌽||to hold dear, valuable, or satisfactory, approve of, believe}}.

The prepositionally transitive senses with {{m|en|in}} are a {{semantic loan|en|la|[[crēdō]] [[in]] [[aliquem]] / [[aliquid]]|nocap=1}}.

====Usage notes====
* The direct transitive sense and the prepositionally transitive sense are similar but can have very different implications.
** To “believe” someone or something means to accept specific pieces of information as truth: ''believe the news'', ''believe the lead witness''. To “believe a complete stranger” means to accept a stranger's story with little evidence.
** To “believe in” someone or something means to hold confidence and trust in that person or concept: ''believe in liberty'', ''believe in God''. To “believe in one's fellow man” means to place trust and confidence in mankind.
* Meanings sometimes overlap. To ''believe in'' a religious text would also require affirming the truth of at least the major tenets. To ''believe'' a religious text might likewise imply placing one's confidence and trust in it, in addition to accepting its statements as facts.
* This is generally a [[stative verb]] that rarely takes the continuous inflection. See [[:Category:English stative verbs]]
