===Etymology===
{{etydate|1826}}. From {{m|en|holla}}, {{m|en|hollo}} (attested 1588). This variant of {{m|en|hallo}} is often credited to {{w|Thomas Edison}} as a coinage for telephone use, but its appearance in print predates the invention of the telephone by several decades.

Ultimately from a variant of {{der|en|ang|ēalā}}, such as {{m|ang|hēlā}}, which was used colloquially at the time similarly to how ''hey'' and (in some dialects) ''hi'' are used nowadays. Thus, equivalent to a compound of {{m|en|hey}} and {{m|en|lo}}. Used when drawing attention to yourself.

Possibly influenced by {{ncog|osx|halo}}, {{lg|imperative}} of {{m|osx|halōn|t=to call, fetch}}, used in hailing a ferryman, akin to {{cog|goh|hala}}, {{m|goh|hola|hola!}}, imperative forms of {{m|goh|halōn}}, {{m|goh|holōn|t=to fetch}}. For more information, see {{l|en|hallo}}.

The ''{{w|Oxford English Dictionary}}'' and ''{{w|Merriam-Webster Dictionary}}'' also suggested that it is a variant of {{m|en|holla}}, a variant of {{m|en|holloo}}. Further beyond, the origin remains uncertain. OED and Merriam-Webster suggested that it has a connection between {{m|en|hallow|t=to shout, to cry out loud}}, which came from Old French {{m|fro|holloer}}, which, according to ''Altfranzösisches Wörterbuch'', is from {{cog|osx|halōn}}.

====Usage notes====
The greeting {{m|en|hello}} is among the most universal and neutral in use. It may be heard in nearly all social situations and nearly all walks of life and is unlikely to offend.

In the derived senses, the word-final stress may be emphasized to focus attention, but word-initial stress is also heard in certain contexts, such as some uses of {{m|en|hello there}}, and even in isolation.
