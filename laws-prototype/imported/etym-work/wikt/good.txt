=====Usage notes=====

=====Usage notes=====
The Germanic cognates (listed in the [[#Etymology_2|Etymology]]) illustrate a [[null morpheme]] difference from the adjective that is fully {{glossary|standard}} in those languages, as it would be in modern English [[if not for]] a widespread {{glossary|prescriptive}} misapprehension. The idea that adverbs in English cannot or should not have a [[null morpheme]] difference from the adjective is a widespread notion that bars ''good'' ([[adverb#Noun|adv]]) and ''[[bad#Adverb|bad]]'' (adv) from {{glossary|formal}} use even though they are [[ubiquitous]] in {{glossary|informal}} use. It also sometimes drives people to inject ''[[-ly#Suffix|-ly]]'' [[hypercorrection|hypercorrectively]]; for example, ''[[thusly]]'' is stated by most usage guides to be a needless replacement of ''[[thus#Adverb|thus]]''. More in Wikipedia at ''[[w:flat adverb|flat adverb]]''.
