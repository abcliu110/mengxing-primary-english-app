The sense {{m|en|maize}} ({{taxfmt|Zea mays|species}}) is an {{ellipsis|en|Indian corn|nocap=1}} that developed in 18th century North America.<ref>{{cite-book|en|year=1921|location=New York|publisher={{w|Alfred A. Knopf}}|author=w:H. L. Mencken|title=w:The American Language|page=63|pageurl=https://archive.org/details/americanlanguag00mencgoog/page/63/mode/1up|passage=The earliest settlers, following this usage, gave the name of ''Indian corn'' to what the Spaniards{{...}} had called ''maíz''. {{...}} But gradually the adjective fell off, and by the middle of the eighteenth century ''maize'' was called simply ''corn'' and grains in general were called ''breadstuffs''.}}</ref>
