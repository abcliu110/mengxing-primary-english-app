===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:werre<id:war>|tree=1}}
{{inh+|en|enm|werre}}, from Late {{inh|en|ang|werre//wyrre||armed conflict}}, from {{der|en|xno|-}} and {{der|en|fro-nor|guerre//werre}} (compare modern {{cog|fr|guerre}}), from {{der|en|la-med|werra}}, from {{der|en|frk|*werʀu|t=confusion; quarrel}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*wers-|t=to mix up, confuse, beat, thresh}}. Gradually displaced native {{ncog|ang|beadu}}, {{m|ang|hild}}, {{m|ang|ġewinn}}, {{m|ang|orleġe}}, {{m|ang|wīġ}}, and many others as the general term for "war" during the Middle English period.

Related to {{cog|goh|werra|t=confusion, strife, quarrel}} and {{cog|de|verwirren|t=to confuse}}, but not to {{m|de|Wehr|t=defense}}. Also related to {{cog|osx|werran|t=to confuse, perplex}}, {{cog|nl|war|t=confusion, disarray}}, {{cog|fy|war|t=struggle, resistance, defense, effort, exertion, activity}}, and Romance terms borrowed from Germanic, such as {{cog|it,pt,es|guerra|t=war}}. Not likely related to {{m|en|worse}} or {{m|en|wurst}}.
