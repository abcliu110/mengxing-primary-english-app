===Etymology 1===
From [[w:Early Modern English|Early Modern English]] {{m|en|dadd}}, {{m|en|dadde}} {{gl|{{circa2|1500}}}}, undoubtedly older, from unrecorded {{inh|en|enm|*dadde}}, of {{unc|en|nocap=1}} ultimate origin. Compare {{cog|fy|deite|t=dad, daddy}}, {{cog|swg|Dede|t=Godfather}}.

* Perhaps of {{der|en|cel}} origin, compare {{cog|cy|-}} and {{cog|br|tad}} (from {{cog|cel-bry-pro|*tad}}), {{cog|sga|data}}; and possibly related to {{cog|ru|дя́дя||uncle}} and/or {{cog|ru|де́душка||grandfather}}, all imitative. In Welsh, when subject to [[Appendix:Welsh_mutations|soft mutation]] (which occurs in [[vocative]] contexts, among others), ''tad'' becomes ''dad''.
* Perhaps {{onomatopoeic|en|title=imitative}} of a child's first uttered syllables {{m|und||da}}, {{m|und||da}}.<ref>{{R:New English Dictionary}}</ref>
* Possibly from a metathetic variation of a hypothetical {{cog|ang||*ætta}}, {{m|ang||*atta|t=father}}, from {{cog|gmw-pro|*attō}}, from {{cog|gem-pro|*attô}} {{gloss|"father, forefather"; whence also {{cog|frr|ate}}, {{m|frr|aatj}}, {{m|frr|taatje}}, {{m|frr|tääte|t=father; dad}}, {{cog|gmh|tate|t=father, dad}} (whence {{cog|de|Tate|t=dad}}, {{cog|bar|tatte|t=dad}}, {{cog|cim|tatta|t=dad}}), {{cog|is|táta|t=dad}}}}, from {{cog|ine-pro|*átta|t=father}}, whence {{cog|sa|तत|t=father}}.
