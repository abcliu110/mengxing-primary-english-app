==English==

===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*h₂ey-|id1=life|*kʷ-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|either}}, from {{inh|en|ang|ǣġhwæþer}}, from {{der|en|gmw-pro|*aiw}} + {{m|gmw-pro|*gahwaþar}}. Akin to {{cog|osx|eogihwethar}}, {{m|osx|iahwethar}} ({{cog|nds|jeed}}); {{cog|odt|*iogewether}}, {{m|odt|*iowether}}, {{m|odt|*iother}} ({{cog|nl|ieder}}); {{cog|goh|eogihwedar}}, {{m|goh|iegihweder}}, {{m|goh|ieweder}} ({{cog|de|jeder}}).

The pronunciations with {{IPAfont|/iː/}} and {{IPAchar|/eɪ/}} respectively reflect Middle English forms in {{IPAchar|/ɛː/}} and {{IPAchar|/æi/}}; the origin of that with {{IPAchar|/aɪ/}} is less clear, but it may reflect a Middle English form with {{IPAchar|/iː/}}; see {{m+|enm|either}} for more discussion.

===Alternative forms===

===Etymology===
From {{inh|enm|ang|ǣġþer}}, {{m|ang|ǣðer}}, a contraction of {{m|ang|ǣġhwæþer}}.

{{col-top|2|Notes on phonological development}}
The form with {{IPAfont|/æi̯/}} represents the regular development of the Old English sequence {{IPAchar|/æːj/}} in {{m|ang|ǣġþer}}. The monophthongal form with {{IPAfont|/ɛː/}} may reflect a development of the {{IPAchar|/æː/}} resulting from a West Saxon Old English tendency for the loss of {{IPAchar|/j/}} between a vowel and a dental consonant (compare {{m|ang|onġēan}}), a development of an {{m|ang||*ǣhþer}} (see below), or a restressing of an unstressed form with {{IPAchar|/ɛ/}}. 

While no unambiguous representation of such a pronunciation is found before John Jones (1701), the most immediately parsimonious explanation of modern English pronunciations of {{m|en|either}} with {{IPAchar|/aɪ/}} is that they descend from a Middle English {{IPAchar|/iː/}}. However, the source of this form is not immediately obvious; it may reflect {{m+|ang||*ǣhþer}} ({{IPAchar|/ˈæːx.θer/}}), a divergent simplification of {{m|ang|ǣġhwæþer}}, where {{IPAchar|/æː/}} was regularly raised to {{IPAchar|/eː/}} then diphthongised to {{IPAchar|/ei̯/}} before {{IPAchar|/x/}} (as in e.g. {{m|enm|heigh}}); later, {{IPAchar|/x/}} was lost and {{IPAchar|/ei̯/}} developed to {{IPAchar|/iː/}} (which then would develop to Modern English {{IPAchar|/aɪ/}}). Another possibility is that it reflects {{m+|ang-ken||*ēġþer}}, where {{IPAchar|/eːj/}} would regularly develop into {{IPAchar|/ei/}}. Furthermore, the form with {{IPAfont|/ɛː/}} may also reflect a collateral development of {{m|ang||*ǣhþer}} where {{IPAchar|/x/}} was lost before the raising of {{IPAchar|/æː/}} to {{IPAchar|/eː/}} before it could occur.

An alternative possibility is that no Middle English form with {{IPAchar|/iː/}} existed; in that case, the modern form with {{IPAchar|/aɪ/}} would represent a reflex of Middle English {{IPAchar|/æi/}}. Viëtor posits a special development of that sound an open syllable (compare a potential parallel in {{m|en|Reigate}}), while another possibility is a borrowing of a dialectal form with that vowel into standard English, where it was identified with the reflex of {{IPAchar|/iː/}} as that of {{IPAchar|/æi/}} was monophthongal ({{IPAchar|[ɛː]}} or {{IPAchar|[eː]}}) at that point.
