===Etymology===
{{wp}}
[[File:Pineapple 'Victoria' with slice.jpg|thumb|A pineapple with a slice]]
[[File:F-1 french grenade.jpg|thumb|100px|A pineapple-style hand grenade]]
From {{inh|en|enm|pinappel||pinecone|lit=pine-apple/pine-fruit}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*pīnapplu}}. Later applied to the fruit of the pineapple plant due to its resemblance to a [[pinecone]]. Compare the {{cog|dum|-}} and {{cog|nl|pijnappel|t=pinecone", formerly also "pineapple}}, {{cog|af|pynappel|t=pineapple}}, {{cog|gml|pinappel}}, {{cog|goh|pīnapful}}, {{cog|gmh|pīnaphel}}, and early Modern {{cog|de|pinapfel}} — all in the sense of “pine cone”. Compare also the post-Classical {{cog|la|pomum pini}}, the {{cog|fro|pume de pin}}, the {{cog|frm|-}} and {{cog|fr|pomme de pin}} and {{cog|es|piña}}. {{surf|+com|en|pine|apple}}.

Sense "An Australian fifty dollar note": From the yellow colour of a $50 banknote in the first, second, and third series of rice [[w:Banknotes of the Australian dollar|Australian dollar notes]]. Compare with [[lobster]] for a $20 Australian banknote, and [[watermelon]] for a $100 Australian banknote.
