From {{inh|en|enm|flour}}, from {{der|en|xno|flur}}, from {{der|en|la|flōrem}}, accusative of {{m|la|flōs}}, from {{der|en|itc-pro|*flōs}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*bʰleh₃-||to thrive, bloom}}. {{doublet|en|fleur|flor|flour|bloom|blossom}}.

=====Usage notes=====
* In its most common sense as "a colorful conspicuous structure", the word ''flower'' includes many structures which are not anatomically flowers in the botanical sense. In sunflowers and daisies, for example, what is considered to be a single flower in the colloquial sense is, anatomically, a cluster of many small flowers (a {{m|en|capitulum}}, a form of {{m|en|pseudanthium}} and {{m|en|inflorescence}}). Similarly, in poinsettias, the colorful structures that are colloquially considered its flowers are actually made up of {{m|en|bract}}s, or modified leaves, with the anatomical flowers being small, inconspicuous green structures at the center. Conversely, the botanical definition of ''flower'' includes many structures that would not be considered a flower by the average person, such as the [[catkin]]s of a willow tree or the downy flowers found atop a [[cattail]] stalk.
