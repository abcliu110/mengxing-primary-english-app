=====Usage notes=====
In the sense of "as a", it is a [[fossil word]] <small>(Standard English only)</small>, found usually only in obsolete set phrases like: "''to take a woman '''to''' wife''", "''to have someone '''to''' friend''", "''to have something '''to''' birthright''" etc. In northern dialects, {{rfclarify|en}} where it is rare but still in common use, it is often used in combination with {{m|en|with}}.

===Etymology 1===
{{PIE word|nb|dwóh₁}}
{{inh+|nb|non|tvá}}, the masculine accusative of {{m|non|tveir||two}}, from {{inh|nb|gem-pro|*twai}}, ultimately from {{inh|nb|ine-pro|*dwóh₁||two}}. The modern form continues an inflected form of the Old Norse numeral: its masculine nominative was {{m|non|tveir}}, its feminine nominative and accusative {{m|non|tvær}}, and its neuter nominative and accusative {{m|non|tvau}}. Modern Bokmål {{m|nb|to}} is indeclinable.

Cognate with {{cog|nn|to}}, {{cog|da|to}}, {{cog|sv|två}}, {{cog|is|tveir}}, {{cog|nl|twee}}, {{cog|de|zwei}} and {{cog|en|two}}.

===Etymology 2===
[[File:Flax fibers J1.jpg|thumb|Flax fibres before spinning.]]
{{inh+|nb|non|tó||wool, flax; material for spinning}}, from {{inh|nb|gem-pro|-}}. The word belongs to the family represented by {{m|gem-pro|*tawwą||rope}}. Older etymological accounts distinguish a stem ''*tōwa-'' behind the Norse form from an [[ablaut]] variant ''*tauwa-'' represented by {{cog|nl|touw||rope}}.

The further origin is uncertain. Kroonen tentatively derives the Germanic rope word from {{m|ine-pro|*deh₁-||to bind, tie}}, with a suffix in ''-w-''. Another analysis connects the family with {{m|gem-pro|*tawjaną||to make, prepare}}, possibly from {{m|ine-pro|*dewh₂-||to join, bind}}; this would also relate it to {{cog|en|taw}} and {{cog|en|tool}}. The figurative senses compare a person's qualities or capabilities to the material of which they are made.

Cognate with {{cog|nn|to}}, {{cog|is|tó||wool}}, dialectal {{cog|sv|to||spinning material}}, {{cog|nl|touw||rope}}, {{cog|fy|tou||rope}} and {{cog|en|tow}}.

===Etymology 3===
[[File:Scrambling in Romanian Carpathians.jpg|thumb|A grassy ledge between rock faces in the Bucegi Mountains, Romania.]]
{{inh+|nb|non|tó||grassy ledge on a mountainside}}. The further origin is uncertain. Torp tentatively reconstructs Proto-Germanic {{m|gem-pro|*tanhwō}} and compares {{m|non|tá||hard-trodden ground in front of a house; enclosed path}} and the family of {{m|gem-pro|*tanhuz||tough; tenacious}}, suggesting an original meaning of “firm ground”.

Cognate with {{cog|nn|to}}, {{cog|fo|tó}}, {{cog|is|tó}} and dialectal {{cog|sv|to||hollow between hills}}. If the proposed Germanic connection is correct, also more distantly related to {{cog|nl|taai}}, {{cog|de|zäh}} and {{cog|en|tough}}.
