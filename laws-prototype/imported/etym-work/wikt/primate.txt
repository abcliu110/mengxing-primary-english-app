===Etymology 1===
{{etydate|1876}}. From {{bor|en|fr|primate}}, from the {{glossary|substantivization}} of  {{der|en|la|prīmās|prīmās, prīmātis|one of the first, chief, excellent, noble}}, same as [[#Etymology 2|Etymology 2]]. So named due to the belief that primates are the highest order of mammals or animals.

====Noun====
{{en-noun}}

# {{senseid|en|mammal}} {{lb|en|zoology}} Any of the order {{taxfmt|Primates|order}} of [[mammal]]s characterized by relatively large brains, grasping [[hand]]s or [[feet]], and forward-facing [[eye]]s, including [[ape]]s, [[monkey]]s, [[lemur]]s, [[tarsier]]s, [[loris]]es, [[galago]]s, etc.
#: {{ux|en|'''Primates''' range from lemurs to gorillas.}}
# {{rfv-sense|en}} {{lb|en|informal}} An [[anthropoid]]; an [[ape]], including [[human]]s.

====Noun====
{{en-noun}}

# {{senseid|en|Catholic}}{{lb|en|Christianity}} In the [[Catholic Church]], a rare title conferred to or claimed by the [[see]]s of certain [[archbishop]]s, or the highest-ranking [[bishop]] of a present or historical, usually political circumscription.
#: {{ux|en|The Archbishop of Quebec is the '''primate''' of Canada.}}
# {{senseid|en|Orthodox}} {{lb|en|Christianity}} In the [[Orthodox Church]], the presiding bishop of an ecclesiastical jurisdiction or region. Usually, the expression ''primate'' refers to the first [[hierarch]] of an [[autocephalous]] or [[autonomous]] Orthodox church. Less often, it is used to refer to the ruling bishop of an archdiocese or diocese.
# {{senseid|en|Anglican}} {{lb|en|Christianity}} In the [[Anglican Church]], an [[archbishop]], or the highest-ranking [[bishop]] of an ecclesiastic [[province]].

=====Usage notes=====
* To disambiguate from sense 1, which is always pronounced /'pɹaɪmeɪt/, sense 2 is often pronounced /ˈpɹaɪmət/ (rhymes with "climate"), but it can also be pronounced the same as sense 1.
