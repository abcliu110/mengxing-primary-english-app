==English==

===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|alredy|t=fully; already}}, equivalent to {{af|en|al-#Etymology_1|t1=all, completely|ready}}. Cognate with {{cog|fy|alreeds|t=already}}, {{cog|nl|alreeds|t=already}}, {{cog|af|alreeds|t=already}}, {{cog|gml|alreide}}, {{m|gml|alreids}} {{gl|"already"; whence modern {{cog|nds-de|alreeds|t=already}}}}, {{cog|da|allerede|t=already}}, {{cog|sv|allaredan|t=already}}, {{cog|nn|allereie|t=already}}. More at {{m|en|all}}, {{m|en|ready}}.

The use as an intensifier in American English is a {{semantic loan|en|yi|שוין|nocap=1}}, attested from 1903.

In Singapore English, the use of ''already'' as a marker of action completion and change of state is analogous to {{sl|en|nan-hbl|-|notext=1}} {{zh-l|了|tr=liáu}}, {{sl|en|nan-tws|-|notext=1}} {{zh-l|了|tr=liao{{sup|2}}}} and {{sl|en|cmn|了|tr=le|notext=1}}. Compare {{ncog|ms|sudah|(su)dah}} and {{ncog|yue|咗|tr=zo<sup>2</sup>}}, {{zh-m|喇|tr=laa<sup>3</sup>}}.

====Usage notes====
* '''Already''' may be used with the [[present perfect]] (''I have already done that''), the [[past perfect]] (''I had already done it by then''), the [[future perfect]] (''When you arrive, the business will already have been completed'') or the [[simple future]] (''When you arrive, the business will already be complete'').
<!-- material copied (and since modified) from Webster's Revised Unabridged Dictionary (1913) -->
* "'''Already'''" and "all ready" do not mean the same thing. The two-word term can be used to mean "fully prepared."
* {{q|Singapore English}}: See {{m|en|liao#Usage notes}}.
