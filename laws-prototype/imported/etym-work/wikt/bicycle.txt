===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:bor|fr:bicycle|:afeq|bi-|cycle<id:cicle>|tree=1}}
{{root|en|ine-pro|*kʷel-}}
Borrowed from {{bor|en|fr|bicycle}} (modern {{m|fr|bicyclette}}), from {{m|fr|bi-|t=two}} + {{m|fr|cycle|t=cycle}}. {{surf|en|bi-|cycle}}. First attested in English in 1868, and in French in 1847.

{{sense|promiscuous woman}} From double meaning of {{m|en|ride}} ("to transport oneself upon" vs. "to mount someone to have sex with them"). A communal bicycle would have many riders.
