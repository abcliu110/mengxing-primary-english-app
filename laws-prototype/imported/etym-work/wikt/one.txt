===Etymology 1===
{{PIE word|en|h₁óynos}}
{{etymon|en|id=num|:inh|enm:on<id:one>|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|oon}}, {{m|enm|on}}, {{m|enm|oan}}, {{m|enm|an}}, from {{inh|en|ang|ān|t=one}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*ain}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*ainaz|t=one}}, from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*h₁óynos|t=single, one}}. {{doublet|en|a|an|Uno}}.

Cognate with {{cog|sco|ae}}, {{m|sco|ane}}, {{m|sco|wan}}, {{m|sco|yin|t=one}}; {{cog|frr|ån|t=one}}; {{cog|stq|aan|t=one}}; {{cog|fy|ien||one}}; {{cog|nl|een}}, {{m|nl|één|t=one}}; {{cog|nds-de|een}}; {{cog|de|ein}}, {{m|de|eins|t=one}}; {{cog|da|en|t=one}}; {{cog|sv|en|t=one}}; {{cog|nn|ein|t=one}}, {{cog|is|einn|t=one}}; {{cog|la|ūnus|t=one}} ({{cog|itc-ola|oinos}}); {{cog|ru|оди́н}}, {{cog|es|uno}}. {{false cognate|ml|ഒന്ന്}}, {{ncog|ta|ஒன்னு}}, {{m|ta|ஒண்ணு}}, {{m|ta|ஒன்று}}.

The use as an indefinite personal pronoun may have been influenced by unrelated {{ncog|fr|on}},<ref name="Etymonline"/> although the Germanic languages widely use cognates for the same sense (usually in non-subject function, but also in subject function, e.g. {{m+|lb|een}}).

Verb form from {{inh|en|enm|onen}}.

====Pronunciation====
Around the 14th century, in southwest and western England, the word began to be pronounced with an initial {{IPAchar|/w/}}<ref name="Etymonline">{{R:Etymonline}}</ref><ref name="Century">{{R:Century|atone}}</ref> (compare e.g. {{m|en|woak}}, {{m+|enm|wocke}}, a dialectal form of {{m|en|oak}}),<ref>Christopher Upward, George Davidson, ''The History of English Spelling'' (2011), section "O"</ref> and the spellings {{m|enm|won}} and {{m|enm|wone}} began to be found alongside {{m|enm|on}}, {{m|enm|one}};<ref>''[//quod.lib.umich.edu/m/middle-english-dictionary/dictionary/MED30547 Middle English Dictionary]'': "ō̆n"</ref> the {{IPAchar|/w/}}, though initially nonstandard, had become the norm by the 18th century.<ref name="Etymonline"/> In {{m|en|alone}}, {{m|en|atone}}, and {{m|en|only}},<ref name="Century"/> as well as in the dialectal form {{m|en|'un|un, 'un}}<ref name="Etymonline"/> (and in {{m|en|none}} and {{m|en|no}}),<ref>Oliver Farrer Emerson, ''the History of the English Language'' (1921), page 314</ref> the older pronunciations without {{IPAchar|/w/}} are preserved,<ref name="Etymonline"/><ref name="Century"/> while {{m|en|once}} shows the same {{IPAchar|/w/}}.
