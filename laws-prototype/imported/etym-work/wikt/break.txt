===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=separate|:inh|enm:breken|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|breken}}, from {{inh|en|ang|brecan||to break}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*brekan}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*brekaną||to break}}, from {{inh|en|ine-pro|*bʰreg-||to break}}. {{doublet|en|bray}}.
{{col-top|2|cog}}
Cognates of Germanic origin include {{cog|sco|brek||to break}}, {{cog|fy|brekke||to break}}, {{cog|nl|breken||to break}}, {{cog|nds|breken||to break}}, {{cog|de|brechen||to break}}, {{cog|fr|broyer||to crush, grind}}, {{cog|got|𐌱𐍂𐌹𐌺𐌰𐌽|t=to break, destroy}}, {{cog|no|brek||desire, yearning}}.

Also cognate with {{cog|sq|brishtë||fragile}}, {{cog|la|frangō||break, break up, shatter|pos=verb}}, whence {{cog|en|fracture}} and other terms – {{m|en|fragile}}, {{m|en|frail}}, {{m|en|fraction}}, and {{m|en|fragment}}.
{{col-bottom}}

The modern pronunciation shows an irregular change of Early Modern English {{IPAchar|/ɛː/}} to {{IPAchar|/eɪ/}} in the standard language (present also in {{m|en|steak}}); contrast this with the development of other words such as {{m|en|speak}} and {{m|en|wreak}}.
