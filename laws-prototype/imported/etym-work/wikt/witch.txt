===Etymology 1===
{{wp|+ (word)}}
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:wicche|tree=1}}
{{root|en|ine-pro|*weyk-|id=separate}}

The noun is from {{inh|en|enm|wicche}}, from {{inh|en|ang|wiċċe|t=(female) witch; sorceress}} and {{m|ang|wiċċa|t=(male) witch; sorcerer; warlock}}, deverbative from {{m|ang|wiċċian|t=to practice sorcery}}, from {{der|en|gem-pro|*wikkōną}} (compare {{cog|fy|wikje}}, {{m|fy|wikke|t=to foretell; to warn}}, {{cog|nds-de|wicken|t=to soothsay}}, {{cog|nl|wikken}}, {{m|nl|wichelen|t=to dowse; to divine}}), from {{der|en|ine-pro|*wik-néh₂-}}, derivation of {{m|ine-pro|*weyk-|t=to consecrate; to separate}};<ref>Guus Kroonen, ''Etymological Dictionary of Proto-Germanic'' (Leiden: Brill, 2013), 586.</ref> akin to {{cog|la|victima|t=sacrificial victim}}, {{cog|lt|viekas|viẽkas|t=life-force}}, {{cog|sa|वि॒नक्ति॑|t=to set apart; to separate out}}. Possibly related to {{m|en|wicked}}; see that entry for more. The verb derives from the noun.<ref>{{R:OED2|part of speech=''v.''{{sup|2}}|noformat=1|id=00286238|passage=f. {{smallcaps|witch}} ''n.''{{sup|3}}}}</ref>

The mathematical curve derives from a (mis)translation by {{w|John Colson}} of {{der|en|it|versiera}} (the term Italian mathematicians used for such curves), which meant "rope" (from {{m+|la|versōria|t=rope to adjust a sail}}), but which is spelled the same as {{m|it|versiera||witch}} (from {{m|it|avversaria}}).
