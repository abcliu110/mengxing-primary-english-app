===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=open|:inh|enm:keye<id:open>|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|keye}}, {{m|enm|kaye}}, {{m|enm|keiȝe}}, from {{inh|en|ang|cǣġ||key, solution, experiment}} (whence also {{cog|sco|key}} and {{m|sco|kay||key}}), from [[w:Anglo-Frisian languages|Anglo-Frisian]] {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*kaiju}}, of uncertain origin.<ref>{{cite-web |titleurl=https://en.oxforddictionaries.com/definition/key |title=Archived copy |accessdate=19 March 2018 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20190525150410/https://en.oxforddictionaries.com/definition/key |archivedate=25 May 2019 }}</ref> The only sure cognates are {{cog|stq|Koai|t=key}}, {{cog|fy|kaai|t=key}}, and {{cog|frr|kai}}, {{m|frr|koie|t=key}}. Possibly from {{der|en|gem-pro|*kēgaz}}, {{m|gem-pro|*kēguz||stake, post, pole}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*ǵogʰ-}}, {{m|ine-pro|*ǵegʰ-}}, {{m|ine-pro|*ǵegʰn-||branch, stake, bush}}, which would make it cognate with {{cog|gml|kāk||whipping post, pillory}}, and perhaps to {{cog|dum|keige||javelin, spear}} and {{cog|gml|keie}}, {{m|gml|keige||spear}}.

For the semantic development, note that medieval keys were simply long poles (ending in a hook) with which a crossbar obstructing a door from the inside could be removed from the outside, by lifting it through a hole in the door. Liberman has noted, however, "The original meaning of *kaig-jo- was presumably '*pin with a twisted end.' Words with the root *kai- followed by a consonant meaning 'crooked, bent; twisted' are common only in the North Germanic languages."<ref>{{R:Etymonline}}</ref>
