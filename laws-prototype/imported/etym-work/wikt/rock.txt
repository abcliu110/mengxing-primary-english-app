===Etymology 1===
{{etymon|en|id=stone|:inh|enm:rokke|tree=1}}
{{inh+|en|enm|rocke}}, {{m|enm|rokke|t=rock formation}}, from {{inh|en|ang|*rocc|t=rock}}, as in {{cog|ang|stānrocc|t=high stone rock, peak, obelisk}}, and also later from {{der|en|xno|roche|roque}}, (compare Modern {{cog|fr|roc}}, {{m|fr|roche}}, {{m|fr|rocher}}), from {{der|en|la-med|rocca}} (attested 767), of {{unc|en|nocap=1}} origin, sometimes said to be of {{der|en|cel}} (in particular, perhaps {{der|en|cel-gau|-}}) origin (compare {{cog|br|roc'h}}).<ref>{{R:Etymonline|rock}}</ref> Related also to {{cog|gml|rocke|t=rock ledge}}. Diez suggests that it derives from Vulgar Latin *''rupica'', from {{m|la|rūpēs}}, but this is formally impossible since /o/ reflects a short /u/.<ref>https://www.etymonline.com/word/rock</ref>

===Etymology 3===
{{ety|en|id=genre of music|:ellipsis|rock and roll}}
Shortened from {{m|en|rock and roll}}. Since the meaning of {{m|en||rock}} has adapted to mean a simpler, more modern, [[metal]]-like genre, {{m|en||rock and roll}} has generally been left referring to earlier forms such as that originating in the 1950s, notably more [[swing]]-oriented style.

====Noun====
{{en-noun|-}}

# {{senseid|en|Q11399}}{{lb|en|music genre}} A style of music characterized by basic drum-beat, generally [[4/4]] [[riff]]s, based on (usually electric) guitar, [[bass guitar]], drums, keyboards (often), and vocals.
