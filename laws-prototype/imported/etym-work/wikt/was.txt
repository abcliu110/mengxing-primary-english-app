===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:inh|enm:was|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|was}}, from {{inh|en|ang|wæs}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*was}}, from {{inh|en|ine-pro||*h₂we-h₂wós-e}} from {{m|ine-pro|*h₂wes-||to reside}}, whence also {{m|en|vestal}}.

See also {{cog|sco|was}}, {{cog|fy|was}} (dated, ''wie'' is generally preferred today), {{cog|nl|was}}, {{cog|nds|was}}, {{cog|de|war}}, {{cog|sv|var}}); also {{cog|bsh|vos-}}, {{cog|sa|उवास|tr=uvā́sa}}.

The paradigm of “to be” has been since the time of Proto-Germanic a synthesis of three originally distinct verb stems. The infinitive form {{m|en|be}} and its derived forms {{m|en|been}} and {{m|en|being}} are from {{der|en|ine-pro|*bʰuH-||to become}}. The present-tense indicative forms {{m|en|am}}, {{m|en|is}}, and {{m|en|are}} are both derived from {{der|en|ine-pro|*h₁es-||to be}}. Lastly, the past forms starting with ''w-'' such as ''was'' and {{m|en|were}} are from {{der|en|ine-pro|*h₂wes-||to reside}}.

====Usage notes====
* ''Was'' is colloquially used with prepositions, chiefly but not exclusively in southern regions. Otherwise it is generally replaced with a pronominal adverb containing {{m|de|wo-}} (or in a few cases {{m|de|wes-}}). Hence: {{l|de||'''''Womit''' hast du das gemacht?''|'''With what''' did you do that?}}, instead of '''''Mit was''' hast du das gemacht?'', and {{m|de|weswegen}} instead of ''wegen was''.
* The genitive case, and the dative case if necessary for clearness, can be paraphrased by means of {{m|de|[[welcher]] [[Sache]]||what thing}}. [[possessive|Possessive]] genitives are more commonly paraphrased with {{m|de|wovon||of what}}. It is also possible to use the genitive form {{m|de||wessen}}, but it wouldn't be used in questions such as '''''Wessen''' ist das?'' because here it would be understood as the genitive form of {{m|de|wer}}.
* The colloquial ''was'' meaning "something" can only be the first word in a sentence if followed by an adjective: {{l|de||'''''Was''' Wichtiges fehlt noch.''|'''Something''' important is missing.}} Otherwise the full form ''etwas'' must be used: {{l|de||''Etwas fehlt noch.''|Something is missing.}} The reason for this is that the latter sentence could be misinterpreted as a question if ''was'' were used.

====Declension====
{{de-decl-sg-nogender|was|{{l|de|wessen}}, {{l|de|wes}} {{q|archaic}}|was|was}}
* The dative form is mostly used in informal contexts, namely with prepositions (see usage note above) and in the sense of “something”.
* Using it as a bare dative object in questions is not altogether ungrammatical, but awkward: {{m|de||'''Was''' hat er sich gewidmet?|'''To what''' did he dedicate himself?}}. Only the double form {{m|de||wem oder was|t=to whom or what}} is unproblematic.
* Where a bare dative object would be required in formal style, the sentence is generally rephrased, such as by using {{m|de|welcher}} with a suitable noun in context: {{m|de||'''Welchen Interessen''' hat er sich gewidmet?|t='''To what interests''' did he dedicate himself?}}

====Usage notes====
Notes on the verb ''węsen'' (to be): In recent times (~1800) the old [[subjunctive mood|subjunctive]] ''wer'' is used in place of ''was'' by many speakers. This might be the old subjunctive which is now used as a [[preterite]] or a reduction of ''weren'', which is the preterite plural indicative of the verb. It might also be an imitation of the High German cognate ''war''. Many smaller dialectal clusters do this, but no dialect does it. That means: even though there are many regions within e.g. Lower Saxony that use ''wer'' for ''was'', maybe even the majority, there is no straight connection between them, i.e. which form is used can depend on preference, speaker and specific region. Due to this "one town this way, one town that way"-nature of the situation no form can be named "standard" for a greater dialect, such as Low Saxon.

=====Usage notes=====
* After the Early Middle English period and especially from later in the 14th century onwards, {{m|enm|wes}} is displaced by pandialectal ''was'' in Kent and the West Midlands.
* As with e.g. {{m|enm|is}}, the restressed form {{IPAchar|/waːs/}} (and {{IPAchar|/wɛːs/}} corresponding to {{IPAchar|/wɛs/}}) is sometimes required by rhyme; it is unclear how much this reflected normal spoken usage, though compare the late spelling {{m|enm|waas}} and {{m+|enm-nor|wase}}.
