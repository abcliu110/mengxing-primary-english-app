===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*gʷelH-|id=throw}}
{{ety|en|:af|volley|ball<id:Q18545>|tree=1}}
From {{af|en|volley|ball<id:Q18545>}}. In 1895, in Holyoke, Massachusetts, William G. Morgan created a new game called Mintonette, a name derived from the game of badminton. After an observer, Alfred Halstead, noticed the volleying nature of the game at its first exhibition match in 1896, played at the International YMCA Training School (now called Springfield College), the game quickly became known as volleyball (it was originally spelled as two words: "volley ball") {{rfv-etym|en}}

# {{senseid|en|Q1734}}{{lb|en|uncountable}} A [[game]] played on a [[rectangular]] [[court]] between two [[teams]] of two to six [[player]]s which involves striking a [[ball]] back and forth over a [[net]].
# {{lb|en|countable}} The [[inflated]] ball used in such a game.
