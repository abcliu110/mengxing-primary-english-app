===Etymology===
The verb is from {{inh|en|enm|crien}} (13th century), from {{der|en|fro|crier}}, from {{der|en|la-vul|*crītāre}}, generally thought to derive from {{m+|la-cla|quirito|quirītāre}} ({{m+|gmw-pro|*krītan}} has also been suggested as a source). The noun corresponds to {{inh|en|enm|cry}}, {{m|enm|crie}}, from {{der|en|fro|cri}}, a deverbal of {{m|fro|crier}}.

{{col-top|2|etymology note}}
{{cog|enm|crien}} eventually displaced native {{cog|enm|galen||to cry out}} (from {{cog|ang|galan}}), {{cog|enm|greden||to cry out}} (from {{cog|ang|grǣdan}}), {{cog|enm|yermen||to bellow, mourn, lament}} (from {{cog|ang|ġierman}}), {{cog|enm|hoen|hooen, hoen|to cry out}} (from {{cog|non|hóa}}), {{cog|enm|remen||to cry, shout}} (from {{cog|ang|hrīeman}}, compare {{cog|ang|hrēam||noise, outcry, lamentation, alarm}}), {{cog|enm|gretan|greten, graten|to weep, cry, lament}} (from {{cog|ang|grǣtan}} and {{cog|non|gráta}}). More at {{m|en|greet}}, {{m|en|regret}}.

Already in the 13th century, the meaning was extended to include the sense "to shed tears" (natively {{m|en|weep}}); ''cry'' used in this sense had mostly replaced ''weep'' by the 16th century.
{{col-bottom}}
