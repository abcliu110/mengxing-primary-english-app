===Etymology===
{{etymon|en|:af|gas<id:matter>|-ol<id:oil>|-ine<id:chemistry>|tree=1}}
From {{suffix|en|gas|ol}} (suffix used in the names of oils) {{suffix|en||ine}} (suffix used in the names of derivatives). So called because it was originally used in the manufacture and enrichment of domestic gases, such as [[air gas]].<ref>{{cite book|last=O'Connor|first=Henry|year=1909|title=Petrol Air-Gas: A Practical Handbook on the Installation and Working of Air-Gas Lighting Systems for Country Houses|location=London|publisher=Crosby Lockwood & Son|page=14|url=https://archive.org/details/petrolairgasprac00oconrich/page/14}}</ref>

Some sources suggest that the name originated from the brand name ''Cazeline'', a [[petroleum]]-derived lighting oil,<ref>[http://books.google.co.uk/books?id=nwY5AQAAMAAJ&pg=PA368#v=onepage&q&f=true ''The Solicitors' Journal and Reporter''], volume 9, page 368, 1865</ref> from the surname of the man who first marketed it in 1862, [[w:John Cassell|John Cassell]],<ref>John Lloyd, John Mitchinson, ''1,227 QI Facts To Blow Your Socks Off'', Faber & Faber, 2012 {{ISBN|0571297951}}.</ref> and the suffix ''–eline'', from Greek {{m|grc|ἔλαιον||oil, olive oil}}, from {{m|grc|ἐλαία||olive}}. ''Gasolene'' is found from 1863, and ''gasoline'' from 1864.<ref>{{cite-web |url=http://blog.oxforddictionaries.com/2012/04/the-origin-of-gasoline/ |title=Archived copy |accessdate=20 September 2016 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170729091400/http://blog.oxforddictionaries.com/2012/04/the-origin-of-gasoline/ |archivedate=29 July 2017 }}</ref>
