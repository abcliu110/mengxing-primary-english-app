===Etymology===
From {{inh|en|enm|trust|trust, trost|t=trust, protection}}. Long considered a borrowing from {{cog|non|traust|t=confidence, help, protection}}, from {{cog|gem-pro|*traustą}}, but the root vocalism is incompatible, so ''trust'' has come to be considered a reflex of an unattested {{inh|en|ang|*trust}}, from a rare zero-grade Proto-Germanic variant of the same root also attested in {{cog|gmh|getrüste|t=host}}.<ref>{{cite-journal|title=Getting a word in: Contact, etymology and English vocabulary in the twelfth century|author=Richard Dance|year=2014|journal=Journal of the British Academy|volume=2|url=https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/1554/07_Dance_1803.pdf|doi=10.5871/jba/002.153|page=166|passage=it is now usually agreed [that ''trust''] cannot be explained as a loan from ON (cp. OIcel ''traustr'' ‘trusty’, ''treysta'' ‘to make trusty, trust’) but must be referred to a zero-grade derivation on the same PGmc root, which happens not to be recorded in OE; see e.g. OED s.v. ''trust'' adj., d’Ardenne (1961: glossary s.v. ''trusten''), Hoad (1985: 139–40).|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20260110002027/https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/1554/07_Dance_1803.pdf|archivedate=10 January 2026}}</ref> Ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*deru-|t=be firm, hard, solid}}.
