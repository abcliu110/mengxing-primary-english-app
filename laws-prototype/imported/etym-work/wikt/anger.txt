===Etymology===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*h₂enǵʰ-}}
From {{inh|en|enm|anger|t=grief, pain, trouble, affliction, vexation, sorrow, wrath}}, from {{der|en|non|angr}}, {{m|non|ǫngr|t=affliction, sorrow}} (compare {{cog|non|ang}}, {{m|non|ǫng|t=troubled}}), from {{der|en|gem-pro|*angazaz|t=grief, sorrow}}, from {{der|en|ine-pro|*h₂enǵʰ-|t=narrow, tied together}}.

Cognate with {{cog|da|anger||regret, remorse}}, {{cog|nb|anger||regret, remorse}}, {{cog|sv|ånger||regret}}, {{cog|is|angur||trouble}}, {{cog|ang|ange}}, {{m|ang|enge||narrow, close, straitened, constrained, confined, vexed, troubled, sorrowful, anxious, oppressive, severe, painful, cruel}}, {{cog|de|Angst||anxiety, anguish, fear}}, {{cog|la|angō||squeeze, choke, vex}}, {{m|la|angor||strangulation; anguish, torment}} (whence the English {{glossary|doublet}} {{dbt|en|angor|notext=1}}), French [[angoisse]], {{cog|sq|ang||fear, anxiety, pain, nightmare}}, {{cog|ae|𐬄𐬰𐬀𐬵||strangulation; distress}}, {{cog|grc|ἄγχω||to squeeze, strangle}}, {{cog|sa|अंहस्}}, {{m|sa|अंहु||anxiety, distress, affliction|lit=narrowness}}. Also compare with {{cog|en|anguish}}, {{m|en|anxious}}, {{m|en|quinsy}}, and perhaps to {{m|en|awe}} and {{m|en|ugly}}. The word seems to have originally meant “to choke, squeeze”.<ref name="webster_1913">{{R:Webster 1913}}</ref>

The verb is from {{der|en|enm|angren}}, {{m|enm|angeren}}, from {{der|en|non|angra}}. Compare with {{cog|is|angra}}, {{cog|nn|angra}}, {{cog|nb|angre}}, {{cog|sv|ångra}}, {{cog|da|angre}}.
