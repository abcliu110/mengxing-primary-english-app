===Etymology 1===
From {{inh|en|enm|plesen}}, {{m|enm|plaisen}}, borrowed from {{der|en|fro|plaise}}, conjugated form of {{m|fro|plaisir}} or {{m|fro|plaire}}, from {{der|en|la|placeō|t=to please, to seem good}},<ref name="dictionary">{{R:Dictionary.com}}</ref> from the {{der|en|ine-pro|*pleHk-|t=pleasingness, permission}}. In this sense, displaced native {{ncog|ang|līcian}}, whence {{w|Modern English}} {{m|en|like}}.

===Etymology 2===
Short for {{m|en|if you please}}, an intransitive, [[ergative]] form taken from {{m|en||if it please you}}<ref name="dictionary" /><ref>{{R:Online Etymology Dictionary}}</ref> which is a {{calque|en|fr|s'il vous plaît|nocap=1}}, which replaced ''[[pray]]''. ''If it please you'' is a [[present subjunctive]] form, but most current uses of ''please'' are not parsed that way.

* {{q|for the exaggerated way it is often pronounced as the expression of annoyance}} {{alt|en|puh-lease}}

=====Usage notes=====
* The word is most commonly found at the beginning of the clause, at its end, or directly before the verb. Any other positioning, especially when set off by a pause in speech, is often the sign of a non-native speaker.
