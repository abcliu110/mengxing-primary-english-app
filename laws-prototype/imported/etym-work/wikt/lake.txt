===Etymology 1===
[[File:Glarus Kloentalersee.jpg|thumb|A mountain lake.]]
{{ety|en|id=body of water|:inh|enm:lake<ety:inh<ang:lacu>>|enm:lac<ety:bor<fro:lac>>|tree=1}}
Arose from a conflation of

* {{inh|en|enm|lake|t=small stream of running water, pool, lake}}, from {{inh|en|ang|lacu|t=stream, pool, pond, lake}}, from {{inh|en|gmw-pro|*laku}}, from {{inh|en|gem-pro|*lakō|t=stream, pool, body of water", originally "a place where water runs off and collects}}, ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*leg-|t=to leak, drain}}; with
* {{noncog|enm|lac|t=lake}}, from {{noncog|fro|lac|t=lake, ditch, pit}}, a borrowed term, likely from {{noncog|la|lacus|t=lake, tub, vat}} (see {{m+|fro|lac}} for more).

The first element is related to {{cog|nl|laak|t=stream, drainage ditch, pond}}, {{cog|nds-de|Lake}}, {{m|nds-de|Laak|t=drainage, marshland}}, {{cog|de|Lache|t=puddle, pool}}, {{cog|no|løk|t=a deep, slow-moving stream; a widening in a stream or river}}, {{cog|fo|løkur|t=small brook}} and {{m|fo|lækja|t=water hole, well, watershoot in a brook}}, {{cog|is|lækur|t=stream}}.<ref>{{R:OED Online|code=105167|entry=lake|pos=n.3|date=September 2021}}</ref>

Despite their similarity in form and meaning, {{m+|ang|lacu}} is not related to {{noncog|en|lay#Etymology_2|t=lake}}, {{noncog|la|lacus|t=hollow, lake, pond}}, {{noncog|gd|loch|t=lake}}, {{noncog|grc|λάκκος|t=waterhole, tank, pond, pit}}, all from {{noncog|ine-pro|*lókus|*lókus, *l̥kwés|t=lake, pool}}.<ref>{{R:gem:EDPG|head=Lagu-}}</ref>

=====Usage notes=====
* As with the names of {{m|en|[[river]]s}}, {{m|en|[[mount]]s}} and {{m|en|[[mountain]]s}}, the names of lakes are typically formed by adding the word before or after the unique term: {{m|en|Lake Titicaca}} or {{m|en|Great Slave Lake}}. Generally speaking, names formed using adjectives or attributives see {{m|en||lake}} added to the end, as with {{m|en|Reindeer Lake}}; {{m|en||lake}} is usually added before proper names, as with {{m|en|Lake Michigan}}. This derives from the earlier but now uncommon form {{m|en||lake of ~}}: for instance, the 19th-century {{m|en|Lake of Annecy}} is now usually simply {{m|en|Lake Annecy}}. There are exceptions to this generalization, however, including notably the names of the individual [[Finger Lakes]] (e.g. [[Oneida]] Lake, [[Seneca]] Lake, [[Cayuga]] Lake). It frequently occurs, however, that foreign placenames are misunderstood as proper nouns, as with the Chinese {{m|en|Taihu||Great Lake}} and {{m|en|Qinghai||Blue Sea}} being frequently rendered as {{m|en|Lake Tai}} and {{m|en|Qinghai Lake}}.
