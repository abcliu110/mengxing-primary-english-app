===Etymology 1===
{{root|en|ine-pro|*teh₂g-|id=touch}}
The verb is from {{inh|en|enm|tasten}}, borrowed from {{der|en|fro|taster||to taste, touch or hit}}, from [[unattested]] {{der|en|la-vul|*tastāre||to touch or feel}}, from {{m|la-vul||*taxitāre}}, an [[w:Innovation (linguistics)|innovated]] {{lg|iterative}} form of {{der|en|la-cla|taxo|taxāre|to touch sharply}}, from {{m|la|tango|tangere|to touch, to grasp}}, ultimately from {{der|en|ine-pro|*teh₂g-|id=touch}}, which is assumed to have had the same meaning as {{m|la||tangere}}.<br/>
The noun came from the verb, and the two [[conflate]]d after English lost its [[infinitive]] suffix {{m|en|-en|id=verb ending}}, though {{m|enm|tasten}} was most likely already used [[nominatively]] (as a {{lg|gerund}}), similar to {{w|Modern English}} {{m|en|tasting}}.

Almost fully {{displaced|en|''native'' [[smack]]|nocap=1|sl=1}}, from {{ncog|enm|smac,smak,smacke}}, from {{ncog|ang|smæc,smæċċ||taste, smatch}}.<br/>
{{displaced|en|smatch}}, from {{ncog|enm|smacchen,smecchen}}, from {{ncog|ang|smæċċan||to taste; to smack}}; displaced also {{ncog|enm|buriȝen}}, from {{ncog|ang|bierġan||to taste}}.
