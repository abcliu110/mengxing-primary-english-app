# {{n-g|Indicating movement towards or location at a higher place or position.}}
## [[away|Away]] from the surface of the [[Earth]] or other [[planet]]; in [[opposite]] direction to the downward pull of [[gravity]].
##: {{ux|en|I looked '''up''' and saw the airplane overhead.}}
##: {{ux|en|Shoots grow '''up''' and roots grow down.}}
## To or at a physically higher or more elevated position.
##: {{ux|en|All day we climbed '''up''' and '''up'''.}}
##: {{ux|en|Lift the carpet '''up''' and look underneath.}}
##: {{ux|en|He lives '''up''' in the hills.}}
##* '''1925''', Walter Anthony and Tom Reed (titles), [[w:Rupert Julian|Rupert Julian]] (director), ''[[w:The Phantom of the Opera|The Phantom of the Opera]]'', silent movie
##*: ‘<!--sic-->The Phantom! The Phantom is '''up''' from the cellars again!’
## To an upright or erect position.
##: {{ux|en|sit '''up'''; stand '''up'''; get '''up''' out of bed}}
## {{lb|en|figuratively}} To a higher level of some quantity or notional quantity, such as [[price]], [[volume]], [[pitch]], [[happiness]], etc.
##: {{ux|en|Gold has gone '''up''' with the uncertainty in the world markets.}}
##: {{ux|en|Turn it '''up''', I can barely hear it.}}
##: {{ux|en|Listen to your voice go '''up''' at the end of a question.}}
##: {{ux|en|Cheer '''up''', the weekend's almost here.}}
# {{n-g|Indicating movement in any other direction visualised as "up".}}
## To or towards what is considered the top of something, irrespective of whether this is presently physically higher.
##: {{ux|en|Go back '''up''' to the top of the page.}}
##: {{ux|en|As I lay on my back, a pain shot '''up''' from my toes to my chest.}}
##: {{ux|en|Let’s move '''up''' to the front of the bus.}}
## To the [[north]] (as north is at the top of typical maps).
##: {{ux|en|I live in Florida, but I'm going '''up''' to New York to visit my family this weekend.}}
## Towards or at a central place, or any place that is visualised as ‘up’ by virtue of local features or local convention, or arbitrarily, irrespective of direction or elevation change.
##: {{ux|en|We travelled from Yorkshire '''up''' to London.}}
##: {{ux|en|I'm going '''up''' to the other end of town.}}
##: {{ux|en|He lives '''up''' by the railway station.}}
## {{lb|en|rail transport}} Towards the principal [[terminus]], towards [[milepost]] zero.
## {{lb|en|UK|academia|dated}} To university, especially to [[Cambridge]] or [[Oxford]].
##: {{ux|en|She's going '''up''' to read Classics this September.}}
##* {{quote-book|en|year=1867|author=John Timbs|title=Lives of wits and humourists|page=125
|passage=The son of the Dean of Lichfield was only three years older than Steele, who was a lad of only twelve, when at the age of fifteen, Addison went '''up''' to Oxford.}}
##* {{quote-book|en|year=1998|author=Rita McWilliams Tullberg|title=Women at Cambridge|page=112
|passage=Others insinuated that women 'crowded '''up''' to Cambridge', not for the benefits of a higher education, but because of the proximity of 2,000 young men.}}
##* {{quote-book|en|year=2002|author=Peter Harman|title=Cambridge Scientific Minds|page=79
|passage=A precocious mathematician, Babbage was already well versed in the Continental mathematical notations when he went '''up''' to Cambridge.}}
## {{lb|en|sailing}} [[against|Against]] the [[wind]] or [[current]].
## Towards the source of a river, against the direction of flow.
##: {{ux|en|We started in London and rowed all the way '''up''' to Oxford.}}
## {{lb|en|Cartesian graph}} In a positive vertical direction.
# To or in a position of equal advance or equality; not short of, back of, less advanced than, away from, etc.; usually followed by ''to'' or ''with''.
#: {{ux|en|I was '''up''' to my chin in water.}}
