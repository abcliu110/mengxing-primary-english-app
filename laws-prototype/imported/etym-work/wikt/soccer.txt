===Etymology===
Originally [[British English]]; as an abbreviation for {{m|en|association football}}, via abbreviation {{suffix|en|assoc.|er|id2=Oxford|pos2=suffix}}; earlier {{m|en|socker}} (1885), also {{m|en|socca}} (1889), with {{m|en|soccer}} attested 1888.

Compare contemporary {{m|en|rugger}}, from {{m|en|rugby}}.<ref>{{R:Online Etymology Dictionary}}</ref> Similarly constructed coinages from the same period include: {{m|en|brekker||breakfast}}, {{m|en|fresher||freshman}} and {{m|en|footer||football}}. See {{w|Oxford -er}}.

====Usage notes====
* Since the 1990s, {{m|en|football}} has become more commonly used in the UK and in some other countries where Association football is the most popular football game, including English-speaking territories in Africa, the Caribbean and South Asia, as well as for some English-as-a-second-language (ESL) speakers worldwide. Countries where another type of football sport (or a different football 'code') is more popular are likely to use ''soccer''. These include US, Canada, South Africa, Ireland and some other countries (see [[:wikipedia:Names for association football|here]]). Both ''football'' and ''soccer'' are used in Australia and New Zealand, although ''football'' also refers to other codes in these countries.
* ''soccer'' was widely used in the UK until the 1990s, although sometimes perceived as [[upper class]] or [[snobbish]]. Since then, the term has come to be seen as an [[Americanism]] (possibly due to media coverage of the {{w|1994 FIFA World Cup}} hosted in the US and the rise of {{w|Major League Soccer}}) and its usage has declined significantly. The word is still encountered in the UK in some legacy contexts, such as the title of the show ''{{w|Soccer Saturday}}'', but otherwise its use is often criticized as inauthentic or incorrect.
