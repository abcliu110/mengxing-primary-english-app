===Etymology===
{{ety|en|:inh|enm:cross|tree=1}}
From {{inh|en|enm|cross}}, {{m|enm|cros}}, from {{inh|en|ang|cros|t=rood, cross}}, from {{der|en|non|kross}}, from {{der|en|sga|cros}}, from {{der|en|la|crux}} ({{m|la|crucī}}). In this sense displaced native {{cog|enm|rode}}, from {{cog|ang|rōd|t=cross}}; see {{m+|en|rood}}. {{dbt|en|crouch|t1=cross|crux}}.  Compare {{cog|cy|croes}}, {{cog|ga|crois}}.

The sense of "two intersecting lines drawn or cut on a surface; two lines intersecting at right angles" without regard to religious signification develops from the late 14th century.
