Perhaps {{back-form|en|drumslade||drummer|nocap=1}}, from {{der|en|dum|trommelslach||drumbeat}}, from {{m|dum|trommel||drum}} + {{m|dum|slach||beat}} ({{cog|nl|slag}}).

Or perhaps borrowed directly from a continental Germanic language; compare {{der|en|dum|tromme||drum}}, {{der|en|gml|trumme||drum}} et al. Compare also {{cog|gmh|trumme}}, {{m|gmh|trumbe||drum}}, {{cog|goh|trumba||trumpet}}. Compare [[Ancient Greek]] {{l|grc|τῠ́μπᾰνον||kettledrum}} whence [[Modern English]] {{l|en|timbre}}.

=====Usage notes=====
* When used in the plural, "drums" or "the drums" often specifically means a drum kit as used for contemporary styles such as rock or jazz; a classical [[percussionist]] would be very unlikely to say that they "play the drums" on a piece, even if the only parts they play are, indeed, drums (as opposed to [[marimba]], [[xylophone]], or similar).
