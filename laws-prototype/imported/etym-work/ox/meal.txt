noun senses 1 to 2 Old English mǣl (also in the sense ‘measure’, surviving in words such as piecemeal ‘measure taken at one time’), of Germanic origin. The early sense of meal involved a notion of “fixed time”; compare with Dutch maal ‘meal, (portion of) time’ and German Mal ‘time’, Mahl ‘meal’, from an Indo-European root meaning ‘to measure’. noun sense 3 Old English melu , meolo , of Germanic origin; related to Dutch meel and German Mehl , from an Indo-European root shared by Latin molere ‘to grind’.
