Origin and history of wrap wrap (v.) early 14c., wrappen , "roll or fold together; envelop, surround; cover and fasten securely, swaddle; fold (something) up or back on itself," a word of uncertain origin. Perhaps (Watkins) via Scandinavian (compare Danish dialectal vravle "to wind"), from PIE *werp- "to turn, wind," from root *wer- (2) "to turn, bend." Or perhaps a variant of lap (v.2). Related: Wrapped ; wrapping . also from early 14c. wrap (n.) late 15c., wrappe , "wrapper or covering," specifically "fine cloth used as a cover or wrapping for bread;" from wrap (v.). In reference to various types of women's garments, it is attested by 1827; as a cover or blanket to be worn about the person, by 1861. The meaning "plastic film or cellophane used as a wrap" is from 1930. The meaning "end of a filming session" is attested from 1970 (compare wrap-up ). The meaning "sandwich material folded up in a flour tortilla" is by 1998. The figurative phrase under wraps "in concealment" is recorded from 1939. also from late 15c.
