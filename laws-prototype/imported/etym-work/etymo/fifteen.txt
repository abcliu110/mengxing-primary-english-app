Origin and history of fifteen fifteen (adj., n.) "1 more than fourteen; the number which is one more than fourteen; a symbol representing this number;" Old English fiftyne , from fif "five" (from PIE root *penkwe- "five") + tyne (see -teen ). For vowel shift, see met (v.). Cognate with Old Saxon fiftein , Old Frisian fiftine , Old Norse fimtan , Swedish femton , Dutch vijftien , German fünfzehn , Gothic fimftaihun "fifteen." French quinze , Italian quindici "fifteen" are from Latin quindecim (from quinque "five;" see quinque- ; + -decim (see -teen ). The number of players forming a side in rugby.
