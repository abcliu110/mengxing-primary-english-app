Origin and history of vital vital (adj.) late 14c., in physiology and philosophy, "of or pertaining to the animating principle; manifesting life," from Old French vital and directly from Latin vitalis "of or belonging to life," from vita "life," related to vivere "to live" (from PIE root *gwei- "to live"). By mid-15c. as "alive, living, containing life." The sense of "necessary or important" is from 1610s, via the notion of "essential to life" (late 15c.). Figuratively, "that which is essential to continued existence" of anything is by 1610s. Vital capacity is recorded from 1852. Vital statistics , those concerned with or relating to facts of life (birth, marriage, death, etc.), is by 1837; the colloquial meaning "a woman's bust, waist, and hip measurements" is from 1952. Related: Vitally . also from late 14c.
