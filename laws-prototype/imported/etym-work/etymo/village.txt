Origin and history of village village (n.) late 14c., "inhabited assemblage of houses larger than a hamlet but smaller than a town," from Old French vilage "houses and other buildings in a group" (usually smaller than a town), from Latin villaticum "farmstead" (with outbuildings), noun use of neuter singular of villaticus "having to do with a farmstead or villa," from villa "country house" (see villa ). By 1865 in reference to a small, self-contained district within a city. As an adjective from 1580s, "of, belonging to, or characteristic of a village." Village idiot is recorded from 1825. also from late 14c.
