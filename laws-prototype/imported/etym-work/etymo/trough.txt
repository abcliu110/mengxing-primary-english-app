Origin and history of trough trough (n.) Old English trog "flat, shallow wooden vessel; tray, hollow vessel, canoe," from Proto-Germanic *trugaz (source also of Old Frisian, Old Saxon, Old Norse trog , Middle Dutch troch , Dutch trog , Old High German troc , German trog ), from PIE *dru-ko- , from root *deru- "be firm, solid, steadfast," with specialized senses "wood, tree" and derivatives referring to objects made of wood. Formerly pronounced in English with a hard -gh- (as in Scottish loch ); the pronunciation shifted to "-ff," but spelling remained.
