Origin and history of cow cow (n.) "female of a bovine animal," especially the domestic ox, Middle English cu, qu, kowh , from Old English cu "cow," from Proto-Germanic *kwon (source also of Old Frisian ku , Middle Dutch coe , Dutch koe , Old High German kuo , German Kuh , Old Norse kyr , Danish, Swedish ko ), earlier *kwom , from PIE root *gwou- "ox, bull, cow." Applied to the females of various large animals from late 14c. As an insulting or degrading word for a woman, 1690s. cow (v.) "intimidate, daunt the fear or courage of," c. 1600, probably [OED] from Old Norse kuga "oppress," which is of unknown origin but perhaps has something to do with the Scandinavian forms of cow (n.) on the notion of "easily herded." Related: Cowed ; cowing . also from c. 1600
