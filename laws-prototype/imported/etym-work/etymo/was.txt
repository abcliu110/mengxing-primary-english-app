Origin and history of was was (v.) Middle English, from Old English wesan, wæs, wæron , 1st and 3rd person singular of wesan "to remain," from Proto-Germanic *wesanan (source also of Old Saxon wesan , Old Norse vesa , Old Frisian wesa , Middle Dutch wesen , Dutch wezen , Old High German wesen "being, existence," Gothic wisan "to be"). This is reconstructed to be from PIE root *wes- (3) "remain, abide, live, dwell" (cognates Sanskrit vasati "he dwells, stays;" compare vestal ). In Old English wesan was a distinct verb, but it came to supply the past tense of am . and lost its separate identity. This probably began to develop in Proto-Germanic, as it is also the case in Gothic and Old Norse. See be . As a noun, "something past and gone," late 14c.
