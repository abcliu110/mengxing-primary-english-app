Origin and history of restive restive (adj.) early 15c., restif , restyffe , of animals, "not moving forward," from Old French restif "motionless, brought to a standstill" (Modern French rétif ), from rester "to remain" (see rest (v.2)). Rare or archaic in the original sense; the prevailing meaning "refusing to stand still" especially of horses (attested by 1680s) probably is based on the notion of "unmanageable, impatient in restraint" in reference to a horse refusing to go forward (1650s). But it also is perhaps influenced by rest (v.), an old aphetic form of arrest "to stop, check," and by confusion with restless . Compare resty in the same sense, 1510s of horses, c. 1600 of persons. Related: Restively; restiveness . also from early 15c.
