Origin and history of wilderness wilderness (n.) c. 1200, "uncultivated place, tract of land inhabited only by wild beasts," from archaic wildern (adj.) "deserted or uninhabited," from Old English wild-deoren , an adjective from wild-deor , wildior "undomesticated animal, woodland deer;" see wild (adj.) + deer (n.). The second element probably is -ness , but the word could be explained as from wild-deor + adjectival -en (2) + genitive -s . As figurative of that in which one loses one's way, by mid-14c. Similar formation in Dutch wildernis , German Wildernis , though the usual form there is Wildnis . also from c. 1200
