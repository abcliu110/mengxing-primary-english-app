Origin and history of afternoon afternoon (n.) "part of the day from noon to evening," c. 1300, from after + noon . In 15c.-16c., the form was at afternoon ; from c. 1600 it has been in the afternoon . As an adjective from 1570s. Middle English had also aftermete "afternoon, part of the day following the noon meal" (mid-14c.). also from c. 1300
