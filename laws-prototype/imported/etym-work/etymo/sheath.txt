Origin and history of sheath sheath (n.) Middle English shethe , "close-fitting case or covering for a blade," from Old English sceaþ , scæþ , from Proto-Germanic *skaith- (source also of Old Saxon scethia , Old Norse skeiðir (plural), Old Frisian skethe , Middle Dutch schede , Dutch schede , Old High German skaida , German scheide "a sheath, scabbard"), perhaps from an extended form of PIE root *skei- "to cut, split," on the notion of a split stick with the sword blade inserted. Extended to any somewhat similar tube-like covering by 1550s; the meaning "condom" is recorded from 1861; the sense of "close-fitting dress or skirt" is attested from 1904.
