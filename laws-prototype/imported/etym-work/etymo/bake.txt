Origin and history of bake bake (v.) Old English bacan "to bake, to cook by dry heat in a closed place or on a heated surface," from Proto-Germanic *bakan "to bake" (source also of Old Norse baka , Middle Dutch backen , Old High German bahhan , German backen ), from PIE *bheg- (source also of Greek phogein "to roast"), extended form of root *bhē- "to warm" (see bath ). Related: Baked (Middle English had baken ); baking . Baked beans is attested by 1803. bake (n.) 1560s, "process of baking," from bake (v.). As "social gathering at which baked food is served," 1846, American English. also from 1560s
