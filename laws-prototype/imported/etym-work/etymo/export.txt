Origin and history of export export (v.) by 1610s, "take or carry out of a place;" perhaps from late 15c., from Latin exportare "to carry out, bring out; send away, export," from ex "out, away" (see ex- ) + portare "to carry" (from PIE root *per- (2) "to lead, pass over"). The sense of "send out (commodities) from one country to another" is recorded in English by 1660s. Related: Exported ; exporting ; exporter . also from 1610s export (n.) "the act of exporting; that which is exported, a commodity carried from one place or country to another for sale," 1680s, from export (v.). also from 1680s
