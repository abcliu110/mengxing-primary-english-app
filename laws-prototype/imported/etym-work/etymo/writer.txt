Origin and history of writer writer (n.) Old English writere "one who can write; clerk, scribe, copyist;" also "one who produces philosophical books or literary compositions;" agent noun from writan (see write (v.)). By c. 1200 in reference to a writer of a letter. Also in Middle English "reporter of events and deeds, chronicler, translator." As a surname by late 13c. ( Adam le Wrytar ). The meaning "sign-painter" is from 1837. Writer's cramp , occupation-neurosis in the hand of one who writes much, is attested by 1843 (also scrivener's palsy ); writer's block , periodic disability among those who write creatively or constructively, is so called by 1950.
