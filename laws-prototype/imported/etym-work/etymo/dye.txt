Origin and history of dye dye (n.) "coloring matter in solution," Middle English deie , from Old English deah , deag "a color, hue, tinge," from Proto-Germanic *daugo (source also of Old Saxon dogol "secret," Old High German tougal "dark, hidden, secret," Old English deagol "secret, hidden; dark, obscure," dohs , dox "dusky, dark"). dye (v.) "fix a color or colors in the substance of by immersion in coloring matter held in solution," Middle English deien , Old English deagian "to dye," from the source of dye (n.). Spelling distinction between dye and die was not firm till 19c. "Johnson in his Dictionary , spelled them both die , while Addison, his near contemporary, spelled both dye " [Barnhart]. Related: dyed . For dyed in the wool (or grain ) see wool (n.).
