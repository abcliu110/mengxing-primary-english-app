Origin and history of stormy stormy (adj.) early 14c., stormi , "characterized by violent weather," from late Old English storemig (12c.), from storm (n.) + -y (2). Figurative use by mid-14c., of persons or events, "turbulent, agitated, characterized by violent disturbance or strife." Related: Stormily ; storminess . Middle English also had stormish , of persons, "fickle, liable to change, inconstant" (mid-15c.). also from early 14c.
