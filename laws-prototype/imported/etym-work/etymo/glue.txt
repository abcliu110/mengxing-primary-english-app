Origin and history of glue glue (n.) "viscous adhesive substance," early 13c., from Old French glu "glue, birdlime" (12c.), from Vulgar Latin *glutis or Late Latin glutem (nominative glus ) "glue," from Latin gluten "glue, beeswax," from PIE *gleit- "to glue, paste" (source also of Lithuanian glitus "sticky," glitas "mucus;" Old English cliða "plaster"), from root *glei- "clay," also forming words with a sense of "to stick together" (see clay ). Formerly also glew . In reference to glue from boiled animal hoofs and hides, c. 1400. Glue-sniffing attested from 1963. also from early 13c. glue (v.) "join or fasten with glue," late 14c., from Old French gluer , gluier "smear with glue; join together," from glu "glue, birdlime" (see glue (n.)). Related: Glued ; gluing . also from late 14c.
