Origin and history of already already (adv.) c. 1300, "in a state of readiness" (an adjectival sense, now obsolete), literally "fully ready, quite prepared," a contraction of all ready ; see all + ready (adj.). Compare Norwegian, Danish allerede "already." As an adverb, "by this time, previous to some specified time," late 14c. The colloquial use in U.S. as a terminal emphatic (as in enough, already! ) is attested from 1903, translating Yiddish shoyn , which is used in same sense. The pattern also is attested in Pennsylvania German and in South African. also from c. 1300
