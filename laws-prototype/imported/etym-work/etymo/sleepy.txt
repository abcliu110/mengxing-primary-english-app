sleepy (adj.) c. 1300, slepi , "lethargic, weary, overcome with sleep, tending to fall asleep," from sleep (n.) + -y (2). Perhaps from an unrecorded Old English slæpig . Old English did have slæpor , slæpwerig in the sense "sleepy;" slæpnes "sleepiness." Similar formation in Old High German slafag . It is attested from late 14c. as "inducing sleep." Of places, "quiet, unexciting" from 1813 (Irving's Sleepy Hollow is from 1820). Sleepy-head "idle, lazy person" is from 1570s. Related: Sleepily ; sleepiness . Sleepish "somewhat sleepy" is attested from 1520s. also from c. 1300
