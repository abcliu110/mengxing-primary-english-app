Origin and history of suggest suggest (v.) 1520s, "place before another's mind; put forward a proposition," from Latin suggestus , past participle of suggerere "bring up, bring under, lay beneath; furnish, afford, supply; prompt" (see suggestion ). The meaning "act so as to call up the idea of (something else); introduce to another's mind by indirect association" is from 1709. It also had a bad sense, "seduce, tempt away from" (1580s).
