Origin and history of preview preview (v.) c. 1600, "to see beforehand," from pre- "before" + view (v.). Marked "rare" in Century Dictionary (1895). The meaning "to show (a film, etc.) before its public opening" is from 1928. Related: Previewed ; previewing . also from c. 1600 preview (n.) "a foretaste," 1880, from preview (v.); specifically "a showing of a book, film, etc. before public release" by 1920. also from 1880
