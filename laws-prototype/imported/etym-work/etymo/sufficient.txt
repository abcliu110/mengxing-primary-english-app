Origin and history of sufficient sufficient (adj.) "adequate for a purpose, enough," early 14c., from Old French soficient "satisfactory," or directly from Latin sufficientem (nominative sufficiens ) "adequate," present participle of sufficere "to supply as a substitute," from sub "up to" (see sub- ) + combining form of facere "to make, to do" (from PIE root *dhe- "to set, put"). It has been used in specialized legal and technical senses since Middle English. also from early 14c.
