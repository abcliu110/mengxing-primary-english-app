Origin and history of estimate estimate (n.) 1560s, "valuation," from Latin aestimatus "determine the value of," figuratively "to value, esteem," verbal noun from aestimare (see esteem (v.)). Earlier in sense "power of the mind" (mid-15c.). Meaning "approximate judgment" is from 1580s. As a builder's statement of projected costs, from 1796. also from 1560s estimate (v.) 1530s, "appraise the worth of," from Latin aestimatus , past participle of aestimare "to value, appraise" (see esteem (v.)). Meaning "form an approximate notion" is from 1660s. Related: Estimated ; estimates ; estimating . also from 1530s
