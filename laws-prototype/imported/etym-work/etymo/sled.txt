Origin and history of sled sled (n.) early 14c., sledde , "a dragged vehicle used for transport of heavy goods over hard ground or ice," from Middle Dutch sledde "sled," from Proto-Germanic *slid- (source also of Old Saxon slido , Old Norse sleði , Danish slæde , Swedish släde , Old High German slito , German Schlitten "sledge"), from the same root as Old English slidan (see slide (v.)). Not found in Old English. In reference to a sleigh used for travel or recreation, it is attested from 1580s, now mainly American English. In reference to a pair of runners connected by a framework with a light platform or seat, used for pleasure coasting, by 1873. also from early 14c. sled (v.) 1718, "transport on a sled" (transitive); 1780, "ride on a sled" (intransitive), from sled (n.). Related: Sledded ; sledder ; sledding . also from 1718
