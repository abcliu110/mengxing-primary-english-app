Origin and history of railway railway (n.) 1812 in the modern sense, from rail (n.1) + way (n.). Also compare railroad (n.). Earlier used of any sort of road on which rails (originally wooden) were laid for easier transport (1776). Rude railway-trains, with all your noise and smoke, I love to see you wheresoe'er ye move : Though Nature seems such trespass to reprove : Though ye the soul of old romance provoke, I thank you, that from misery ye unyoke Thousands of panting horses. [Richard Howitt, from "Railway Sonnets," Hood's Magazine, March 1845] Railway time "standard time adopted throughout a railway system" is by 1847. also from 1812
