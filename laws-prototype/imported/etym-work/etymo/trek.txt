Origin and history of trek trek (n.) 1849, in a South African context, "a stage of a journey by ox wagon;" from Afrikaans trek , "a journey or migration; distance between one place and the next; action of drawing" a vehicle, as an ox; from Dutch trekken "to march, journey," originally "to draw, pull," from Middle Dutch trecken , which probably is related to the source of track (n.). Historically, especially in reference to the Groot Trek (1835 and after) of more than 10,000 Boers, who, discontented with the English colonial authorities, left Cape Colony and went north and north-east. In general use in English as a noun, "long journey or toilsome expedition," by 1941. also from 1849 trek (v.) by 1850, in a South African context, "to travel or migrate by ox wagon," from Afrikaans, from Dutch trekken "to march, journey," originally "to draw, pull" (a vehicle, as an ox), from Middle Dutch trecken , which probably is related to the source of track (n.). Also compare trek (n.). By 1911 in the general sense of "make a long journey or toilsome expedition." Related: Trekked ; trekking . also from 1850
