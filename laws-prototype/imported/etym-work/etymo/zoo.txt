Origin and history of zoo zoo (n.) "collection of living animals," by 1866, said to be from 1847 as a colloquial shortening of the name of the Zoological Gardens of the London Zoological Society, which were established 1828 in Regent's Park to house the society's collection of wild animals. The first three letters were taken but treated as one syllable. "From a mere vulgarism, this corruption has passed into wide colloquial use" [Century Dictionary]. The extended slang meaning "crowded and chaotic place" is recorded by 1935. Zoo-keeper is attested by 1885. also from 1866
