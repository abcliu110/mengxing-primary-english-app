Origin and history of truly truly (adv.) Middle English treuli , "with faithfulness to friends or nation; with constancy to a lover," from true (adj.) + -ly (1), or from Old English treowlice "faithfully, loyally," from treowe . Similar formation in Dutch treuwelijk , German getreulich , Swedish troligen . By c. 1300 as "in accordance with a promise or duty; honorably;" late 14c. as "in reality; exactly, accurately." Well and truly have been paired since mid-15c. Yours truly before a signature at the end of a letter is attested by 1788, from earlier fuller phrases.
