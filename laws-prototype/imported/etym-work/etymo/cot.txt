Origin and history of cot cot (n.1) "small, light bed," 1630s, from Hindi khat "couch, hammock," from Sanskrit khatva , probably from a Dravidian source (compare Tamil kattil "bedstead"). Sense extended to "canvas hammock bed on shipboard" (by 1769), then "portable bed of canvas or similar material, fastened to a light frame, capable of folding up" (1854). Meaning "small bed or crib for a child" is by 1818. also from 1630s cot (n.2) "hut, peasant's cottage, small house," a variant of cote (see cottage ).
