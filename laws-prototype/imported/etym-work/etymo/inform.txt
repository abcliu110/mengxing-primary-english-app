Origin and history of inform inform (v.) early 14c., enfourmen , "to train or instruct in some specific subject," from Old French informer , enformer "instruct, teach" (13c.) and directly from Latin informare "to shape, give form to, delineate," figuratively "train, instruct, educate," from in- "into" (see in- (2)) + formare "to form, shape," from forma "form" (see form (n.)). In early use also enform until c. 1600. The sense of "report facts or news, communicate information to" is recorded by late 14c. Related: Informed ; informing . also from early 14c.
