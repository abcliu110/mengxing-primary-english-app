Origin and history of spend spend (v.) "to pay out or away, deprive oneself of" (money, wealth), Middle English spenden , from Old English -spendan (in forspendan "use up"), from Medieval Latin spendere , a shortening of Latin expendere "to weigh out money, pay down" (see expend ) or possibly of dispendere "to pay out" (see dispense ). The word was borrowed generally in Germanic: Old High German spendon , German and Middle Dutch spenden , Old Norse spenna . The Middle English word is also probably in part from or merged with Old French despendre , from Latin dispendere . In reference to anything of exchangeable value (labor, thoughts, time, etc.), "consume, use up," attested from c. 1300. The notion of "consume or use wastefully or fruitlessly" is by late 14c. The intransitive sense "exhaust, wear (oneself) out" is from 1590s (see spent ).
