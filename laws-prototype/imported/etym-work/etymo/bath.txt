Origin and history of bath bath (n.) Old English bæð "an immersing of the body in water, mud, etc.," also "a quantity of water, etc., for bathing," from Proto-Germanic *badan (source also of Old Frisian beth , Old Saxon bath , Old Norse bað , Middle Dutch bat , German Bad ), from PIE root *bhē- "to warm" + *-thuz , Germanic suffix indicating "act, process, condition" (as in birth , death ). The etymological sense is of heating, not immersing. The city in Somerset, England (Old English Baðun ) was so called from its hot springs. Bath salts is attested from 1875 (Dr. Julius Braun, "On the Curative Effects of Baths and Waters"). Bath-house is from 1705; bath-towel is from 1958.
