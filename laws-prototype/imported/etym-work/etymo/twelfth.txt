Origin and history of twelfth twelfth (adj., n.) "next in order after the eleventh," also an ordinal numeral; "being one of twelve equal parts into which a whole is regarded as divided;" late 14c., with -th (1), altering Middle English twelfte , from Old English twelfta , from twelf (see twelve ). The earlier form is cognate with Old Norse tolfti , Danish tolvte , Old Frisian twelefta , Dutch twaalfde , Old High German zwelifto , German zwölfte . As a noun meaning "a twelfth part," from 1550s. Twelfth Night is Old English twelftan niht , the eve of Epiphany, which comes twelve days after Christmas, formerly an occasion of social rites and a time of merrymaking. also from late 14c.
