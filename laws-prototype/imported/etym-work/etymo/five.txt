Origin and history of five five (num.) "1 more than four; the number which is one more than four; a symbol representing this number;" Old English fif "five," from Proto-Germanic *fimfe (source also of Old Frisian fif , Old Saxon fif , Dutch vijf , Old Norse fimm , Old High German funf , Gothic fimf ), from PIE root *penkwe- "five." The lost *-m- is a regular development (compare tooth ). Five-and-ten (Cent Store) is from 1880, American English, with reference to prices of goods for sale. Five-star (adj.) is from 1913 of hotels, 1945 of generals. Slang five-finger discount "theft" is from 1966. The original five-year plan was 1928 in the U.S.S.R. Five o'clock shadow attested by 1937.
