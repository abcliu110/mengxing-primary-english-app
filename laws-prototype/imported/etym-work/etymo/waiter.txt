Origin and history of waiter waiter (n.) late 14c., "watchman, spy" (senses now obsolete); late 15c., "attendant at a meal, servant who waits at tables;" agent noun from wait (v.). In the surviving sense, originally of household servants; in reference to table attendants at inns, eating houses, etc., it is attested from 1660s. Also from 1650s as "one who abides in expectation" of some event or time. also from late 14c.
