Origin and history of woodpecker woodpecker (n.) one of a large family of birds, 1520s, from wood (n.) + pecker . There are scores if not hundreds of local names for the birds in Britain and U.S., some of from Middle English: woodpeck, woodpie, woodspite, woodspeck, wood-tapper; hickwall , in an enormous range of variations, wood-wale, woodhacker, wood-hack, wood-jobber, wood-knacker , pickatree , etc. Other names are based on its laughing note, such as yaffingale (c. 1600), also yaffle (1802); yucker , yuckel (U.S.). A bird of great mythological importance in Latin but not so in Greek, "though the Dryopes were probably, like the descendants of Picus, a Woodpecker-tribe" [Thompson, "Glossary of Greek Birds"] also from 1520s
