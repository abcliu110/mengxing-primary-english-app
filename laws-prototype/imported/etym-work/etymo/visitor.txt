Origin and history of visitor visitor (n.) "one who comes to see or stay with another," late 14c., visitour , agent noun from visit (v.) and from Anglo-French visitour , Old French visiteor "visitor, inspector," from visiter . Visitor regards the person as coming to see another, but making a longer stay than a caller and enjoying more of social intercourse. Guest regards the person as admitted to hospitality and hence generally as welcome. [Century Dictionary] The sports-team sense is attested by 1900. Alternative visiter is attested from late 14c. It is rare but persistent; OED (1989) finds it as late as Jane Austen, De Quincey, Dickens. The visitor center in a tourist spot is attested by 1964, American English. also from late 14c.
