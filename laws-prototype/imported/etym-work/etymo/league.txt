league (n.1) "alliance," mid-15c., ligg , from French ligue "confederacy, league" (15c.), from Italian lega , from legare "to tie, to bind," from Latin ligare "to bind" (from PIE root *leig- "to tie, bind"). Originally among nations, subsequently extended to political associations (1846) and sports associations (1879). League of Nations is attested from 1917 (created 1919). also from mid-15c. league (n.2) itinerary unit in medieval England, distance of about three statute miles, late 14c., ultimately from Late Latin leuga (source also of French lieue , Spanish legua , Italian lega ), which is said by Roman writers to be from Gaulish. A vague measure (perhaps originally an hour's hike), in England it was a conventional, not a legal measure, and in English it is found more often in poetic than in practical writing. also from late 14c. league (v.) "to form a league," 1610s, from league (n.1). Related: Leagued ; leaguing . also from 1610s
