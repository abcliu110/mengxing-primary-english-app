Origin and history of ensue ensue (v.) c. 1400, "seek after, pursue; follow (a path)," from Old French ensu- , past participle stem of ensivre "follow close upon, come afterward," from Late Latin insequere , from Latin insequi "to pursue, follow, follow after; come next," from in- "upon" (from PIE root *en "in") + sequi "follow" (from PIE root *sekw- (1) "to follow"). Early 15c. as "follow (as a consequence), to result;" mid-15c. as "to follow" in time or space, "to come or appear next, be subsequent to, happen subsequently." Related: Ensued ; ensues ; ensuing . also from c. 1400
