Origin and history of wonderful wonderful (adj.) late Old English wunderfoll , "extraordinary, marvelous; unnatural, magical" see wonder (n.) + -ful . By c. 1200 as "of exceptional quality, so far above the common as to seem marvelous;" mid-14c. as "of the nature to excite astonishment," also "great in degree." Related: Wonderfully; wonderfulness . In colloquial language wonderfully sometimes weakens to little more than "very;" and wonderful (like powerful ) is sometimes vulgarly used as "exceedingly." The Elizabethans used wondrous and wondrously so. Old English wundorlic "extraordinary; puzzling; divine; terrifying" survived into Middle English as wonderli .
