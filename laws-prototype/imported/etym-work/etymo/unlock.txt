Origin and history of unlock unlock (v.) late 14c., unloken , "open (something which has been locked) with a key, unfasten, unfetter," from un- (2) "reverse, opposite of" + lock (v.). The figurative sense of "lay open" is attested from 1530s. Old English had unlucan "to unlock, open." Related: Unlocked ; unlocking . also from late 14c.
