Origin and history of noodle noodle (n.1) "long, narrow strip of dried dough," 1779, from German Nudel , which is of unknown origin; speculated to be ultimately from Latin minutulus "tiny, very small" or a variant of German Knödel "dumpling" and ultimately related to the Germanic source of knot . West Flemish noedel and French nouille are German loan-words. also from 1779 noodle (v.) "improvise or play casually on a musical instrument," 1937 (implied in noodling ), from noun meaning "improvised music," 1926, perhaps from noodle (n.), on analogy of the suppleness of the food and that of the trills and improvised phrases in jazz improvisations. Related: Noodled . also from 1937 noodle (n.2) "head," early 15c., nodel , "head, back of the head," perhaps from Latin nodulus (see nodule ). The meaning "simpleton, stupid person" (1753) probably is derived from it. Noodle-thatcher was old slang for "wig-maker" (1716). also from early 15c.
