Origin and history of assist assist (v.) early 15c., assisten , "to help, aid, give assistance or support to in some undertaking or effort," from Old French assister "to stand by, help, put, place, assist" (14c.), from Latin assistere "stand by, take a stand near, attend," from assimilated form of ad "to" (see ad- ) + sistere "stand still, take a stand; to set, place, cause to stand" (from PIE *si-st- , reduplicated form of root *sta- "to stand, make or be firm"). Related: Assisted ; assisting . Medical assisted suicide is attested by 1884. also from early 15c. assist (n.) 1570s, "an act of assistance," from assist (v.). In the sporting sense attested 1877 in baseball, 1925 in ice hockey. also from 1570s
