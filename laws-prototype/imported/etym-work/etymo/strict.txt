strict (adj.) early 15c., in a physical sense, "restricted as to space or extent; narrow, drawn in tight, small," from Latin strictus "drawn together, close, tight," past participle of stringere (2) "to draw or bind tight" (see strain (v.)). Original sense obsolete; the surviving senses seem to have evolved on the notions of "tenseness" or "closeness." The meaning "stringent and rigorous" (of law) is by 1570s. In reference to qualities or conditions generally, "exact, careful," by 1590s. The figurative sense of "absolute, unbroken" (of silence, etc.) is by 1580s. Strict tempo for regular rhythm in dance music is by 1936. also from early 15c.
