Origin and history of wedding wedding (n.) Old English weddung "state of being wed; pledge, betrothal; act or action of marrying," verbal noun from wed (v.). The meaning "nuptials, ceremony of marriage" is recorded from early 13c.; the usual Old English word for the ceremony was bridelope , literally "bridal run," in reference to conducting the bride to her new home. Another old word was wife-thing (Old English wif-þing ), with thing (n.) in the archaic sense of "meeting, assembly." Wedding ring , given by one of a married pair to another, is from late 14c.; wedding cake , richly decorated to grace a wedding, is recorded from 1640s; of a delicately ornate style of architecture from 1879. Wedding dress , worn by the bride at her wedding, is attested from 1779; wedding reception from 1856. Wedding day is by 1550s; earlier wedding-daies "days before and after a wedding" (mid-15c.).
