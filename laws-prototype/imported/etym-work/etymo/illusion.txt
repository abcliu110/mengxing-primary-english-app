Origin and history of illusion illusion (n.) mid-14c., illusioun , "mockery, scorning, derision;" late 14c., "act of deception; deceptive appearance, apparition; delusion of the mind," from Old French illusion "a mocking, deceit, deception" (12c.), from Latin illusionem (nominative illusio ) "a mocking, jesting, jeering; irony," from past-participle stem of illudere "mock at," literally "to play with." This is from assimilated form of in- "at, upon" (see in- (2)) + ludere "to play" (see ludicrous ). The sense of "deceptive appearance" developed in Church Latin. Related: Illusioned "full of illusions" (1920). also from mid-14c.
