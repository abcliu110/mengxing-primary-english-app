Origin and history of utterly utterly (adv.) "completely, entirely, to an absolute degree," late 14c., from utter (adj.)) + -ly (2). In 14c. also "to be sure, precisely, exactly." Earlier the same word meant "truly, plainly, outspokenly," from utter (v.). Old English uterlic (adj.) meant "external," hence early Middle English outreli "outwardly, superficially; unashamedly." Compare German äusserlich . also from late 14c.
