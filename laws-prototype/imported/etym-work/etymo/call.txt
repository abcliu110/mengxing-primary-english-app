Origin and history of call call (v.) mid-13c., callen , "cry out; call for, summon, invoke; ask for, demand, order; give a name to, apply by way of designation," from Old Norse kalla "cry loudly, summon in a loud voice; name, call by name," reconstructed to be from Proto-Germanic *kall- , which is reconstructed to be from PIE root *gal- "to call, shout." Related: Called ; calling . Old English cognate ceallian "to shout, utter in a loud voice" was late and rare, the usual word being clipian (source of Middle English clepe , yclept ), but it might have contributed to the Middle English word. Old English also had hropan, hruofan , cognate of German rufen . The "heads-or-tails" coin-toss sense is from 1801; the card-playing sense "demand that the hands be shown" is from 1670s; the specific poker sense of "match or raise a bet" is by 1889. The meaning "make a short stop or visit" (Middle English) was literally "stand at the door and call." The "attempt a telephone connection with" sense is from 1882. To call up "summon" is from mid-15c. To call for "demand, require" is from 1530s (earlier in this sense was call after , c. 1400). To call (something) back "revoke" is from 1550s. To call (something) off "cancel" is by 1888; earlier call off meant "summon away, divert" (1630s). To call (someone) names is from 1590s. To call out someone to fight (1823) corresponds to French provoquer . Other Germanic cognates include Middle Dutch kallen "speak, say, tell," Dutch kallen "to talk, chatter," Old High German kallon "speak loudly, call." also from mid-13c. call (n.) early 14c., cal , "a loud cry, an outcry," also "a summons, an invitation," from call (v.). From 1580s specifically as "a summons (by bugle, drum, etc.) to military men to perform some duty;" it is attested from 1680s as "the cry or note of a bird." The sense of "a short formal visit" is from 1862; the meaning "a communication by telephone" is from 1878. It is attested from 1670s as "requirement, duty, right," hence, colloquially, "occasion, cause." also from early 14c.
