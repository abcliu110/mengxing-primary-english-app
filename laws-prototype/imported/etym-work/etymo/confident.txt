Origin and history of confident confident (adj.) 1570s, "self-reliant, sure of oneself;" c. 1600, "fully assured, having strong belief," from French confident , from Latin confidentem (nominative confidens ) "firmly trusting, reliant, self-confident, bold, daring," present participle of confidere "to have full trust or reliance," from assimilated form of com , here perhaps an intensive prefix (see com- ), + fidere "to trust" (from PIE root *bheidh- "to trust, confide, persuade"). Related: Confidently . also from 1570s
