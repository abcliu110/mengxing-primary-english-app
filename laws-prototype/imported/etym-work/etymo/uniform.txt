Origin and history of uniform uniform (adj.) late 15c., uniforme , "having always the same form, unvarying in formal character," from French uniforme (14c.), from Latin uniformis "having only one form or shape," from uni- "one" (see uni- ) + forma "form" (see form (n.)). Related: Uniformly (mid-15c.). also from late 15c. uniform (n.) "distinctive clothes worn by one group, a dress of the same kind as others worn by members of the same body," 1748, originally military, from French uniforme , from the adjective (see uniform (adj.)). The meaning "person in uniform" is attested by 1978 (compare suit (n.), used by 1979 as a derisive term for "businessman"). also from 1748 uniform (v.) 1680s, "make alike, reduce to uniformity," from uniform (adj.). The meaning "dress in a uniform" is from 1861 (U.S. Civil War). Related: Uniformed . also from 1680s
