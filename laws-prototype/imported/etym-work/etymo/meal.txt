Origin and history of meal meal (n.1) late 12c., mēl , "an occasion of taking food, a feast, a supply of food taken at one time for relief of hunger," also (c. 1200) "an appointed time for eating;" from Old English mæl, Anglian mēl , "fixed time, occasion; a meal," from Proto-Germanic *mela- (source also of Old Frisian mel "time;" Middle Dutch mael , Dutch maal "time; meal;" Old Norse mal "measure, time, meal;" German Mal "time," Mahl "meal;" Gothic mel "time, hour"), which is reconstructed (Watkins) to be from PIE root *me- (2) "to measure." The original sense of "time" is preserved in English in piecemeal ; compare Middle English poundmele "by pounds at a time; generously." Meals-on-wheels for a social service offering home delivery of food to persons unable to purchase or prepare their own is attested by 1952 (from 1947 as a mobile food delivery service without reference to social services). Meal ticket is attested by 1865 in a literal sense of "ticket of admission to a dining hall;" the figurative sense of "source of income or livelihood" is from 1899. also from late 12c. meal (n.2) "the edible part of ground grain;" Middle English mēle , from Old English melu , from Proto-Germanic *melwan "grind" (source also of Old Frisian mele "meal," Old Saxon melo , Middle Dutch mele , Dutch meel , Old High German melo , German Mehl , Old Norse mjöl "meal;" literally "what is ground;" Old Saxon, Old High German, Gothic malan , German mahlen "to grind"), from PIE root *mele- "to crush, grind." The verb form is not found in Old English. Forms with an -a- begin in late Middle English. "Now commonly understood to exclude the product of wheat (this being called FLOUR)" [OED].
