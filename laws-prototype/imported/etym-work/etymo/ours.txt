Origin and history of ours ours (pron.) "that or those belonging to us," c. 1300, oures , a double possessive (with genitive suffix -s (1)), originating in northern England, it has taken over the absolute function of our (q.v.). In Middle English ourn , ouren also were used. Ours is a later possessive form from our , and is used in place of our and a noun, thus standing to our in the same relation as hers to her , yours to your , mine to my ....
