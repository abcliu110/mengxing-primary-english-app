Origin and history of our our (pron.) Old English ure "of us, pertaining to or belonging to us," genitive plural of the first person pronoun, from Proto-Germanic *ons (source also of Old Saxon usa , Old Frisian use , Old High German unsar , German unser , Gothic unsar "our"), from PIE *nes- , oblique case of personal pronoun in first person plural (source of Latin nos "we," noster "our"). Also compare ours . Ourselves (late 15c.) "we or us, not others," modeled on yourselves , replaced original construction we selfe, us selfum , etc. It often is added to we for emphasis.
