Origin and history of solo solo (n.) 1690s, "piece of music intended for one voice or instrument," also a performance of such, from Italian solo , literally "alone," from Latin solus "alone" (see sole (adj.)). In reference to dance from 1794. As an adjective in English from 1712, originally in the non-musical sense of "alone, unassisted." Of musicians or instruments, "play a solo part," 1862. in reference to aircraft flights from 1909. The verb is attested by 1858 in music, 1886 in a non-musical sense. Related: Soloed ; soloing . also from 1690s
