Origin and history of unconscious unconscious (adj.) 1712, "unaware, not marked by conscious thought, not known or perceived as existing in itself," from un- (1) "not" + conscious (adj.). Also from 1712 as "not possessing consciousness, non-conscious;" the meaning "temporarily insensible, knocked out" is recorded by 1860. Related: Unconsciously ; unconsciousness . In psychology, the noun the unconscious (1876) is a loan-translation of German das Unbewusste . The adjective in the psychological sense is recorded from 1912, developed in Freud's theories. also from 1712
