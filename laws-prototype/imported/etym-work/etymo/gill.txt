gill (n.1) "organ of breathing in fishes," early 14c., of unknown origin, perhaps related to Scandinavian words, such as Old Norse gjölnar which perhaps means "gills," and Old Danish -gæln (in fiske-gæln "fish gill"); said to be ultimately from a PIE *ghel-una- "jaw" (cognate: Greek kheilos "lip"). Related: Gills . also from early 14c. gill (n.2) liquid measure (in modern use commonly a quarter of a pint), late 13c., from Old French gille , a wine measure, and from Medieval Latin gillo "earthenware jar," words of uncertain origin, perhaps related to the source of gallon . also from late 13c. Gill fem. proper name, shortened form of Gillian . Also see Jill . Gill-flirt "giddy young woman" is from 1630s.
