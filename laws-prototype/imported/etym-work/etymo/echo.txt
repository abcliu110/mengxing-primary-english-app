Origin and history of echo echo (n.) mid-14c., "sound repeated by reflection," from Latin echo , from Greek ēkhō , personified in classical mythology as a mountain nymph who pined for love of Narcissus until nothing remained but her voice. It is from or related to ēkhē "sound," ēkhein "to resound," from extended form of PIE root *(s)wagh- "to resound" (source also of Sanskrit vagnuh "sound," Latin vagire "to cry," Old English swogan "to resound"). Related: Echoes . Also see echo chamber . also from mid-14c. echo (v.) 1550s (intrans.), c. 1600 (trans.), from echo (n.). Related: Echoed ; echoing . also from 1550s
