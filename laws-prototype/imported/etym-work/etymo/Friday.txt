Origin and history of Friday Friday (n.) sixth day of the week, Old English frigedæg "Friday, Frigga's day," from Frige , genitive of *Frigu (see Frigg ), Germanic goddess of married love. The day name is a West Germanic translation of Latin dies Veneris "day of (the planet) Venus," which itself translated Greek Aphrodites hēmera . Compare Old Norse frijadagr , Old Frisian frigendei , Middle Dutch vridach , Dutch vrijdag , German Freitag "Friday," and the Latin-derived cognates Old French vendresdi , French vendredi , Spanish viernes . In Germanic religion, Freya (q.v.) corresponds more closely in character to Venus than Frigg does, and some early Icelandic writers used Freyjudagr for "Friday." A fast-day in the Church, hence Friday face (17c.) for a gloomy countenance.
