Origin and history of among among (prep.) "in, in the midst of," early 12c., from Old English onmang , in late Old English sometimes amang , a contraction of ongemang "among, during," from the phrase on gemang , literally "in the crowd or company (of)," from on (see a- (1)) + gemengan "to mingle." Ths second element is from Proto-Germanic *mangjan "to knead together," which is perhaps from a nasalized form of PIE root *mag- "to knead, fashion, fit." The collective prefix ge- was dropped 12c. leaving onmong , amang , among . Compare Old Saxon angimang "among, amid;" Old Frisian mong "among." also from early 12c.
