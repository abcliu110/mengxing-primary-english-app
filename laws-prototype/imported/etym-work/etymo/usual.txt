Origin and history of usual usual (adj.) late 14c., "generally recognized;" mid-15c., "commonly encountered," from Old French usuel "current, in currency (of money), valid" (13c.) and directly from Late Latin usualis "ordinary," from Latin usus "custom" (see use (v.)). Phrase as usual "in such manner as is common" is recorded by 1716. The usual suspects is from a line delivered by Claude Rains (as a French police inspector) in "Casablanca" (1942). also from late 14c.
