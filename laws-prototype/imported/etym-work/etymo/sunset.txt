Origin and history of sunset sunset (n.) "apparent descent of the sun below the horizon in evening," late 14c., from sun (n.) + set (v.). Perhaps a shortening of sun-setting , or perhaps taken from a Middle English subjunctive such as before the sun set . Old English had sunnansetlgong "sunset," while sunset meant "west." Figurative use from c. 1600. To ride off into the sunset (1963) is from a stereotypical ending of Westerns or romance films. also from late 14c.
