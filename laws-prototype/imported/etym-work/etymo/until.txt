Origin and history of until until (prep., conj.) c. 1200, "onward to and into; onward as far as," from till (prep.). The first element is un- "as far as, up to" (also in unto ), from Old Norse *und "as far as, up to," from Proto-Germanic *und- (source also of Old English oð "up to, as far as," Old Frisian, Old Saxon, Gothic und ), from PIE *nti- , from root *ant- "front, forehead," with derivatives meaning "in front of, before." The two syllables have the same meaning. Originally also used of persons and places. As "continually, up to the time of" early 14c. As a conjunction from c. 1300. Similar formation in Swedish intill , Danish indtil (northern English and Scottish formerly also had intill / intil "into, in"). The Modern German equivalent, bis (Old High German biaz ), is a similar compound, of Old High German bi "by, at, to" and zu "to." also from c. 1200
