Origin and history of unstable unstable (adj.) c. 1200, "apt to move, easily moved," from un- (1) "not" + stable (adj.). The sense of "fickle, vacillating, irresolute, easily swayed" is attested from late 13c. The meaning "liable to fall" is recorded from c. 1300; that of "structurally insecure" by late 14c. Related: Unstably ; unstableness ; unstability . An Old English word for this was feallendlic . Similar formation in Middle High German unstabel . also from c. 1200
