Origin and history of without without (adv., prep.) "outwardly, externally," Middle English withouten , from Old English wiðutan "outside of, from outside," etymologically, "against the outside" (opposite of within ), see with + out (adv.). As "lacking, destitute of" (opposite of with ), attested from c. 1200. In use by late 14c. as a conjunction, short for without that .
