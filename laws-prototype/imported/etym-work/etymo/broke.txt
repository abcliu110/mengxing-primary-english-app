Origin and history of broke broke (adj.) from obsolete past participle of break (v.); extension to "insolvent" is first recorded 1716 ( broken in this sense is attested from 1590s). Old English cognate broc meant, in addition to "that which breaks," "affliction, misery." Phrase go for broke "risk all-or-nothing," is attested by 1943, American English, as the motto of the 442nd Infantry Regiment, composed of Japanese-Americans, in World War II. They call themselves the "Go for Broke" boys, having chosen their motto from soldier slang born of the dice games which means "shoot the works." They boast that they have "a year and three minutes to live—a year of training and three minutes of action." [editorial in Dayton Journal-Herald, June 13, 1943]
