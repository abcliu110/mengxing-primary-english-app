Origin and history of voyage voyage (n.) c. 1300, viage , "a journey by land or sea," a pilgrimage, embassy, military expedition, also figuratively, of a spiritual journey; from Old French voiage "travel, journey, movement, course, errand, mission, crusade" (12c., Modern French voyage ), from Late Latin viaticum "a journey" (source also of Spanish viaje , Italian viaggio ), in classical Latin "provisions for a journey," noun use of neuter of viaticus "of or for a journey," from via "road, journey, travel" (see via ). Later (post-Middle English) especially or exclusively "a passage by sea or water from point to point." Figuratively, of human life, etc., late 14c. also from c. 1300 voyage (v.) late 15c., viagen , "take a journey; carry out an enterprise;" from Old French voyager , from voiage (see voyage (n.)). By c. 1600 especially "take a journey over water." Related: Voyaged ; voyaging . also from late 15c.
