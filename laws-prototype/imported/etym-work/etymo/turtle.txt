Origin and history of turtle turtle (n.1) "tortoise," by mid-17c. (also tortel ), originally "marine tortoise," from a modification of French tortue , tortre (13c.) "turtle, tortoise" (often grouped with the diabolical beasts), a word of unknown origin. The classical Latin word was testudo , from testa "shell." The English word is said to be a sailors' accommodation of the French one, influenced by "a whimsical association" [Century Dictionary] with the similar-sounding turtle (n.2), the dove. Also compare tortoise . It later was extended to land tortoises; sea-turtle is attested from 1610s. Turtle-soup is attested by 1763. To turn turtle "turn over, capsize" is by 1818; it also was a method of capturing tortoises. The tortoise was not native to England in historical times, and the Latin word often is glossed in Middle English by snail , in Old English by shield , also bordðeaca "shield-covering," gehused snægl "housed snail." also from mid-17c. turtle (n.2) "turtle-dove," bird native to the British Isles and other parts of Europe, Middle English turtel , from Old English turtle , dissimilation of Latin turtur "turtle-dove," imitative of the bird's cooing. Old English and Middle English also had the word as turtur . Graceful, harmonious and affectionate to its mate, hence a term of endearment in Middle English and a symbol of conjugal affection (Jonson's "happy state of turtle-billing lovers," etc.). Turtle-dove (also turtledove ) is attested from c. 1300 ( turtuls douues , plural) and was applied to similar birds elsewhere, in U.S. to the common Carolina dove, also known as mourning-dove . turtles and duues habbet sorinesse for song. [c. 1200, Trinity Homilies] turtle (v.) by 1660s (implied in turtling ), "pursue and capture turtles; hunt turtle eggs," from turtle (n.1). The meaning "act like a turtle" in any sense is by 1914, often on the notion of retreat into impenetrable defense. Related: Turtler (1690s). The sense of "behave like a turtle-dove" (from turtle (n.2)), attested by 1701, is rare. also from 1660s
