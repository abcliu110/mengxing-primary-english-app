Origin and history of list list (n.1) "catalogue consisting of names in a row or series," c. 1600, from Middle English liste "border, edging, stripe" (late 13c.), from Old French liste "border, band, row, group," also "strip of paper," or from Old Italian lista "border, strip of paper, list," both from Germanic sources (compare Old High German lista "strip, border, list," Old Norse lista "border, selvage," Old English liste "border of cloth, fringe"), from Proto-Germanic *liston , from PIE *leizd- "border, band." The original Middle English sense is now obsolete. The sense of "enumeration" is from strips of paper used as a sort of catalogue. The native Old English word lingered as list in a few specialized senses. List price is from 1871. also from c. 1600 list (v.1) "to tilt, lean, incline to one side," especially of a ship, 1880, earlier spelled lust (1620s), a word of unknown origin. Perhaps it is an unexplained spelling variant of Middle English lysten "to please, desire, wish, like" (see list (v.4)) with a sense development from the notion of "leaning" toward what one desires (compare incline (v.)). Related: Listed ; listing . also from 1880 list (v.2) also lyst , "hear, harken," now poetic or obsolete, Middle English listen , lesten , lusten , earlier hlesten from Old English hlystan , "listen, listen to, hear, hearken," from hlyst "sense of hearing," from Proto-Germanic *hlustjanan (source also of Old Norse hlusta ), from PIE root *kleu- "to hear." Related: Listed ; listing . This variant, with "noun-formative -t-" [Century Dictionary], existed alongside Old English hlysnan "listen, listen with astonishment." Middle English Compendium reports it "rather generally replaced by listenen after 1400" (see listen (v.)). Perhaps this was in reaction to confusion in identical but unrelated list (v.4) "to wish, desire" (from Old English lystan ), which is related to lust (v.), and in Middle English often was written lest- or lust- . [Piers Plowman] list (v.3) "put down in a list or catalogue; to make a list of," 1610s, from list (n.1). The meaning "place real estate on the market" is from 1904. The meaning "put an edge around" (c. 1300, now probably obsolete) is from Old French lister or else from list (n.2). Related: Listed ; listing . also from 1610s list (v.4) "to wish, desire, like, choose; be pleasing to;" archaic, Middle English lusten , listen "to please, desire," from Old English lystan "to please, cause pleasure or desire, provoke longing," from Proto-Germanic *lustjan , from *lustuz- , from PIE root *las- "to be eager, wanton, or unruly" (see lust (n.)). Compare list (v.2). Related: Listed ; listing . Germanic cognates include Old Saxon lustian , Dutch lusten "to like, fancy," Old High German lusten , German lüsten , Old Norse lysta "desire, wish, have a fancy." also from mid-12c. list (n.2) "a narrow strip," Old English liste "border, hem, edge, strip," from Proto-Germanic *liston (source also of Old High German lista "strip, border, list," Old Norse lista "border, selvage," German leiste ), from PIE *leizd- "border, band" (see list (n.1)). The Germanic root also is the source of French liste , Italian lista . The word has had many technical senses in English, including "lobe of an ear" and "a stripe of color." This also is the list in archaic lists "place of combat" (late 14c.), from an earlier sense "boundary;" the fighting ground being originally at the boundary of fields. list (n.3) "a lean (of a ship) to one side," 1834, from earlier lust , from the verb (see list (v.1)). also from 1834 list (n.4) c. 1200, "pleasure, enjoyment;" mid-13c., "desire, wish, will, choice," from list (v.4).
