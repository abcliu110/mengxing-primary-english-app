Origin and history of themselves themselves (pron.) emphatic pronoun, plural of himself , herself , itself ; mid-15c. in northern dialect, standard from 1540s; an alteration of Middle English tham-self, theim-self , emphatic plural pronoun, also reciprocal pronoun (14c.); see them + self . The s elf , originally an inflected adjective, came to be treated as a noun with a meaning "person" and pluralized. It displaced Old English heom selfum (dative). Themself returned late 20c. as some writers took to replacing himself with gender-neutral everyone , anyone , etc. also from mid-15c.
