Origin and history of photograph photograph (n.) "a picture obtained by any process of photography," 1839, coined by English polymath and photography pioneer Sir John Herschel (son of the astronomer) from photo- "light" + -graph "something written." It won out over other suggestions, such as photogene and heliograph . Photogram (1859), based on telegram , did not catch on. Neo-Anglo-Saxonists prefer sunprint ; and sun-picture (1846) was an early Englishing of the word. The verb is first found (along with photography and photographic ) in a paper Herschel read before the Royal Society on March 14, 1839. Related: Photographed ; photographing . also from 1839
