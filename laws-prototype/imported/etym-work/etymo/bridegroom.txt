Origin and history of bridegroom bridegroom (n.) "man newly married or about to be," Old English brydguma "suitor," from bryd "bride" (see bride ) + guma "man," from Proto-Germanic *gumon- (source also of Old Norse gumi , Old High German gomo ), literally "earthling, earthly being," as opposed to the gods, from suffixed form of PIE root *dhghem- "earth." The ending was altered 16c. by folk etymology after groom (n.) "groom, boy, lad" (q.v.). A common Germanic compound (compare Old Saxon brudigumo , Old Norse bruðgumi , Old High German brutigomo , German Bräutigam ), except in Gothic, which used bruþsfaþs , literally "bride's lord."
