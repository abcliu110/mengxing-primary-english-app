although (conj.) early 14c., althagh , "admitting that, in spite of the fact that," a contraction of all though , preserving the once-common emphatic use of all . " All though was originally more emphatic than though , but by 1400 it was practically only a variant of it, and all having thus lost its independent force, the phrase was written as one word" [OED]. The choice between although and though often is determined by metrics.
