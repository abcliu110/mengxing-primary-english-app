Origin and history of ninth ninth (adj., n.) "next in order or rank after the eighth; being one of nine equal parts into which a whole is regarded as divided;" c. 1300, nynþe , an alteration or replacement (by influence of nine ) of nigonðe , from Old English nigoða , nigende , for which compare seventh . Also see -th (1). As a noun, "ordinal numeral corresponding to nine," late 13c. As the name of a musical interval, 1590s. Related: Ninthly . also from c. 1300
