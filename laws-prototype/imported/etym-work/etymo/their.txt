Origin and history of their their (pron.) plural possessive pronoun, "of, belonging to, or pertaining to them," c. 1200, from Old Norse þierra "of them," genitive of plural personal and demonstrative pronoun þeir "they" (see they ). It replaced Old English hiera . As an adjective from late 14c. Its use with singular objects, historically scorned by grammarians, is attested from c. 1300, and OED (1989) quotes this in Fielding, Goldsmith, Sydney Smith, and Thackeray. Theirs (c. 1300) is a double possessive. Alternative form theirn (1836) is attested in Midlands and southern dialect in U.K. and the Ozarks region of the U.S. There is a Middle English theiren from early 15c. also from c. 1200
