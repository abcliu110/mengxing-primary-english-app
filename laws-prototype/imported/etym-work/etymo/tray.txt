Origin and history of tray tray (n.) Middle English trei, "shallow, open vessel or rimmed, flat board," for household use, carrying food, dishes, etc.; from Old English treg , trig "flat wooden board with a low rim," from Proto-Germanic *traujam (OED 1989 compares Old Swedish tro , a corn measure), from PIE *drou- , variant of root *deru- "be firm, solid, steadfast," with specialized senses "wood, tree" and derivatives referring to objects made of wood. The original sense of the Germanic word might have been "wooden vessel." Also perhaps a unit of measure in Middle English equal to 16 corn bushels. "Formerly more widely applied to shallow open vessels generally" (OED); later applied to flat rimmed boards for other than household purposes.
