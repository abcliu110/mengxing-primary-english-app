unkind (adj.) Middle English unkind , from Old English uncynde "unnatural, not in accord with the regular course of nature" (a sense now obsolete); see un- (1) "not" + kind (adj.). The meaning "lacking in kindness, not sympathetic" is recorded from mid-14c. In Middle English also used of unseasonable weather, unproductive seed, exhausted soil, chills, fevers, incest, and foreigners.
